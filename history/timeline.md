# Coach company timeline (1941–2002)

## Lead

This timeline tracks Coach from its 1941 founding as a six-person Manhattan
leather workshop through its 2001 Signature-C launch — the vintage era this
archive covers. Events are drawn from trade-press and reference sources;
each is tagged with a confidence level, and unresolved questions are
preserved in the disputes and open-pulls sections at the bottom.

## Era summary

**Pre-Cashin (1941–1961).** Coach began in 1941 as **Gail Leather
Products** (not, despite a persistent community misnomer, "Manhattan
Leather Bags"), a small Manhattan workshop of six leatherworkers producing
men's wallets and billfolds [fundinguniverse-coach][wwd-coach-early-years].
Miles and Lillian Cahn, future owners, **joined the company in 1946 as
employees, not founders** — the company predates them by five years
[fundinguniverse-coach]. Through the 1950s the firm operated as a contract
shop; around 1960 a "Coach"-branded line of cowhide purses was introduced,
predating the Cahns' purchase [fundinguniverse-coach]. In 1961 the Cahns
bought the business outright, beginning the family-ownership chapter.

**Cashin (1962–1974).** At Lillian Cahn's suggestion, the new owners hired
Bonnie Cashin in 1962 to launch a women's handbag line; her first
"Cashin Carry" collection shipped that fall [wwd-coach-early-years]
[wikipedia-cashin]. Cashin was a contracted designer — never a Coach
employee — and was internally referred to as "the Bonnie Cashin account"
[wwd-coach-early-years]. Over a twelve-year run she introduced the brass
side pockets, the kissing-lock coin purse, brighter colors, the brass
turnlock (1964 — see timeline), and
the Cashin Carry tote silhouette that defined Coach's early visual
language [wwd-coach-early-years][vintagecoachie].

**Classic NYC (1975–1994).** The post-Cashin period built Coach into a
national specialty brand. Lew Frankfort joined in 1979
[encyclopedia-com-coach-inc]; the Cahns sold to **Sara Lee Corporation in
1985** for a reported ~$30M, with Frankfort named president that same year
[fundinguniverse-coach][encyclopedia-com-coach-inc]. Through the late
1980s Coach expanded into department-store boutiques, opened a Madison
Avenue flagship (1987), launched in England and Japan (1988), and reached
$100M in sales by 1989 [fundinguniverse-coach]. The early 1990s brought
the Sheridan, Camden, and Manhattan collections and a two-level New York
flagship (1994) [fundinguniverse-coach].

**Transition (1995–2002).** Frankfort became **Chairman and CEO in 1995**
(promoted from president — a promotion some sources conflate as "since
1985," but the more detailed corporate histories distinguish the two
dates) [fundinguniverse-coach][encyclopedia-com-coach-inc]. Reed Krakoff
arrived as creative director c. 1996 [wikipedia-cashin][vintagecoachie];
licensing deals with Movado, Motorola, and Baker Furniture followed
[fundinguniverse-coach]. Coach went public in October 2000 at $16/share
under ticker COH, raised $118M, and was fully spun out from Sara Lee in
April 2001 [fundinguniverse-coach]. The 2001 Signature collection (the
double-C logo) marked the visual pivot away from solid-leather identity
toward logo monogramming — the cutoff event for this archive's vintage
scope [fundinguniverse-coach][wwd-coach-early-years].

## Timeline

### 1941 — Coach founded as Gail Leather Products
Family-run Manhattan loft workshop with six leatherworkers, producing
hand-made small leather goods (men's wallets and billfolds). The original
1941 founders are **not the Cahns** and have not been identified in
free secondary sources.
- confidence: confirmed
- sources: [fundinguniverse-coach][encyclopedia-com-coach-leatherware]
  [wwd-coach-early-years][vintagecoachie]

### 1946 — Miles and Lillian Cahn join Gail Leather Products
The Cahns, knowledgeable about the leather business, joined the contractor
as employees — fifteen years before they would buy the company. This is
the most-corrected fact in vintage-Coach folklore.
- confidence: confirmed
- sources: [fundinguniverse-coach][wwd-coach-early-years]

### 1950 — Miles Cahn begins managing factory operations
- confidence: likely
- sources: [fundinguniverse-coach]

### c. 1960 — "Coach" brand of cowhide purses introduced
Per FundingUniverse, the Coach-branded line of cowhide handbags was
introduced as a signature product around 1960 — predating both the Cahn
purchase and the Cashin engagement. Coach-branded handbags existed before
Bonnie Cashin's 1962 hire.
- confidence: likely
- sources: [fundinguniverse-coach]

### 1961 — Miles and Lillian Cahn purchase Gail Leather Products
A leveraged buyout from the original owners; marks the transition to
family ownership.
- confidence: confirmed
- sources: [fundinguniverse-coach][encyclopedia-com-coach-leatherware]
  [wwd-coach-early-years]

### 1962 — Bonnie Cashin hired as designer
Hired at Lillian Cahn's suggestion to start a women's handbag line. Cashin
was a contracted designer, never a Gail Leather or Coach employee;
internally referred to as "the Bonnie Cashin account." First "Cashin
Carry" collection shipped Fall 1962.
- confidence: confirmed
- sources: [wwd-coach-early-years][wikipedia-cashin][vintagecoachie]

### 1962–1974 — Bonnie Cashin tenure
Twelve-year design partnership. Cashin introduced side pockets, the
kissing-lock coin purse, brighter colors, and the "Cashin Carry" tote
silhouette in Fall 1962. The brass turnlock came two years later (see
1964 entry).
- confidence: confirmed
- sources: [wwd-coach-early-years][wikipedia-cashin][vintagecoachie]

### Pre-1969 — Classic Shoulder Bag (9170): "the original Coach bag"
Per Spring 1989 catalog primary copy, the **Classic Shoulder Bag
(Style 9170)** is described as **"the original Coach bag"** — Coach's
own brand-published claim: "After more than two decades, women still
regard this bag — the original Coach bag — as an essential part of
their Coach collection." 1989 minus "more than two decades" = pre-1969.
The 9170 was already in continuous production by 1969, placing its
introduction within or shortly after the early Cashin years. Cashin
attribution is `tentative` until UCLA Cashin papers can confirm
authorship. The 9170 is one of the longest-running Coach silhouettes
in the archive — 25+ year production run as of Spring 1989.
- confidence: confirmed (per primary catalog), with Cashin
  designer attribution `tentative`
- sources: [coach-catalogs-1960-1989]

### 1964 — Brass turnlock introduced
Cashin introduced the brass turnlock/toggle fastening in 1964, two years
after her 1962 hire. The fastener was used **non-exclusively** across her
collections for multiple manufacturers — Coach, Philip Sills, Meyers,
Crescendoe-Superb, HBA, and D. Klein — though Coach is most associated
with it because of longevity and brand visibility, not exclusivity. Early
Cashin Carry totes (Fall 1962, Spring/Fall 1963) predate the turnlock and
used different closures.
- confidence: likely
- sources: [vintagefashionguild-cashin]
- notes: New source added to registry under H stream. Primary anchor
  (USPTO design patent or 1964 press piece) still to be pulled.

### Fall 1981 — "Small family owned company since 1941" voice locked-in
Per Fall 1981 catalog primary copy: "Coach Bags are made by a small,
privately held company that has been making leather bags, belts and
billfolds since 1941. It is a family-owned business deeply committed
to the excellence of its products and the quality of its service."
This canonical Coach copy appears verbatim across Spring/Summer 1982,
Fall 1982, Spring 1984, Fall-Winter 1984, and is preserved through
the Sara Lee acquisition (Spring 1985 + Winter 1985 retain identical
phrasing). The voice was therefore **established as canonical Coach
copy at least 4 years before Sara Lee** and was **inherited, not
authored,** by the post-acquisition team. Adjacent Cahn-era
brand-voice anchors first documented in 1981 Fall: "naked leather"
patina copy and the "almost fifteen years ago" model-introduction
anchor (placing core Coach silhouettes at 1966-1967, the Cashin
period). "Glove Tanned Cowhide" naming first occurs Fall 1982.
- confidence: confirmed
- sources: [coach-catalogs-1960-1989]

### Fall 1981 — 40-year anniversary acknowledged subtly
1981 = 40 years from 1941. The Fall 1981 catalog never says "40th
Anniversary" on its cover; the only anniversary anchor is a
factory-page line: "The Coach Factory was first organized **nearly
forty years ago**, starting out as a small leathergoods workshop
employing only six people." Spring/Summer 1982 updates this to
"**over forty years ago**." There is no commemorative issue, no
anniversary edition, no anniversary badge — the milestone is
treated with the same understatement that defines the brand voice.
- confidence: confirmed
- sources: [coach-catalogs-1960-1989]

### Fall 1981 — Factory: 125 staff, 2 floors of 516 W 34th St NYC
Per Fall 1981 catalog, the Coach factory employs **125 people** on
**2 floors** of the West 34th Street NYC building. By Spring 1982
the workforce has grown to **150 people on 3 floors** —
real-time-documented factory growth across two seasons.
- confidence: confirmed
- sources: [coach-catalogs-1960-1989]

### 1981-1982 — Mail-order only; no retail stores, no toll-free line
Coach in 1981-1982 is **mail-order-only via 516 West 34th Street,
New York City 10001** [coach-catalogs-1960-1989]. There is **no
800 number, no list of Coach-owned stores, no Madison Avenue
flagship, no Paris store** — the entire retail/toll-free
infrastructure documented in the 1985 catalogs was built **between
Fall 1982 and Winter 1985**. The Spring 1984 catalog already shows
the six-store network (Paris, NYC Madison, DC, Seattle, SF, Boston)
fully established but still uses the regular NYC phone (212)
594-3914 — no toll-free until Spring 1985 (800-262-2422). The
six-boutique footprint that Sara Lee inherited at the July 1985
acquisition was therefore stable for at least 16 months prior.
- confidence: confirmed
- sources: [coach-catalogs-1960-1989]

### 1981-1984 — No sub-collection branding
1981-1984 catalogs have **no Lightweights, no Spectator, no
Continental, no Madison, no Sheridan**. Every bag is "just a Coach
bag." The catalog architecture is uniformly Bags → Briefcases /
Portfolios → Belts → Wallets / Bag Fittings. The collection-
diversification era of 1989+ is a completely different
organizational model. The first sub-collection branding is the
Lightweight Collection's Spring 1989 establishment (per M2),
followed by Spectator + Continental Series in Fall 1989.
- confidence: confirmed
- sources: [coach-catalogs-1960-1989]

### Spring 1984 — "It's Not A Coach® Bag Without The Coach® Tag"
Anti-counterfeiting brand-protection messaging is primary-confirmed
in the **Spring 1984** catalog: "It's Not A Coach® Bag Without The
Coach® Tag." This pushes the documented start of Coach's
counterfeit-awareness messaging back from earlier framings as a
1985+ Sara-Lee-era development. The Cahns were already running
anti-counterfeit copy at least 16 months before the acquisition.
- confidence: confirmed
- sources: [coach-catalogs-1960-1989]

### Spring 1984 — Classic Shoulder Bag (9170): "the first bag we ever made"
Spring 1984 catalog primary copy explicitly identifies the Classic
Shoulder Bag (Style 9170) as **"the first bag we ever made"** —
the bag that established Coach's unlined-outside-seam construction
signature. This corroborates the Spring 1989 framing as "the
original Coach bag" (per M2). The 9170 carries a brand-published
foundational-silhouette claim across multiple catalog years
1984-1989.
- confidence: confirmed
- sources: [coach-catalogs-1960-1989]

### 1984 — School Bag (9695) name in use by Fall-Winter 1984
**Earlier archive framing** (per M1) placed the "Handle Shoulder
Bag" → "School Bag" rename in **Winter 1985** under Sara Lee
ownership. **N3 finding overrides this**: the Fall-Winter 1984
catalog already labels Style 9695 as **"School Bag"** at $144. The
Spring 1984 catalog does not show 9695 (Handle Shoulder Bag is
absent in spring catalog visible scan). The rename therefore
happened **between Spring 1984 and Fall-Winter 1984** — entirely
within Cahn-era ownership, not a Sara-Lee-era action.
- confidence: confirmed
- sources: [coach-catalogs-1960-1989]

### 1984→1985 palette transition — Sage retired, Bone introduced
- **Fall-Winter 1984 palette** (per N3): Burgundy, Saddle, Red, Mocha,
  Tabac, British Tan, Navy, Putty, Black, **Sage** (10 colors,
  including Sage, no Bone)
- **Spring 1985 palette** (per M1): same 10 minus Sage, plus **Bone**
- The Sage→Bone palette swap is therefore a **late-Cahn-era /
  pre-Sara-Lee** change (M1 had earlier framed it as a 1985
  transition with broader window).
- The 10-color palette had been stable for ~3 years prior: 1981
  Fall, 1982 S/S, 1982 Fall all show identical palettes (Saddle,
  Navy, Mocha, Red, British Tan, Putty, Sage, Burgundy, Black,
  Tabac).
- confidence: confirmed
- sources: [coach-catalogs-1960-1989]

### July 1985 — Cahns sell Coach to Sara Lee Corporation
Specific to month per primary 10-K. Reported sale price ~$30 million. Sara
Lee acquired the factory, six boutiques, and the Madison Avenue flagship.
The Cahns reportedly sold to focus on Coach Farm goat dairy in Gallatinville
NY (started 1983).
- confidence: confirmed
- sources: [coach-10k-fiscal-2003][fundinguniverse-coach]
  [encyclopedia-com-coach-leatherware][wwd-coach-early-years]

### Late 1985 — Sara Lee transition: brand-voice preserved, prices up ~10%
Three primary 1985 catalogs (Spring, Summer, Winter) capture the
transition months. **Brand voice is preserved verbatim** in Winter 1985
— months after Sara Lee acquired Coach: "We are a small family owned
company that has been making leather bags, belts and billfolds since
1941" appears unchanged. The "no unnecessary ornamentation, no gadgets,
no gimmicks" / "baseball glove leather" / "brass through and through"
framing is preserved across the transition. **However**: ~10%
across-the-board price increase Spring→Winter 1985, 10+ new SKUs added
Winter 1985, toll-free number changed (262-2422 → 544-3004), Table of
Contents added to catalog format, and the **Handle Shoulder Bag was
renamed "School Bag"** (style 9695 unchanged). Six retail locations
documented in 1985 catalogs: Paris (Rue Jacob — international), NYC
(754 Madison Ave), Washington DC, Seattle, San Francisco, Boston.
**Coach Water Buffalo Hide** documented as **vegetable-tanned in
India** — distinct from glove-tanned cowhide. **12 stitches per inch**
construction primary-confirmed; outside-seam construction with
continuous leather binding documented as the Coach portfolio signature
(predates the 1991 "hand-folded seam binding" language by 6 years).
A genuine **Fall 1985** catalog (which would have introduced the price
increases first) appears to be missing from the corpus —
`1985-fall.pdf` is content-identical to `1985-summer.pdf`.
- confidence: confirmed
- sources: [coach-catalogs-1960-1989]

### July 1985 — Lew Frankfort named President of Coach
Frankfort took the president role at Coach under Sara Lee ownership in
the same month Sara Lee acquired the company. He held this position
through **September 1991** before moving to Sara Lee corporate roles
(see September 1991 entry below). He returned as Chairman/CEO in
November 1995. Per primary S-1, his Coach Inc. CEO/Chairman role dates
from June 1, 2000 (incorporation date of the public-company parent).
- confidence: confirmed
- sources: [coach-s1-2000][coach-10k-fiscal-2003][fundinguniverse-coach]
  [encyclopedia-com-coach-inc]

### Early 1986 — Coach expands in department stores
New boutiques in Macy's and Bamberger's; mall stores multi-state.
- confidence: likely
- sources: [fundinguniverse-coach]

### Spring 1986 — Madison Satchel (9725) launches at $104
Per Spring 1986 catalog primary: "Additions to Our Collection"
explicitly flags the **Madison Satchel (Style 9725)** as **NEW** at
$104 [coach-catalogs-1960-1989]. Earlier archive framing of Madison
Satchel as a 1991+ Madison-Collection-line model is wrong — the
Madison Satchel **as a Classic-line silhouette** debuted Spring
1986, ~5 years before the Madison Collection sub-line launch.
The 9725 number persists into Spring 1989 catalogs (per M2) at
$140 — Madison Satchel was a steady Classics line model from 1986
through 1989+. The "Madison" name was therefore used for two
distinct things: (1) the Madison Satchel single model from 1986;
(2) the Madison Collection sub-line from ~1991.
- confidence: confirmed
- sources: [coach-catalogs-1960-1989]

### Spring 1986 — palette: Saddle retired, Grey added
Single-season palette swap between Winter 1985 and Spring 1986:
**Saddle retired, Grey added** [coach-catalogs-1960-1989]. The
10-color baseline (locked since Fall 1981 per N4) had its first
post-acquisition change. Cumulative palette transitions across the
Sara-Lee-onboarding window:
- Fall-Winter 1984 → Spring 1985: Sage retired, Bone added
- Winter 1985 → Spring 1986: Saddle retired, Grey added
- 1986: Mocha persists, **Mahogany still absent**
- Mahogany debut: 1987 or 1988 (window narrowed; not in 1986)
- confidence: confirmed
- sources: [coach-catalogs-1960-1989]

### Summer 1986 — "45 years" framing first used
The "since 1941" / "forty years" looseness used 1981-Spring 1986
shifts to **"45 years"** in the **Summer 1986** catalog
[coach-catalogs-1960-1989]. By Winter 1986 this becomes a full
**45th Anniversary moment** with a Lew Frankfort signed letter
(see Winter 1986 entry below). The 45-year mark is treated more
loudly than the silent 40-year moment of 1981 — first explicit
anniversary marketing in Coach catalogs.
- confidence: confirmed
- sources: [coach-catalogs-1960-1989]

### Summer 1986 — "Five Production Stages" framework introduced
Coach catalog brand voice expands with a new **"Five Production
Stages"** framework — a brand-narrative architecture for explaining
craftsmanship [coach-catalogs-1960-1989]. First codified Summer
1986; persists in subsequent catalogs.
- confidence: confirmed
- sources: [coach-catalogs-1960-1989]

### Fall 1986 — "Coach Stores" first named in distribution copy
Fall 1986 catalog distribution copy first uses the term **"Coach
Stores"** as a named retail-channel reference
[coach-catalogs-1960-1989]. Predates the broader 1988+ retail
expansion. The 1981-1985 catalogs had listed individual store
addresses; 1986 introduces the **named retail-channel terminology**
that becomes standard in subsequent years. **The six-store retail
list itself was dropped from catalogs in 1986** — 1985 was the
last year with the enumerated address list. The "Coach Stores"
naming substitutes a category for the explicit list.
- confidence: confirmed
- sources: [coach-catalogs-1960-1989]

### Winter 1986 — 45th Anniversary letter, Frankfort signs as President
The Winter 1986 catalog opens with a signed letter from **Lew
Frankfort, President** [coach-catalogs-1960-1989] — **the first
president signature in any Coach catalog in the archive**. Miles
Cahn never signed a Coach catalog letter under his ownership; the
1981-1985 brand voice was uniformly unsigned/corporate. Frankfort's
signature establishes a personal-leadership voice in Coach catalog
copy under Sara Lee ownership.

Critical primary-source data points from the letter:
- **"Six skilled leather workers"** — first primary anchor for the
  1941 founding team size
- **"Over 250 of us"** — first primary-source Coach 1986 headcount;
  anchored against the 125 (Fall 1981) and 150 (Spring 1982) data
  points from N1, this confirms ~67% workforce growth across 1982-1986
- **"True American Classics"** — capitalized branding elevation
- **No mention of Sara Lee** — Coach Leatherware Company preserved
  as standalone identity 18 months post-acquisition. Sara Lee
  remains invisible in catalog brand voice through 1986.
- **"1941" + "45 years"** definitively retires the earlier "since
  1941" / "forty years" looseness — the canonical anniversary
  arithmetic is now anchored.
- confidence: confirmed
- sources: [coach-catalogs-1960-1989]

### Winter 1986 — leather gloves debut; first non-USA Coach products
Coach introduces **leather gloves** in Winter 1986 [coach-catalogs-
1960-1989]. **These are the first Coach products manufactured
outside the USA** — produced in **Hungary** using **European
lambskin**. This precedes the 1994 Costa Rica handbag offshoring
documented elsewhere in the archive by 8 years. The Winter 1986
catalog (page 39) carries a Hungarian gloves narrative explaining
the production. Important era-of-offshoring caveat: a "Coach made
in Hungary" stamp is era-correct for gloves (and possibly other
non-handbag accessories) from Winter 1986 onward, even though
handbags continued to be USA-made through 1994.
- confidence: confirmed
- sources: [coach-catalogs-1960-1989]

### Winter 1986 — stitch count revised: 12 → 14 SPI
The Winter 1986 catalog primary-revises the documented stitch count
from **12 stitches per inch** (1985 catalogs per M1) to **14
stitches per inch** [coach-catalogs-1960-1989]. This is a
brand-published spec change — either an actual production change or
a marketing copy revision. Either way, authentication SPI ranges
should account for the 1985→1986 boundary: pre-Winter-1986 examples
should show ~12 SPI; post-Winter-1986 examples should show ~14 SPI.
- confidence: confirmed
- sources: [coach-catalogs-1960-1989]

### Early Spring 1987 — toll-free number changed (800) 544-3004 → (800) 223-8647
A yellow-card insert in the Early Spring 1987 catalog documents the
toll-free transition. The (800) 544-3004 number used in late 1985
and 1986 is replaced by **(800) 223-8647**
[coach-catalogs-1960-1989]. Per O2 deep-read.
- confidence: confirmed
- sources: [coach-catalogs-1960-1989]

**Coach Lightweights Collection launches in the Early Summer 1987

| 4015 | Drawstring Sac Medium | $124 |

| 4025 | Shoulder Pouch | $124 |

| 4055 | Soft Satchel | $140 |

The Lightweights Collection ships with a **dedicated 11-color

### 1987 — Catalog cadence increases to 5 issues/year
1981-1984 catalogs ran 2-3 issues/year (Spring/Fall ± Summer or
Winter). By 1987, Coach is publishing **5 catalogs per year**:
Early Spring, Spring, Early Summer, Early Fall, Holiday. The
"Early X" naming convention is new in 1987 (the 1981-1984 corpus
used Spring / Summer / Fall / Winter only, and 1985 introduced
"Spring Preview"). The cadence increase reflects the post-Sara-Lee
operational rhythm.
- confidence: confirmed
- sources: [coach-catalogs-1960-1989]

### 1987 — Retail expands to 7 stores; DC dropped
Holiday 1987 catalog back cover lists **7 retail Coach Stores**:
NYC×2, Dallas, Seattle, San Francisco×2, Costa Mesa
[coach-catalogs-1960-1989]. **DC has dropped** from the 1983 store
list. Net change vs 1985: -1 (DC) +2 (NYC×2 vs ×1) +1 (Dallas) +1
(SF×2 vs ×1) +1 (Costa Mesa) -1 (Boston) -1 (Paris). Several store
movements between 1985 and 1987.
- confidence: confirmed
- sources: [coach-catalogs-1960-1989]

### Early Spring → Spring 1987 — City Bag SKU swap
Coach catalog SKU-renumbering documented within 1987: **City Bag
debuts as Style 9785 in Early Spring 1987** (NEW), then
**renumbered to 9790 in Spring 1987**. The abandoned 9785 SKU is
**reassigned to the new "Zipper Tote"** by Early Summer 1987
[coach-catalogs-1960-1989]. Important authentication note: a 1987
"City Bag" with the 9785 number is a brief-window correct-spec
example, not a reproduction or fake. By Spring 1989 (per M2) the
City Bag is firmly Style 9790. Similar same-year naming flux: SKU
**9800** = "Musette Bag Large" in Early Fall 1987 → renamed to
"Carrier Bag" by Holiday 1987.
- confidence: confirmed
- sources: [coach-catalogs-1960-1989]

### 1986 — biggest single-year line expansion (~30 new SKUs)
1986 is the largest single-year line expansion in the deep-read
corpus to date. ~30 new SKUs across the four 1986 catalogs include:
Madison Satchel (9725), Saddlery Bag, Companion Bag (additional
variants), Mini Pouch, Shoulder Sac, Rambler Bag, Camera Bag,
Pocket Purse, Riding Bag, Embassy Briefcase, Diplomat Briefcase,
Metropolitan Brief Bag, Envelope Portfolio, Madison Satchel Large,
Large Tote, four glove styles, Lefax Organizer, plus multiple new
wallets/SLG. **A Twill collection establishes 8000-series numbering
for non-leather variants** — the first proto-sub-collection
naming in the catalog. ~10% second-wave Sara Lee price increase
across Spring → Fall 1986.
- confidence: confirmed
- sources: [coach-catalogs-1960-1989]

### Fall 1989 — Spectator Collection + Continental Series launches
Two named sub-collections debut in Fall 1989, primary-confirmed via
Coach catalog. **Spectator Collection** launches with eight styles
(6375, 6750, 6765, 6790, 6820, 6825, 6850, 6855) — all with **Tabac
trim** originally (transitioned to British Tan trim by 1991). The
6XXX style-number convention is a Fall 1989 innovation, not 1991 as
earlier archive framing suggested. Brand voice: "Traditionally
American in appeal, the Spectator Collection possesses a sportive
spirit..." Simultaneously, the **Continental Series** debuts with
Plaza Bag (9860), Plaza Bag Large (9865), **Court Bag (9870)**, and
Regency Brief (5255) — meaning the Court Bag's Holiday 1991 catalog
appearance was **not** its debut. **Forest Green** debuts as a Coach
color the same Fall 1989 catalog (resolving long-running "Hunter
Green" community misnomer in collector references). **Sandstone**
debuted Summer 1989. **Lightweight Collection** was already fully
established by Spring 1989 with **14+ styles** and hidden magnetic
snap closure already in use. The **Soho Bag (4082)** is **absent
from all five 1989 catalogs** — narrowing its launch window to
between Fall 1989 and Holiday 1991.
- confidence: confirmed
- sources: [coach-catalogs-1960-1989]

### 1988-1990 — Lightweight Collection growth: 12 → 19 styles
The Lightweight Collection (launched Early Summer 1987 with 10
styles per O2) grows steadily: **1988 Holiday roster of 12 styles**

## Disputes

### Frankfort role title timing — 1985 vs 1995
Some sources merge "president 1985" and "CEO 1995" into a single "since
1985" claim. The detailed corporate histories distinguish them: Frankfort
became **president in 1985** under Sara Lee ownership and was promoted to
**Chairman and CEO in 1995**. The archive treats this distinction as
canonical until contradicted by primary documents (e.g., Sara Lee SEC
filings or the Coach S-1 prospectus).
- confidence: disputed (in source wording); resolved: 1985 president,
  1995 chairman/CEO
- sources: [fundinguniverse-coach][encyclopedia-com-coach-inc]

### "Manhattan Leather Bags" — community misnomer
Several blog and community sources call the original 1941 company
"Manhattan Leather Bags." This name appears in no Tier 1 or Tier 2 source.
The authoritative trade press and reference sources name the original
entity **Gail Leather Products**. Treat "Manhattan Leather Bags" as a
community misnomer; cite Gail Leather Products as canonical.
- confidence: disputed; resolved in favor of Gail Leather Products
- sources: [fundinguniverse-coach][encyclopedia-com-coach-leatherware]
  [wwd-coach-early-years]

### Sheridan launch — 1992 vs 1993
FundingUniverse places the Sheridan introduction in early 1992; the Coach
brand product page for the Vintage Sheridan Stewart bag dates the
silhouette to 1993. The discrepancy may reflect collection vs. specific
silhouette: Sheridan as a *collection* may have launched in 1992 with the
specific Sheridan Stewart bag added in 1993. Per-model entries should
record this distinction.
- confidence: disputed
- sources: [fundinguniverse-coach][coach-vintage-sheridan-stewart]
