# Navy

## Lead

Navy is a dark blue Coach color used selectively in the NYC era
[yourgreatfinds-collectors-guide, vintagecoachie]. It is less common
than Black, Burgundy, or British Tan and does not appear as a
default offering on most NYC silhouettes in the working research
record.

## Description

Navy reads as a deep, saturated dark blue on glove-tanned cowhide
— dark enough to be mistaken for Black under poor lighting, but
distinguishable in daylight by its blue undertone. Because Coach's
NYC-era leather was drum-dyed through (the cut edge shows the same
color as the surface — see
[authentication/leather.md](../authentication/leather.md)), a Navy
bag's edges, scuffs, and worn corners stay in the same dark-blue
family rather than fading toward gray or revealing a contrasting
under-color.

## Models documented in Navy

Navy is **not specifically documented** against named models in
research/D-models.md. The color name surfaces in the dealer-compiled
NYC-era palette references but per-model attestation is open and
needs catalog confirmation [yourgreatfinds-collectors-guide,
vintagecoachie].

This gap is consistent with Navy's status as a less-common offering
in the NYC palette; a future pass through dated catalog scans should
populate per-model entries.

## Authentication

- Navy is a Coach color name distinct from Black — under daylight,
  the blue undertone should be visible. A bag listed as "navy" with
  no visible blue cast may actually be Black, faded, or refinished
- A Navy bag should still pass the standard drum-dyed-through
  edge-color check (see authentication/leather.md): the cut edge
  reads dark blue, not gray or brown
- As with all NYC-era colors, painted glossy edges on a bag claiming
  pre-1995 manufacture is a strong fake or reproduction tell,
  independent of the body color (see research/E-authentication.md §7)

## Era anchoring

Navy is currently anchored to the NYC era in the working research,
but its appearance window is not yet pinned to specific catalog
years. It is not useful as an era anchor on its own; combine with
creed wording, serial format, hardware, and country-of-origin (see
[authentication/](../authentication/)) for era placement.
