# Camel

## Lead

Camel is a Coach color name for a light tan / warm-beige neutral
leather, sitting lighter than Saddle and warmer than Bone or Putty
[yourgreatfinds-collectors-guide]. It is documented as part of the
NYC-era neutral palette, with at least one model (Court) explicitly
anchored to a "camel and bone" colorway pairing in research notes.

## Description

Camel reads as a light tan with a warm yellow-brown undertone — not
gray, not pink, not the cooler Bone neutral. On Coach's glove-tanned
cowhide the color is drum-dyed through (the cut edge shows the same
color as the surface — see
[authentication/leather.md](../authentication/leather.md)), so a
Camel bag's edges and worn corners stay the same warm-beige rather
than revealing a contrasting under-color. With handling Camel
darkens slightly toward a richer tan; it does not fade to white.

Because Camel sits in the neutral palette alongside Bone, Putty,
and Khaki, listing-description "beige" or "tan" is not enough to
identify a bag as Camel — match against a catalog color name or a
known dated example.

## Models documented in Camel

The following are documented in Camel (or paired-with-Camel
colorways) in research/D-models.md

- **Court** (style 9870) — research/D-models.md notes "camel

- **Willis** (style 9927) — Camel appears in Willis color

This list is the subset surfaced in research/D-models.md. The full
catalog of Camel offerings across NYC-era models is open and should
be expanded as catalog scans are added.

## Authentication

- Camel should pass the standard drum-dyed-through edge-color
  check: the cut edge reads the same warm beige as the surface
  (see [authentication/leather.md](../authentication/leather.md))
- Camel and Bone differ in warmth, not just brightness — Camel
  has a yellow-brown undertone, Bone is cooler and closer to
  off-white. Side-by-side comparison is the most reliable way to
  distinguish them; lone-bag photos can mislead
- Light neutrals show soiling and edge wear more than dark
  colors; uneven darkening on a Camel bag is wear, not redye

## Era anchoring

Camel appears in NYC-era catalog references and likely persists
into the transition era. It is not useful as an era anchor: the
neutral palette spans decades. Combine with creed wording, serial
format, hardware, and country-of-origin (see
[authentication/](../authentication/)) for era placement.
