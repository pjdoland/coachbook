# Hunter Green

## Resolution: "Hunter Green" is a community misnomer — Coach calls it Forest Green

**Update 2026-05-02 (research stream M2).** Primary 1989 catalog
evidence resolves the long-running color-name question. Coach has
used **"Forest Green"** as its canonical green color name since
**Fall 1989** [coach-catalogs-1960-1989]. "Hunter Green" appears in
collector references and resale listings but does not match Coach
catalog usage. Treat **Forest Green** as the canonical Coach color
name; "Hunter Green" is a community misnomer that propagated through
Tier 3 sources.

This entry is preserved for the community-facing search term but
points at Forest Green as the correct primary attribution.

## Lead

Hunter Green is a distinctive Coach NYC-era color: a deep forest-green
that contrasts strongly with Coach's standard brown and black palette
and appears to have been offered selectively rather than as a default
colorway [yourgreatfinds-collectors-guide]. Documented examples are
uncommon enough that Hunter Green is not a baseline color in the same
sense as British Tan or Black.

(Per primary catalog evidence above, "Hunter Green" appears to be a
community label for what Coach catalogs name **Forest Green**.)

## Description

Hunter Green reads as a deep forest green on glove-tanned cowhide. In
low light or under indoor incandescent light it can read almost black,
which sometimes leads to misidentification in poorly lit listing
photos. Daylight or strong direct light is required to confirm the
color. As with other Coach NYC-era leathers, the dye is drum-dyed
through, so the cut edge of the leather should show the same deep
green as the surface — see
[authentication/leather.md](../authentication/leather.md).

## Models documented in Hunter Green

- **Sutton (style 9814)** — documented in a Hunter Green body with
  British Tan trim per [research/D-models.md](../research/D-models.md)
  [yourgreatfinds-collectors-guide]. `tentative` — single Tier 3
  source.

The full set of NYC-era models offered in Hunter Green is open and
should be expanded as catalog scans are matched.

## Two-tone variants

Hunter Green frequently appears paired with British Tan trim in
Coach's NYC-era color program. The Sutton (9814) variant above is the
documented worked example: green body, British Tan piping/trim
[yourgreatfinds-collectors-guide]. This pairing is a deliberate Coach
combination, not a fake, an aftermarket modification, or a Frankenbag.
A two-tone Hunter Green / British Tan bag should not be flagged as
suspect on the basis of the color combination alone; authenticate via
creed, serial, hardware, and stitching as usual.

## Authentication

- Hunter Green should pass the standard drum-dyed-through edge-color
  check (cut edge = surface color). A bag whose cut edge shows a pale
  or contrasting under-color is not Coach NYC-era leather regardless
  of surface color.
- Confirm color in daylight before describing a bag as Hunter Green —
  indoor light flattens it toward black.
- On two-tone Hunter Green / British Tan bags, both leathers should
  pass the edge-color check independently.

## Era anchoring

Hunter Green appears more commonly on NYC-era (E3–E4) bags than on
later Coach output. It **may** be useful as a soft era hint when
combined with other diagnostics (creed wording, serial format,
hardware, country of origin), but Hunter Green alone does not date a
bag. Coach has reused color names and color formulations across eras,
and reproductions / re-releases can use similar greens. Treat Hunter
Green as a weak signal, not an anchor. `tentative`.
