# Saddle

## Lead

Saddle is a Coach color name for a medium tan-brown leather that sits
between British Tan and Mahogany on the warm-brown axis
[yourgreatfinds-collectors-guide]. It was a common stock color across
the NYC era, though the exact catalog introduction date is not
primary-sourced.

## Description

Saddle reads as a medium tan brown — warmer and lighter than
Mahogany, slightly less reddish than British Tan, and noticeably
darker than Camel. On Coach's glove-tanned cowhide the color is
drum-dyed through (the cut edge shows the same color as the
surface — see [authentication/leather.md](../authentication/leather.md)),
so a Saddle bag's worn corners and edges stay tan-brown rather than
revealing a contrasting under-color. With handling Saddle deepens
toward a richer brown rather than fading.

Because Saddle, British Tan, and Mahogany overlap on the
warm-brown axis, listing-description "tan" is not enough to
identify a bag as Saddle — match against a catalog color name or
a known dated example before treating any tan-brown bag as Saddle.

## Naming caution: "Saddle Bag" is a silhouette, not a color

The "Saddle Bag" model name (e.g., Crescent Saddle Bag, Saddle
Pouch NYC, the early Saddlebag silhouette listed in
research/D-models.md) refers to the bag's **shape** — a
flap-over crescent or curved-front silhouette derived from
equestrian saddlebags — not to the leather color. A Saddle Bag in
Black is a Black bag in the Saddle silhouette; a British Tan
Saddle Bag is a Saddle-silhouette bag in British Tan. Do not
collapse the model name into the color name.

## Models documented in Saddle

The research/D-models.md compilation does not call out specific
models in the **color** Saddle. The color is referenced
generically as a common NYC-era offering in dealer guides
[yourgreatfinds-collectors-guide], but no per-model Saddle
colorway is anchored to a primary or catalog source in current
working files. Treat all Saddle attributions as `tentative` until
a catalog page or dated example is matched.

## Authentication

- Saddle should pass the standard drum-dyed-through edge-color
  check: the cut edge reads the same medium tan-brown as the
  surface, with no generic-tan or natural under-color visible
  (see [authentication/leather.md](../authentication/leather.md))
- Surface color alone does not distinguish Saddle from British
  Tan or aged Mahogany on a worn bag; the catalog color name
  takes precedence over visual matching
- Reproductions in "saddle tan" generic leather should not be
  treated as Coach Saddle without a creed and serial that match
  the era

## Era anchoring

Saddle appears across the NYC era and likely persists into the
transition era. It is not useful as an era anchor: the warm-brown
palette spans decades. Combine with creed wording, serial format,
hardware, and country-of-origin (see
[authentication/](../authentication/)) for era placement.

## Open verification items

1. **Catalog-anchored introduction date** — when did "Saddle"
   first appear as a Coach color name? An NYC-era catalog page
   would settle whether Saddle is a distinct catalog color or a
   community label retrofitted to British Tan / Mahogany
   variants. Currently `tentative`.
2. **Distinction from British Tan** — community references
   sometimes use "Saddle" and "British Tan" interchangeably. A
   dated catalog color card would settle whether these are two
   distinct Coach colors or one color with two names. Flagged
   in [colors/index.md](index.md) "Open verification items."
3. **Per-model Saddle colorways** — no model in
   research/D-models.md is anchored to Saddle as a documented
   colorway. Open: match a dated example or catalog page to a
   specific model offered in Saddle.
