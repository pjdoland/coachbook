# Bone

## Lead

Bone is a Coach color name for an off-white / cream neutral leather,
sitting in the light end of the NYC-era neutral palette alongside
Putty, Khaki, and Camel [yourgreatfinds-collectors-guide]. It is one
of the better-documented light neutrals in working research, with
multiple models anchored to Bone colorways.

## Description

Bone reads as a cool off-white or pale cream — lighter and cooler
than Camel, less green/olive than Khaki, and slightly brighter than
Putty. On Coach's glove-tanned cowhide the color is drum-dyed
through (the cut edge shows the same color as the surface — see
[authentication/leather.md](../authentication/leather.md)), so a
Bone bag's edges, scuffs, and worn corners stay the same cream
neutral rather than revealing a contrasting under-color.

Light neutrals like Bone show soiling, edge darkening, and
hand-oil patina more visibly than dark colors. A clean Bone bag
from the NYC era is uncommon; uniform light cream across body and
edges, with no surface yellowing, is the original presentation.

## Models documented in Bone

The following are documented in Bone in research/D-models.md
[yourgreatfinds-collectors-guide]:

- **Court** (style 9870) — research/D-models.md notes "camel and
  bone documented" for Court colorways
- **Pocket Purse** — research/D-models.md notes "black, bone,
  khaki documented"
- **Wristlet Clutch** — research/D-models.md notes 1970s NYC
  examples in "bone and putty"
- **Scooter** — referenced in dealer compilations as available
  in Bone, though no specific catalog page is matched yet

This list is the subset surfaced in research/D-models.md. Bone
appears to have been a regular small-leather-goods and
neutral-palette offering across the NYC era; the full per-model
catalog is open.

## Authentication

- Bone should pass the standard drum-dyed-through edge-color
  check: the cut edge reads the same cream off-white as the
  surface (see
  [authentication/leather.md](../authentication/leather.md))
- Bone and Putty differ subtly — Bone is closer to true off-white,
  Putty has a slightly grayer/warmer tone. Side-by-side
  comparison is the most reliable way to distinguish them
- Yellowing or uneven dye on a Bone bag may be age-related
  oxidation rather than original finish; redye attempts on light
  neutrals tend to leave streaking visible at edge transitions
- Replaced creed patches on Bone bags are detectable: the
  surrounding leather darkens with handling while a replaced
  patch reads cleaner cream than the body

## Era anchoring

Bone appears across the NYC era from at least the 1970s
(Wristlet Clutch reference) through the early 1990s. It is not
useful as a precise era anchor on its own, but its presence on a
bag with NYC-era construction is consistent. Combine with creed
wording, serial format, hardware, and country-of-origin (see
[authentication/](../authentication/)) for era placement.

## Open verification items

1. **Catalog-anchored introduction date** — when did "Bone"
   first appear as a Coach color name? An NYC-era catalog page
   would settle this. Currently `tentative`.
2. **Bone vs Putty distinction** — research/D-models.md pairs
   them ("bone and putty" on the Wristlet Clutch) which implies
   they are distinct Coach colors, not synonyms. A dated color
   card would confirm.
3. **Discontinuation of Bone** — light neutrals tend to drop in
   and out of seasonal palettes. Whether Bone persisted as a
   stock color into the transition era is open.
