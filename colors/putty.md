# Putty

## Lead

Putty is a light beige Coach color in the NYC-era neutral palette,
sitting alongside Bone and Khaki [yourgreatfinds-collectors-guide,
vintagecoachie]. It is one of the lighter-end neutrals in Coach's
classic NYC range and is less commonly surfaced in dealer-compiled
records than Bone or British Tan.

## Description

Putty reads as a light, slightly grayed beige on glove-tanned cowhide
— softer than Khaki and warmer than a true gray. Because Coach's
NYC-era leather was drum-dyed through (the cut edge shows the same
color as the surface — see
[authentication/leather.md](../authentication/leather.md)), a Putty
bag's edges and worn corners stay in the same beige family rather
than revealing a contrasting under-color. Light-color Coach leathers
are most prone to surface soiling with wear, so survival examples
in clean condition are correspondingly less common than darker
neutrals.

## Models documented in Putty

The following model is documented in Putty in the working research
files [yourgreatfinds-collectors-guide]:

- 1970s Wristlet Clutch — documented in Bone and Putty colorways
  per the dealer-compiled record (research/D-models.md, D1)

This list is the subset surfaced in research/D-models.md; the full
catalog of Putty offerings across Coach's NYC era is open and should
be expanded as catalog scans are added.

## Authentication

- Putty is a Coach color name, not a generic descriptor — a bag
  whose listing description says "beige" or "off-white" should be
  matched to a specific Coach color (Bone, Khaki, Putty, or Natural)
  before being treated as Putty
- A Putty bag should still pass the standard drum-dyed-through
  edge-color check (see authentication/leather.md): the cut edge
  reads in the same beige family as the surface, not white or gray

## Era anchoring

Putty surfaces in the NYC-era neutral range and is not currently
documented as a stock color outside that window. Its presence alone
is not sufficient to date a bag; combine with creed wording, serial
format, hardware, and country-of-origin (see
[authentication/](../authentication/)) for era placement.
