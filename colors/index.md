# Colors

## Lead

This index lists the canonical Coach colors documented across the
archive. Coach uses its own color names, and matching a bag to "British
Tan" or "Mahogany" requires the brand-specific reference rather than a
generic descriptor. Each color has its own page with model coverage and
authentication notes; this page is the navigable summary.

## Palette overview

The most-cited Coach colors fall into four loose groups:

**Browns and tans** — the dominant Coach palette across all eras:
- [British Tan](british-tan.md) — warm reddish medium brown, the
  brand-default color across multiple decades
- [Mahogany](mahogany.md) — deep red-brown; distinguish from British
  Tan-aged-to-mahogany via cut-edge color
- (Saddle, Camel, Tabac — also documented in research; per-page
  entries forthcoming)

**Darks** — common across all eras:
- [Black](black.md) — most common Coach color; available all eras
- [Burgundy](burgundy.md) — deep wine red; sometimes catalog-listed
  as Bordeaux
- [Forest Green](forest-green.md) — Coach's canonical green color
  name, primary-confirmed Fall 1989 debut; **canonical** ("Hunter
  Green" is the community misnomer)
- [Hunter Green](hunter-green.md) — community misnomer for Forest
  Green; entry retained for searchability
- [Navy](navy.md) — selective NYC-era dark blue

**Lights and neutrals** — NYC-era and onward:
- [Putty](putty.md) — light beige in the neutral palette alongside
  Bone and Khaki
- [Sandstone](sandstone.md) — warm sand neutral; primary-confirmed
  Summer 1989 debut
- (Bone, Khaki, Camel, Cream — per-page entries forthcoming)

**Accent and rare** (all `tentative` pending catalog corroboration):
- Persimmon orange (Cashin-era Cashin Carry variant per research/D)
- Watermelon (Kisslock Watermelon Tote per research/D)
- Aqua (rare Drawstring Backpack 9994 per research/D)
- Red (rare Willis variant per research/D)

## Drum-dyed-through diagnostic

Across all colors, the strongest authentication signal is that
Coach's NYC-era leather is **drum-dyed through** — the cut edge of
the leather shows the same color as the surface. A bag whose cut
edges show a different color (typically a generic tan or natural)
under the surface dye is suspect. This is documented in
[authentication/leather.md](../authentication/leather.md).

## Color is not an era anchor

With limited exceptions (Hunter Green leans NYC-era, Aqua appears
on specific 1990s models), Coach color names persist across
multiple decades. **Color alone never dates a bag.** Combine with
creed wording, serial format, hardware, and country-of-origin (see
[authentication/](../authentication/)) for era placement.

## Free swatch service 1981-1982 → paid Color Palette card 1985

Coach offered a **free leather-swatch service** in 1981 Fall, 1982
S/S, and 1982 Fall catalogs: "an actual set of leather swatches can
be had for the asking" [coach-catalogs-1960-1989]. By 1985 (per M1)
this had been **commercialized as the paid Coach Color Palette
card** sold through the catalog. Earlier framing of the 1985 Color
Palette card as a new offering is wrong — it was a commercialization
of an already-existing free Cahn-era service.

## Mocha → Mahogany transition

The **10-color palette stable for 3.5+ years** (Fall 1981 →
Fall-Winter 1984) included **Mocha** as a core color. By Spring
1989 (per M2) Mocha is no longer documented as a Classics palette
color, but **Mahogany** is. The Mocha → Mahogany transition year
is open; specific year between 1985 and 1989 not yet pinned (1986,
1987, 1988 catalogs needed). The 10-color Cahn-era palette and the
late-NYC palette are documented; the bridge year is the open item.

## Sage retired between Fall-Winter 1984 and Spring 1985

The Sage→Bone palette swap is the first documented palette change
in the deep-read corpus. The 10-color palette had been stable since
Fall 1981 (Saddle, Navy, Mocha, Red, British Tan, Putty, Sage,
Burgundy, Black, Tabac). Spring 1985 retires Sage and adds Bone —
a **late-Cahn / pre-Sara-Lee** palette change.

## Open verification items

1. **Catalog-anchored introduction dates** for each color name. Many
   colors are in production for decades; the first catalog
   appearance establishes the lower bound but not the upper. None
   of the per-color introduction dates are currently
   primary-sourced.
2. **Color formula consistency over time** — community sources
   occasionally claim that British Tan, Mahogany, or Black look
   slightly different across decades. No primary source confirms
   any formula change. Inspection of dated examples would settle
   this; flag as `tentative`.
3. **Two-tone variants** — Hunter Green with British Tan trim
   (Sutton 9814) is documented; other two-tone Coach combinations
   (e.g., Cream with Black trim, Saddle with Mahogany trim) are
   referenced anecdotally and need a catalog or dated bag for
   confirmation.
4. **Color names that may be redundant** — Burgundy and Bordeaux
   may refer to the same color or distinct ones; British Tan and
   Saddle may overlap. Each pair needs a dated catalog page or
   color-card scan to settle.
