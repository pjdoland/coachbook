# Mahogany

## Lead

Mahogany is a deep red-brown Coach color name documented across multiple
NYC and transition-era models [yourgreatfinds-collectors-guide]. It is
frequently confused visually with British Tan that has aged into a
mahogany-looking patina; the two are distinct, and disambiguating
original Mahogany dye from British Tan patina is the central
authentication question for this color (see
[british-tan.md](british-tan.md)).

## Description

Mahogany reads as a deep red-brown on glove-tanned cowhide — darker and
more red-shifted than British Tan at any patina stage. As with other
NYC-era Coach colors, Mahogany was drum-dyed through, so the cut edge
shows the same color as the surface (see
[authentication/leather.md](../authentication/leather.md)).

## Models documented in Mahogany

- **Patricia's Legacy Crossbody** (style 9951) — Legacy series
  [yourgreatfinds-collectors-guide]. Tentative; single Tier 3 source.

The full catalog of Mahogany offerings is open and should be expanded
as catalog scans are added.

## Mahogany vs aged British Tan

This is the key authentication caveat for the color. British Tan
develops a darker, more red-brown patina over years of use that
approaches the Mahogany hue [vintagecoachie, coachbagrehab-dating]. A
worn British Tan bag can therefore *look* like Mahogany on the surface
without ever having been dyed Mahogany.

Distinguish:

- **Original Mahogany dye** — the cut edge of the leather (turn-under,
  strap edge, pocket edge) shows Mahogany at any age, because Coach's
  NYC-era leather was drum-dyed through.
- **British Tan aged into a Mahogany-looking surface** — the cut edge
  still shows the original British Tan, because the surface darkening
  is patina, not dye.

Tentative: this disambiguation rule is consistent with the
drum-dyed-through behavior described in the leather sources but has
not yet been corroborated against a primary catalog or Coach material
spec.

## Authentication

The drum-dyed-through cut edge is the disambiguator between original
Mahogany and aged British Tan. Standard Coach color authentication
otherwise applies:

- Mahogany is a Coach color name, not a generic descriptor — match a
  listing's "mahogany" or "burgundy-brown" wording to a specific Coach
  color before treating it as Mahogany.
- Reproductions and counterfeits sometimes match the surface color
  approximately while missing the warm red undertone or the
  drum-dyed-through cut edge.

## Era anchoring

Mahogany appears across NYC-era and transition-era Coach models. Like
British Tan, it persists across multiple decades and is not useful as
an era anchor on its own. Combine with creed wording, serial format,
hardware, and country-of-origin (see
[authentication/](../authentication/)) for era placement.
