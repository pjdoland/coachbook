# Black

## Lead

Black is the most common Coach color across all eras, available from
the Cashin era forward and continuing through every subsequent
production era [yourgreatfinds-collectors-guide, vintagecoachie]. It
sits alongside British Tan, Burgundy, and Mahogany as one of the
core, decade-spanning Coach color names.

## Description

Black on Coach's glove-tanned cowhide reads as a true, saturated
black. Because the NYC-era leather was drum-dyed through (the cut
edge shows the same color as the surface — see
[authentication/leather.md](../authentication/leather.md)), a Black
bag's edges, scuffs, and worn corners stay black rather than
revealing a brown or gray under-color. With handling the surface
develops a slight sheen rather than fading.

## Models documented in Black

The following models are documented in Black in the working research
files [yourgreatfinds-collectors-guide]:

- Court (style 9870)
- Willis (style 9927)
- Sutton (style 9814)
- Daypack (style 9791)
- Crescent Saddle Bag
- Pocket Purse (black, bone, khaki documented)
- Ergo Hobo (style 9033, 1998)
- Many other NYC-era and transition-era models offered Black as a
  stock colorway

This list is the subset surfaced in research/D-models.md; Black was a
default offering on most Coach silhouettes and the full per-model
catalog is open.

## Authentication

- Black should pass the standard drum-dyed-through edge-color check:
  the cut edge reads true black, not gray or brown (see
  authentication/leather.md). A bag whose edge color contrasts with
  the surface is a fake or reproduction signal
- Black absorbs dye fully on glove-tanned cowhide; uneven or
  splotchy surface color on a "vintage" Black bag is a signal of
  redye or refinishing rather than original Coach finishing
- Painted glossy edges on a Black bag claiming pre-1995 manufacture
  is a strong fake or reproduction tell, independent of color (see
  research/E-authentication.md §7)

## Era anchoring

Black persists across every Coach era from Cashin forward through
modern production. It is not useful as an era anchor on its own.
Combine with creed wording, serial format, hardware, lining, and
country-of-origin (see [authentication/](../authentication/)) for
era placement.

## Open verification items

1. **Color formula consistency over decades** — community sources
   occasionally suggest Black's depth or undertone shifted across
   eras (NYC vs Costa Rica production, pre- and post-1985 Sara
   Lee). No primary source confirms a formula change; flag for
   inspection of dated examples.
2. **Earliest catalog appearance of "Black" as a Coach color name**
   — Cashin-era catalogs would settle whether Black was a named
   stock color from 1962 forward or appeared later. Currently
   `tentative`.
