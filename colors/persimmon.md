# Persimmon

## Lead

Persimmon is a Cashin-era orange Coach color documented on the
inaugural Cashin Carry tote [yourgreatfinds-collectors-guide]. The
research record specifically flags a "Persimmon orange variant
documented with narrow kiss lock and double straps"
(research/D-models.md). Because the Cashin Carry was Bonnie Cashin's
earliest Coach work — Fall 1962 collection — Persimmon is one of the
few Coach colors with a plausible early era anchor (E2 / Cashin
era).

## Description

Persimmon reads as a warm, slightly red-leaning orange — named for
the persimmon fruit. On Coach's drum-dyed-through Cashin-era leather
(see [authentication/leather.md](../authentication/leather.md)),
Persimmon's cut edge should match the surface color. Cashin's color
choices for Coach were unusual within the leather-goods market of
the early 1960s, when bright saturated colors on glove-tanned
cowhide were uncommon; Persimmon belongs to that distinguishing
palette.

## Models documented in Persimmon

The following models are documented in Persimmon in the working
research files [yourgreatfinds-collectors-guide]:

- Cashin Carry tote — Persimmon orange variant documented with
  narrow kiss-lock and double straps (research/D-models.md). Note
  that the turnlock closure was introduced in 1964 per
  research/H/vintagefashionguild-cashin; 1962–63 Cashin Carry
  examples used kiss-lock or other closures, consistent with the
  Persimmon variant's narrow kiss-lock

The per-model catalog of Persimmon offerings beyond Cashin Carry is
open.

## Authentication

- Persimmon should pass the standard drum-dyed-through edge-color
  check: the cut edge reads the same warm orange as the surface
  (see authentication/leather.md). A contrasting under-color is a
  fake or reproduction signal
- A Cashin Carry claiming Persimmon should be cross-checked against
  the closure type: kiss-lock and double straps are documented for
  the Persimmon variant; a turnlock Persimmon Cashin Carry would be
  1964 or later (still within Cashin's tenure but post the earliest
  Cashin Carry production)
- Cashin attribution is a high-folklore zone (see STYLE.md §4).
  Cashin's contract ended in 1974; bags introduced after 1974 are
  not Cashin designs regardless of color or silhouette. A Persimmon
  Coach bag dated post-1974 is not Cashin-era

## Era anchoring

Persimmon is anchored to the Cashin era (E2, 1962–1974) via the
Cashin Carry tote attribution. This is one of the few Coach colors
with a meaningful era anchor — most Coach color names persist
across decades. Persimmon Coach bags from later decades have not
been documented in the working research; flag any claimed Persimmon
bag from the Classic NYC era forward as needing extra verification.

## Open verification items

1. **UCLA Cashin papers** — `ucla-cashin-papers` would corroborate
   Persimmon as a Cashin-named or Cashin-selected Coach color. The
   Cashin papers at UCLA Library Special Collections (NOT LACMA)
   are the primary archive. Currently `tentative`.
2. **Catalog-anchored Persimmon appearances** — a 1962–1974 Coach
   catalog page listing Persimmon as a stock color would settle
   the era anchor as `confirmed`.
3. **Per-model Persimmon catalog** — beyond the Cashin Carry, which
   Cashin-era silhouettes were offered in Persimmon?
