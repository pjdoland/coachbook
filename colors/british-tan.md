# British Tan

## Lead

British Tan is one of Coach's most enduring color names — a warm
medium-brown leather color that became a default Coach offering across
multiple decades and silhouettes
[yourgreatfinds-collectors-guide, vintagecoachie]. Among collectors it
is one of the most-recognized Coach colors, alongside Black, Burgundy,
and Mahogany.

## Description

British Tan reads as a saturated, slightly reddish medium brown on
glove-tanned cowhide. Because Coach's NYC-era leather was drum-dyed
through (the cut edge shows the same color as the surface — see
[authentication/leather.md](../authentication/leather.md)), British Tan
develops a darker, richer patina with use rather than rubbing off or
showing a contrasting under-color. Worn examples typically deepen toward
mahogany over years of handling.

The color is consistent enough across eras that two British Tan bags
from different decades will read as the same color name even when their
patinas differ. The naming is Coach's own — non-Coach "tan" leathers
should not be assumed to match.

## Models documented in British Tan

The following models are documented in British Tan in the working
research files [yourgreatfinds-collectors-guide]:

- Court (style 9870)
- Willis (style 9927)
- Embassy Tote (1970s NYC) — large briefcase form, full-grain in
  British Tan per the dealer-compiled record
- Sutton (style 9814) — appears as the British Tan **trim** on a
  Hunter green primary body in one documented variant
- Many other NYC-era models offered British Tan as a stock colorway

This list is the subset surfaced in research/D-models.md; the full
catalog of British Tan offerings across Coach's NYC and transition eras
is open and should be expanded as catalog scans are added.

## Authentication

- British Tan is a Coach color name, not a generic descriptor — a bag
  whose listing description says "tan" should be matched to a specific
  Coach color before being treated as British Tan
- Drum-dyed-through is a leather authenticity signal, not a color
  signal: a bag in British Tan should still pass the standard
  edge-color check (see authentication/leather.md)
- Reproductions and counterfeits sometimes match the surface color
  approximately while missing the warm undertone or the patina
  development; British Tan should darken with handling, not fade

## Era anchoring

British Tan appears in Coach catalogs across the NYC era through the
transition era and into modern Coach Originals re-releases. It is not
useful as an era anchor: the same color name persists for decades, so
British Tan alone does not date a bag. Combine with creed wording,
serial format, hardware, and country-of-origin (see
[authentication/](../authentication/)) for era placement.

## Open verification items

1. **Catalog-anchored color introduction date** — when did "British
   Tan" first appear as a Coach color name? An early NYC-era catalog
   page would settle this. Currently `tentative`.
2. **Color formula changes over time** — community sources occasionally
   note that British Tan looks slightly different across decades (e.g.,
   pre-1985 Sara Lee era vs post-1985). No primary source confirms a
   formula change; flag for inspection of dated examples.
3. **British Tan trim vs body** — Sutton's British Tan trim on Hunter
   green is distinct from British Tan as a body color. Per-model color
   maps should distinguish trim use from body use.
