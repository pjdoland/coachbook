Vintage Coach in glove-tanned cowhide rewards basic care: leather
conditioning, brass polishing, and minor lining repair will keep most
NYC-era bags in usable condition. Coach's historic factory restoration
program — based in Jacksonville, Florida since July 1999
[coach-10k-fiscal-2003] — handled major work and replacement-part
installation that exceeded what an owner could reasonably do at home.
This page is a tentative starter; most leather-care guidance below is
general convention, not Coach-specific.

## Coach Repair / Factory Restoration program

- **July 1999** — Coach consolidated worldwide warehousing,
  distribution, and **repair** functions into a 560,000 sq ft facility
  in Jacksonville, Florida [coach-10k-fiscal-2003]. This is the primary
  anchor for the program's location after July 1999.
- Pre-1999 repair locations are not in the 2003 10-K. Open research
  item.
- **Replacement-component caveat**: Coach Repair installed replacement
  turnlocks, dogleash clips, beanbag hangtags, and creed patches during
  legitimate restoration. A bag with a "wrong era" component may be
  authentic with a Coach Repair replacement, not a fake or a
  Frankenbag. See `authentication/` for the era-keyed matrix; treat
  single-component anomalies as a prompt for further checks, not as a
  verdict.
- **Program end date** — not in the research files; needs research.

## Leather conditioning

Glove-tanned cowhide is full-grain, drum-dyed-through leather that
develops patina rather than cracking. Brand-neutral leather conditioner
(neatsfoot-based products are the conventional choice for bridle-style
leathers) is generally compatible. Avoid silicone-based dressings,
which can build up on the surface and interfere with later
conditioning.

This is general leather-care convention, not a Coach-published
recommendation; flagged `tentative` until a Coach care card or
press source is located.

## Brass polishing

Solid brass hardware (turnlocks, dogleash clips, D-rings, feet) can be
polished with conventional brass polish to restore a bright finish.
Whether to keep an aged honey-to-amber patina or polish to bright is a
personal-preference choice and does not affect authenticity. A magnet
that sticks to "brass" hardware is a red flag that the part is plated
steel — see `authentication/hardware.md`.

## Lining repair

- **Unlined bags** (correct for many E3–E4 NYC-era models such as
  Court, Willis, and similar): no lining to repair.
- **Lined bags**: a deteriorated lining can be replaced by a competent
  cobbler or leather-repair shop. Replacement linings affect resale
  value (collectors often prefer original linings even when worn) but
  do not affect function.

## Edge re-burnishing

Burnished leather edges wear with use and can be re-burnished. **Do
not paint edges.** Glossy painted edges on a bag claiming to be
NYC-era are a strong reproduction or fake signal — authentic vintage
edges are raw or burnished, not painted. See
`authentication/leather.md`.

## Storage

Conventional leather-bag storage applies: a breathable cotton dust bag,
climate-controlled environment (avoid attics, basements, and cars),
away from direct sunlight to limit fading and drying. Stuff the bag
lightly to hold its shape. These are general conventions, not
Coach-specific instructions.
