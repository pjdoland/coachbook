# Research stream O1 — Deep readings of the 1986 catalogs

Compiled 2026-05-02. Companion to `research/M1-catalogs-1985.md` and
`research/N4-cross-cut-1981-1984.md`. Primary-source readings of all
four 1986 Coach catalogs — the **first FULL year under Sara Lee
ownership** (Sara Lee acquired Coach in July 1985).

Files read:

- `1986-spring.pdf` — 26 PDF pages; printed pages 1–48, sage/grey cover
  with blind-embossed Madison Satchel
- `1986-summer.pdf` — 21 PDF pages; printed pages 1–48, ivory/cream
  cover with blind-embossed Shoulder Sac
- `1986-fall.pdf` — 27 PDF pages; printed pages 1–48, kraft/tan cover
  with blind-embossed Madison Satchel (smaller proportions than spring)
- `1986-winter.pdf` — 33 PDF pages; printed pages 1–56, silver/grey
  cover with blind-embossed Saddlery Bag

All 1986 catalogs print "Copyright 1986 Coach Leatherware Company" on
page 1 (the **first time the corporate entity is named in copyright
form**; 1985 catalogs used only "Coach Leatherware"). Toll-free
**(800) 544-3004** is preserved from Winter 1985 across all four 1986
catalogs (no number changes within 1986).

## Headline findings

1. **45th Anniversary explicit only in Winter 1986.** The Winter 1986
   catalog opens with a signed letter from **Lew Frankfort, President**
   addressed "To Our Loyal Customers and New Friends on the Occasion
   of Our 45th Anniversary." This is the **first primary-source
   appearance of Lew Frankfort's name in a Coach catalog** in our
   archive. Spring 1986 used "over 40 years"; Summer through Winter
   1986 explicitly use "45 years." [coach-catalogs-1960-1989]

2. **Coach employee count documented at "over 250" in Winter 1986.**
   Frankfort's letter: "On behalf of all of us here at Coach (there
   are now over 250 of us), we extend our thanks and appreciation."
   This is the first primary-source headcount data point in the
   Sara Lee era. [coach-catalogs-1960-1989]

3. **"Six skilled leather workers" — founding team size confirmed.**
   Frankfort: "The Coach concept was born in 1941 in a small workshop
   in downtown New York City. All we had then was a short lease, six
   skilled leather workers and our concept." Matches the 1981 Fall
   line "starting out as a small leathergoods workshop employing only
   six people" — primary-confirms the **six-founder count** for the
   1941 launch. [coach-catalogs-1960-1989]

4. **Madison Satchel (9725) primary-confirmed as Spring 1986 debut at
   $104.** The Spring 1986 catalog flags Style 9725 with a "New" tag
   in its "Additions to Our Collection" feature on page 1, picturing
   the Madison Satchel alongside three other new styles. **This pins
   the Madison Satchel introduction to Spring 1986**, narrowing what
   was previously an open Fall 1989 / earlier window. The Madison
   Satchel Large (9765) follows in Fall 1986 at $148.
   [coach-catalogs-1960-1989]

5. **Willis (Style 9927) is ABSENT from all four 1986 catalogs.** No
   "Willis" by name and no Style 9927 anywhere in Spring, Summer,
   Fall, or Winter 1986 catalogs across all 56 printed pages of
   Winter and full bag/SLG/briefcase listings of the others. **This
   narrows the Willis introduction window to 1987–1988** (or possibly
   later) — Spring 1989 is the first archive catalog after this set
   that we have. [coach-catalogs-1960-1989]

6. **City Bag (9790) is ABSENT from all four 1986 catalogs.** No
   Style 9790, no "City Bag" name. Confirms City Bag debut is
   post-1986. [coach-catalogs-1960-1989]

7. **Saddle retired, Grey added.** The 1985 "ten Coach Colors" were
   Burgundy, **Saddle**, Red, Mocha, Tabac, Bone, British Tan, Navy,
   Putty, Black. The 1986 "ten Coach Colors" (page 2 in every
   season) are Burgundy, **Grey**, Red, Mocha, Tabac, Bone, British
   Tan, Navy, Putty, Black. **Saddle was retired between Winter 1985
   and Spring 1986; Grey replaced it as the tenth color**, holding
   the palette at exactly ten. [coach-catalogs-1960-1989]

8. **Mocha persists through entire 1986; Mahogany absent.** Mocha is
   in the page-2 ten-color chart in all four 1986 catalogs and
   appears in nearly every product's color list. **Mahogany is not
   present in the 1986 palette.** The Mocha-to-Mahogany transition
   is therefore post-1986 and confirmed before Spring 1989 (per M2)
   — narrows the transition to 1987–1988. [coach-catalogs-1960-1989]

9. **Twill collection introduced as a parallel SKU series.** Spring
   1986 page 2 introduces "Our Twill" — a Khaki cotton twill bag
   line trimmed in Tabac glove-tanned cowhide. Twill-specific style
   numbers all start with **8**: 8050 (Handle Portfolio in Twill),
   8170 (Classic Shoulder Bag in Twill), 8375 (Dinky Bag in Twill),
   8430 (Slim Satchel Large in Twill), 8455 (Basic Bag in Twill),
   8585 (Saddle Pouch Large in Twill), 8625 (Musette Bag in Twill),
   8635 (Convertible Clutch in Twill), 8030 (Large Wetpack in
   Twill). The 8000-series numbering convention for non-leather
   variants is established in 1986. [coach-catalogs-1960-1989]

10. **Lightweight Collection NOT YET present in 1986.** No
    "Lightweight Collection" branding, no sub-collection naming. The
    1986 catalog architecture is still **monolithic** (Bags →
    Accessories → Briefcases → Belts → Wallets), matching
    1981–1985. Lightweight Collection arrives between 1986 and
    Spring 1989. [coach-catalogs-1960-1989]

11. **Stitch count revised upward from 12 to 14 in Winter 1986.**
    Spring/Summer/Fall 1986 keep the 1985 framing: "twelve stitches
    to the inch through several thicknesses of leather." Winter
    1986 page 42 changes this to **"hand-turned and sewn with an
    average of 14 stitches to the inch"** — a primary-source
    construction-spec revision, possibly reflecting tighter
    quality-control standards as Coach scaled past 250 employees.
    [coach-catalogs-1960-1989]

12. **Coach Stores publicly named in distribution copy starting Fall
    1986.** Spring 1986 page 6: "you will find Coach products in
    only the very best Department and Specialty stores." Fall and
    Winter 1986 page 2: "you will find Coach products in **only
    Coach Stores and the very best Department and Specialty
    stores**." Coach's own retail store network is now publicly
    framed as a distribution channel — a notable shift that
    pre-dates the major Coach Store expansion of 1988–1990.
    [coach-catalogs-1960-1989]

13. **Leather gloves introduced as new product category in Winter
    1986.** Four glove styles (2010 Woman's Dress, 2020 Driving,
    2030 Man's Dress, 2040 Man's Two-in-One) launch, made from
    European lambskin in **Hungary** — "we located a workshop of
    expert glovemakers in Hungary dedicated to a 200-year old
    reputation for excellence." First non-USA-manufactured Coach
    products documented. [coach-catalogs-1960-1989]

14. **Lefax-system Organizer (4715) introduced in Winter 1986.**
    Coach partners with London-based Lefax (founded 1910) to
    introduce a leather-bound professional organizer at $140, with
    interchangeable refills. Documents Coach's entry into the
    high-end personal-organizer category in late 1986, predating
    the Filofax-mania that would peak 1987–1988.
    [coach-catalogs-1960-1989]

15. **Three new color types in Fall 1986 belt collection.** Burnished
    Cowhide Belts (5700/5800) introduce **Brown** as a distinct
    belt color from Mocha. Calfskin Belts (5500/5600) introduce
    **Cordovan** and **Redwood** as belt-only colors not in the
    main bag palette. **Stirrup Belts (3100/3200/3300)** continue
    in Dark Brown / Tan from 1985. The "ten Coach Colors" applies
    to bag leathers; belt leathers diversify in Fall 1986.
    [coach-catalogs-1960-1989]

16. **Six "New" 1986 bag-line silhouettes pin previously open
    debuts**: Madison Satchel (9725, Spring), Saddlery Bag (9535,
    Spring), Companion Bag (9300, Spring), Mini Pouch (9740,
    Spring), Shoulder Sac (9730, Spring), Rambler Bag (9735,
    Spring). Three more arrive in Fall: Camera Bag (9760, Fall),
    Pocket Purse (9755, Fall), Madison Satchel Large (9765, Fall),
    Riding Bag (9750) and Riding Bag Small (9745, Fall). Large Tote
    (9780) launches Fall, plus three new briefcases: Embassy
    Briefcase (5090, Spring), Diplomat Briefcase (5170, Spring),
    Metropolitan Brief Bag (5180, Fall), Envelope Portfolio (5190,
    Fall). **1986 is the largest single-year line expansion in our
    archive prior to 1989.** [coach-catalogs-1960-1989]

17. **Catalog page count grew from 48 (Spring/Summer/Fall) to 56
    pages (Winter)** — Winter 1986 adds Glove and Organizer
    sections. Catalog production continues the "expanded TOC"
    pattern that began in Winter 1985. [coach-catalogs-1960-1989]

18. **Across-the-year price drift.** Across the 1986 catalog cycle,
    most bag prices rose modestly (~$2–$10 per item) between Spring
    and Fall, then held for Winter — a **second wave of Sara Lee
    price increases** following the larger ~10% Spring→Winter 1985
    increase. Examples: Basic Bag $90→$94 (Spring→Fall), Madison
    Satchel $104→$114, Convertible Clutch $116→$120, Companion
    Bag $102→$110. [coach-catalogs-1960-1989]

19. **Retail store list disappears from the catalog in 1986.** None
    of the four 1986 catalogs prints the six-store retail address
    list that 1985 Spring/Summer/Winter included (Paris, NYC,
    Washington DC, Seattle, San Francisco, Boston). Catalogs now
    only print the Coach Leatherware Company HQ ("516 West 34th
    Street, New York, NY 10001") and the toll-free order line. The
    public-facing six-store list is **dropped from catalog
    distribution starting Spring 1986**. [coach-catalogs-1960-1989]

20. **"Counterfeit warning" copy is partially preserved but
    de-emphasized.** Spring 1986 keeps "It's Not A Coach® Bag
    Without The Coach® Tag" as a section header on page 2, but the
    extended anti-counterfeit narrative from 1985 ("recent
    proliferation of imitation Coach tags") is dropped. By Winter
    1986 the section title persists but the content is purely
    brand-positive ("the trademark of the genuine article"). The
    aggressive 1985 anti-counterfeit framing is **softened in 1986
    catalogs**. [coach-catalogs-1960-1989]

## Brand Voice 1986 — verbatim quotes

### Spring 1986 — "Additions to Our Collection" (page 1)

> "We are pleased to present some significant additions to our
> classic collection. They are all made with the durability and the
> traditional American styling you have come to expect from Coach.
> They are already being embraced as classics along with the rest of
> our collection."

This is **the first 'curated additions' framing in our archive** —
Coach explicitly highlights newly added models on the inside front
spread. Pre-1986 catalogs simply listed everything. The new framing
("already being embraced as classics") is a **softer, market-reading
voice** than the Cahn-era "no gimmicks, no gadgets" copy.

### Spring 1986 — "Our Concept" (page 2)

> "We have been making the finest leather products for men and women
> for over 40 years and are gratified that during this time they have
> become known as true American classics — clean, straightforward,
> functional designs that are very well made and never go out of
> style."

> "The Coach tag too, has acquired a reputation over the years — the
> trademark of the genuine article — the finest bags, belts,
> billfolds and portfolios."

NEW finding: The phrase **"true American classics"** that becomes a
1990s Coach signature is established by Spring 1986. Pre-1986
catalogs use "true American classics" once (1985), but Spring 1986
makes it the centerpiece of the brand-voice paragraph.

### Spring 1986 — "Our Leather" (page 2)

> "All of our handbags and portfolios are made out of the same
> leather — a full grain, natural glove tanned cowhide that is
> essentially the same leather used in making baseball gloves. We use
> a special formulation of tanning extracts and emollients that
> renders this rugged leather soft and supple without detracting
> from its strength or beauty."

The **"baseball glove leather"** framing carried verbatim from
1985 — preserved into the first full Sara Lee year.

### Spring 1986 — "Our Twill" (page 2) — NEW

> "Some of our most popular styles are now available in Khaki cotton
> twill, trimmed in Tabac glove tanned cowhide. The durable two-ply
> industrial-weight cotton is treated to resist stains."

First documented presentation of the Twill collection. Note **"some
of our most popular styles"** — Twill is a parallel format applied to
existing leather styles, not a new design line.

### Spring 1986 — "Our Factory" (page 6)

> "Coach products are made in a clean, bright, well-organized factory
> located in downtown New York City. Our workforce is drawn from the
> many races, creeds and nationalities, rich in heritage and skills,
> who make their homes here."

Voice continuity: the **"workforce drawn from many races, creeds and
nationalities"** language is preserved verbatim from Spring 1985.

### Spring 1986 — "Our Distribution" (page 6)

> "Quality leather goods must be made in small, carefully supervised
> lots that do not readily lend themselves to mass production. That
> is why you will find Coach products in only the very best Department
> and Specialty stores."

NOTE: Spring 1986 still says **"Department and Specialty stores"** —
no "Coach Stores" in this distribution paragraph yet.

### Summer 1986 — "Our Concept" (page 2) — REVISED

> "Coach has been making fine quality leather products for **45
> years**. We are gratified that during this time they have become
> known as true American classics — straightforward, functional
> designs that are very well made and never go out of style."

**FIRST primary-source explicit "45 years" anchoring**. Spring 1986
said "over 40 years"; Summer 1986 changes to **"45 years"** —
locking the brand-history narrative to 1986 = 45 years from 1941.

> "The Coach tag, too, has acquired a reputation over the years as
> the trademark of the finest bags, belts, billfolds and portfolios."

> "Coach has the shades and silhouettes to take you through all the
> seasons in classic style. Our ten colors are appropriate for use
> throughout the year." (page 1, "Coach® for All Seasons")

### Summer 1986 — "Our Leather" (page 2) — REFINED

> "All of our handbags and portfolios are made out of the same
> leather — a full-grain, natural glove tanned cowhide that is
> essentially the same leather used in making baseball gloves. We use
> a special formulation of tanning extracts and emollients that
> renders this rugged leather soft and supple without detracting from
> its strength or beauty. We take great care to enhance, rather than
> conceal, the natural markings that give our leather its special
> character."

NEW finding: The phrase **"We take great care to enhance, rather
than conceal, the natural markings"** is added in Summer 1986 — a
deliberate framing of natural-leather imperfections as features,
which becomes a Coach 1990s signature.

### Summer 1986 — "Five Production Stages" (introduced)

The Summer 1986 catalog reorganizes its factory-tour photo-essays
into **five named stages**:
- Stage 1 — Cutting (page 7)
- Stage 2 — Preparation (page 16)
- Stage 3 — Assembly (page 16)
- Stage 4 — Sewing (page 28)
- Stage 5 — Finishing (page 28)

> "In this catalogue our factory pictures and captions form a brief,
> guided tour of our five major production stages. They show some of
> the detailed steps and extra care that go into creating Coach
> quality."

NEW finding: The **five-stage production framework** is a Summer
1986 innovation. It's a **dramatized, retail-ready brand story**
that turns earlier catalogs' scattered factory photos into a
narrative.

> "As many as 85 different steps go into making each Coach product."
> (Summer 1986 page 16, Stage 3 — Assembly)

The **"85 steps" claim** is documented in Summer 1986 — re-appears
in Winter 1986 as "as many as eighty-five operations are required to
produce a finished product" (page 48 photo caption).

### Fall 1986 — "Introducing Coach® for Fall" (page 1) — NEW

> "Inside this catalogue you will find the traditional Coach favorites
> and some new additions to our collection. We call your attention,
> in particular, to our expanded briefcase collection for men and
> women, and some handsome new handbag and tote styles. There is
> also a new, larger, credit card wallet for men."

> "You will also find a number of recently introduced silhouettes
> that have already been embraced as enduring Coach classics."

The **"introducing Coach for Fall" greeting style** is the first
seasonal/topical introduction in our archive — Spring 1986 said
"Additions to Our Collection," Summer 1986 said "Coach for All
Seasons," Fall 1986 says "Introducing Coach for Fall." Each season
in 1986 has a **named editorial framing**.

### Fall 1986 — "Prices valid until December 31, 1986" (page 2)

NEW finding: Fall 1986 explicitly prints the price-validity end
date — same convention as Winter 1985, but applied earlier in the
year now (Fall instead of Winter).

### Fall 1986 — "Our Distribution" (page 2) — REVISED

> "Quality leather goods must be made in small, carefully supervised
> lots that do not readily lend themselves to mass production. That
> is why you will find Coach products in **only Coach Stores and the
> very best Department and Specialty stores**."

The Coach-Stores phrasing first appears in **Fall 1986** in the
distribution paragraph — preserved into Winter 1986.

### Winter 1986 — Lew Frankfort 45th Anniversary letter (inside front)

> "**To Our Loyal Customers and New Friends on the Occasion of Our
> 45th Anniversary**"
>
> "The Coach concept was born in 1941 in a small workshop in
> downtown New York City. All we had then was a short lease, six
> skilled leather workers and our concept."
>
> "We believed (and still do) that there was a market for classic
> leather accessories, made very well out of very good leather, and
> that this was the market we should serve."
>
> "We have grown considerably since then and so has our following.
> Coach Bags, Briefcases, Belts and Accessories are today recognized
> throughout the world as true American Classics, still very well
> made out of very good leather — just as they were 45 years ago."
>
> "Throughout all those years we have been sustained by your
> unwavering loyalty. In the years that lie ahead, we will try very
> hard to maintain the same standards of excellence that you have
> come to expect from all Coach products."
>
> "On behalf of all of us here at Coach (there are now over 250 of
> us), we extend our thanks and appreciation, and the hope that you
> will continue to buy and enjoy our products for many more years to
> come."
>
> "Best wishes,"
> "Lew Frankfort"
> "President"

This is **the most consequential single page in 1986** for brand-
history purposes:

- **First Coach president signature** in our catalog archive (Miles
  Cahn never signed a Coach catalog letter; pre-1986 brand voice was
  unsigned/corporate)
- **First explicit anchoring of the "1941 founding" + "45 years"
  combination** — definitively retiring the earlier "since 1941" /
  "forty years" looseness
- **"Six skilled leather workers"** primary-confirms founding team
  size for the first time in our archive
- **"Over 250 of us"** is the first primary-source Coach headcount
  documented in our archive
- **"True American Classics"** elevated to capitalized branding
- **No mention of Sara Lee** — Coach continues presenting itself as
  a self-contained Coach Leatherware Company despite being a Sara
  Lee subsidiary since July 1985

### Winter 1986 — "Coach® for Everyday" (page 1)

> "At Coach, we see style as an enduring quality, one that lasts
> beyond a certain time or season. In fact, every Coach is designed
> for you to use and enjoy everyday."
>
> "This winter we are extending our timeless approach into new areas.
> We invite your attention to our first collection of fine leather
> gloves for men and women. We are also pleased to introduce a
> complete professional organizer as well as several other personal
> leather goods designed to meet a wide range of needs."

The **"timeless approach"** and **"every Coach is designed for you
to use and enjoy everyday"** framings are new in Winter 1986. The
voice is more **lifestyle-aspirational** than the 1985 "small family
owned" voice — but still modest, not slick.

### Winter 1986 — Hungarian gloves narrative (page 39)

> "It gives us great pleasure to introduce what we feel is a fine
> complement to the Coach tradition of quality leathers."
>
> "After much research into materials and techniques, we located a
> workshop of expert glovemakers in Hungary dedicated to a 200-year
> old reputation for excellence. It is with these craftsmen that we
> entrusted the Coach name, and we are very proud of the results."
>
> "We worked together designing these timeless styles. As with all
> Coach leathers, our gloves are individually cut and hand-finished.
> They're made from the finest gloving leather obtainable — a
> fine-grained European lambskin — tanned to enhance natural markings
> and carefully made so they meet the high-quality standards we set
> for every Coach product."

NEW finding: **First documented Coach product manufactured outside
the USA**. Hungarian glovemakers, European lambskin. The "we located
a workshop" framing implies craft-shop sourcing, not mass production.
By contrast, Cahn-era Coach was militantly "made in NYC."

### Winter 1986 — Wallets construction copy (page 42) — REVISED

> "There is no leather more ideally suited to these accessories than
> Coach Water Buffalo Hide. A variety of careful, time-consuming
> processes transform the hides into leather that is remarkably thin
> and supple, yet very firm."
>
> "Our craftsmen take great care in the making of Coach wallets and
> accessories. They cut only from hides chosen specifically for their
> grain and natural markings. During the assembly, every edge is
> hand-turned and sewn with an average of **14 stitches to the
> inch**. This assures you that the product will last and is sturdy
> enough to carry a good number of cards or papers."

The **stitch count is revised from 12 (Spring/Summer/Fall) to 14
(Winter 1986)** — a primary-source increase in the documented
quality spec. Possibly reflects:
- Tighter QC standards as Coach scales past 250 employees
- A genuine production change (finer thread, slower sewing)
- Marketing tightening (14 sounds better than 12)

## Sub-Collections / Catalog Architecture

### No named sub-collections in 1986

The 1986 catalogs follow the same monolithic architecture as
1981–1985:

```
Bags / Bags & Totes        (pages 1-25 to 1-28)
Bag Accessories            (pages 22-23 to 26-27)
Briefcases / Portfolios    (pages 28-34)
Belts                      (pages 35-38)
[Gloves                    (pages 39-41) — Winter 1986 only]
Wallets & Accessories      (pages 39-48 / 42-56)
```

There is **no "Lightweight Collection," "Sheridan Collection,"
"Classic Collection," or "Continental Series"** in 1986. Per M2,
Lightweight Collection is fully established by Spring 1989 — so
the named-collection era begins between Winter 1986 and Spring 1989.

### "Twill" is the closest thing to a sub-collection in 1986

Spring 1986 page 25 features a "Twill collection" callout:
> "Here is a representative sample of our Twill collection. Specific
> product information and prices can be found on the pages noted."

The Twill products (8050, 8170, 8375, 8430, 8455, 8585, 8625,
8635, 8030) are scattered through the catalog (each Twill variant
appears on the same page as its leather counterpart). They share a
distinct numbering pattern (8000-series) and a distinct material
(Khaki cotton twill, Tabac trim), but are **not formally named as a
"Collection"** with title pages or branded section breaks. **Twill
is the proto-sub-collection.** [coach-catalogs-1960-1989]

### "Five Production Stages" is a brand-narrative sub-architecture

Summer 1986 introduces named production stages threaded throughout
the catalog as a photo-essay framework. This is **not a product
sub-collection** but rather a brand-storytelling device — a precursor
to the more aggressively branded narrative architecture of
1989+ catalogs.

## Retail Network / Order Information

### Toll-free number stable

All four 1986 catalogs use **(800) 544-3004** — same as Winter 1985.
No mid-year change in the number.

### Alaska/Hawaii line introduced in Fall 1986

Fall 1986 page 25 (order form spread) introduces a **separate phone
line for Alaska and Hawaii**:

> "From Alaska and Hawaii, call during business hours: (212) 594-3914."

This is the first documented Alaska/Hawaii fulfillment workaround in
our archive. The main toll-free 800 number still applied to the 48
contiguous states.

### Consumer Service direct line introduced in Fall 1986

Fall 1986 also introduces a **direct Consumer Service Department
phone**:

> "Our Consumer Service Department can be reached directly at
> (212) 594-1560, Monday through Friday from 9 A.M. to 5 P.M.,
> Eastern Time."

Both numbers carry into Winter 1986. NEW finding: Coach grew its
post-sales infrastructure in 1986 — separate Alaska/Hawaii line and
a dedicated consumer-service number, beyond the order-only toll-free
800 number.

### Six-store retail list dropped from catalog

The Spring 1985 / Summer 1985 / Winter 1985 catalogs all printed a
list of six Coach Stores (Paris, NYC Madison Ave, Washington DC,
Seattle, San Francisco, Boston). **None of the four 1986 catalogs
prints this list**. The catalogs only show the corporate HQ
address ("516 West 34th Street, New York, NY 10001") on the order
form envelope.

This is a **format change**, not necessarily a store-count change.
The fact that Fall and Winter 1986 say "you will find Coach products
in only Coach Stores and the very best Department and Specialty
stores" implies Coach Stores remain a real network — but the
catalogs no longer enumerate them. NEW finding: Catalog structure
treats Coach Stores as a brand reference, not a directory.

### Order-form features in 1986

Fall 1986 and Winter 1986 catalogs include detailed order-form
spreads (ordering envelope binds into catalog) listing:
- Toll-free 800 number
- Alaska/Hawaii alternate number
- Federal Express Standard Air Service (overnight) at $7.50 per
  shipping address
- UPS 2nd Day Air at $4.00 per shipping address
- Air Mail at $4.00 per shipping address
- Personal check, money order, American Express, VISA, MasterCard
- Presentation boxes (tissue-wrapped, suitable for gift giving)
- Gift wrapping at $2.00 per item

This is **far more retail-detailed** than 1985 catalogs (which only
listed the toll-free number and "free shipping" claim). Coach has
clearly built out direct-to-consumer fulfillment infrastructure
during 1986. [coach-catalogs-1960-1989]

### Payment options expanded

NEW finding: 1986 catalogs accept American Express, VISA, and
MasterCard. (1985 catalogs were not specific about credit-card
options in the pages we read.)

### Guarantee statement appears in Fall 1986

Fall 1986 page 49 introduces a formal "Our Guarantee" statement:

> "If anything you order from this catalogue does not fully meet your
> expectations, by all means, return it to us and you will receive a
> prompt refund or exchange, whichever you prefer."

NEW finding: 1986 is the first year a formal money-back guarantee is
printed in our catalog archive.

## Open Verification Items

1. **Saddle Pouch family — restoration confirmation.** M1 noted the
   Saddle Pouch Large (9585) and Saddle Pouch Standard (9590) were
   tentatively absent from Winter 1985. Both are present in Fall
   1986 (renamed "Saddle Pouch" and "Saddle Pouch Small"). The
   Spring 1986 catalog has the Saddle Pouch Large at $140 — so the
   Standard/Small variant may have been the only one missing in
   Winter 1985, not both. Worth re-reading Winter 1985 to confirm.

2. **Sling Bag (9710) and Flight Bag (9700) status.** These were
   tentatively discontinued by Winter 1985 (M1). Neither appears in
   any 1986 catalog. **Confirmed retired by Spring 1986.**

3. **The Marketing Tote (9665) name oscillation.**
   - Spring 1986: "Marketing Tote"
   - Summer 1986: "Open Marketing Tote"
   - Fall 1986: "Marketing Tote"
   - Winter 1986: "Open Marketing Tote"

   Two name versions appear within one year. This is unusual
   inconsistency. Worth tracking forward — does the 1987 line
   settle on one?

4. **Compact Pouch (9620) dimension change.**
   - Spring 1986: 7"×7"×1½"
   - Fall 1986: 7½"×7"×1½"

   Half-inch increase in height between Spring and Fall 1986.
   Possibly a typo, possibly a real restyling.

   These are flagged "New" in Spring 1986 belt section. Were they
   absent from Winter 1985? Worth re-reading Winter 1985's belt

   1986 documents this as part of the Five Production Stages

   Coach Leatherware Company" appears on page 1 of every 1986
