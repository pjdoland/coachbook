## Primary findings

### Properties as of June 1, 2000 (NEW manufacturing geography detail)

The S-1 lists **six leased facilities**:

| Location | Use | Sq ft |
|----------|-----|-------|
| 516 West 34th Street, NYC | Corporate | 140,000 |
| Carlstadt, New Jersey | Corporate & Product Development | 93,000 |
| Jacksonville, Florida | Distribution & Customer Service | 560,000 |
| **Medley, Florida** | **Manufacturing** | **107,000** |
| **Lares, Puerto Rico** | **Manufacturing** | 66,000 |
| Florence, Italy | Product Development | 16,000 |

Plus 106 retail + 63 factory leased stores in the U.S.

**Two NEW facts beyond the 2003 10-K:**

1. **Medley, Florida** (107,000 sq ft Manufacturing) — this is the
   long-rumored "Miami-area facility opened 1988" referenced by
   FundingUniverse. The 2003 10-K does NOT list Medley, indicating
   Coach closed it between June 2000 and June 2003. The closure
   date is not stated in either filing — open research item.

2. **Carlstadt was 93,000 sq ft in June 2000**, shrinking to
   55,000 sq ft by June 2003. Between those dates, Coach reduced
   the Carlstadt footprint substantially (matches the "Carlstadt
   warehouse closed in 1999" reference in the S-1's MD&A —
   Carlstadt's warehouse function moved to Jacksonville July 1999;
   Carlstadt retained reduced-scope manufacturing and product
   development).

3. **NYC corporate footprint grew** from 140,000 sq ft (2000) to
   160,000 sq ft (2003) — Coach expanded HQ even as it closed
   manufacturing.

MBA in Marketing from **Columbia University**.

### Predecessor entity name confirmed

The S-1 explicitly states: "Sara Lee acquired the **Coach Leatherware
Company**, our predecessor, in 1985." So the corporate name evolution
is:

- **1941** — Founded as Gail Leather Products (or Manhattan
  Leather Bags per community misnomer)
- **1961** — Cahns purchase; the company began operating under the
  "Coach Leatherware" brand around that time per FundingUniverse
- **By 1985** — Acquired as "Coach Leatherware Company" by Sara Lee
- **June 1, 2000** — "Coach Inc." (Maryland corp) incorporated as
  the public-company parent

The "Coach Leatherware Company" naming is the corporate-form
predecessor to Coach Inc. and is distinct from "Gail Leather
Products" (which may have been the formal corporate name 1941–
~1961) and "Coach Leatherware" (the brand identifier post-Cashin).

### 1985 sales figure: $19.0 million

The S-1 gives 1985 (acquisition-year) sales as $19.0 million. WWD
coverage of the IPO filing said $18 million — close but slightly
different. The S-1 figure $19.0M is the primary canonical number.
Compound annual growth 1985–1997: ~32% → reaching $540.4M peak in
fiscal 1997 (the high-water mark before the 1998–1999 declines).

### 1997 peak / 1998-1999 declines

Per S-1 (primary):
- FY1997 net sales: $540.4M (peak)
- FY1998: -3.4% (first decline since Sara Lee acquisition)
- FY1999: -2.8% (second consecutive decline; net $507.8M)
- First 39 weeks fiscal 2000: $427.2M, +9.7% YoY
- Cause of declines: shift in consumer preferences from leather to
  mixed material/non-leather, plus economic downturn in Asia

This explains why Coach was repositioning the brand under Krakoff
when it filed the S-1. The Bleecker Satchel (2000) and Signature C
(2001) launches were part of this repositioning.

### "First direct mail catalog" by 1980s

The S-1 states the brand "had grown into a niche maker and marketer
of traditionally styled, high-quality leather goods with expanding
national brand recognition, selling our products through upscale
department and specialty stores, our own retail stores **and our
first direct mail catalog**" by the 1980s. This is the primary
anchor for Coach's first direct mail catalog dating to the 1980s.

### Carlstadt warehouse closure 1999

The S-1's MD&A discussion (lines ~1435+, ~5678+) states:
- During 1999, Coach closed the Carlstadt, NJ warehouse
- Manufacturing operations at Carlstadt were significantly reduced
- Carlstadt retained for prototype manufacturing
- $1.1M associated reorganization costs

This is the primary anchor for the Carlstadt → Jacksonville
transition in 1999, completing the picture from the 2003 10-K's
"July 1999 Jacksonville consolidation."
