## Initial findings — Fall 1981 catalog (pages 1–6)

The Fall 1981 catalog is the **first full-numbered-creed-era catalog** sampled (numbered creed introduced earlier in 1970s, this is mid-stream). Documents:

### "Nearly forty years ago" — primary triangulation on founding date

The "Coach factory in downtown New York City" page reads:
> "The Coach Factory was first organized nearly forty years ago, starting out as a small leathergoods workshop employing only six people."

1981 − 40 = **1941**. This is the closest primary-source corroboration of the canonical 1941 date (10-K, S-1). The 1975 catalog's "35 years" (= 1940) and the 1981 catalog's "nearly forty years" (= 1941) bracket the actual founding within a year. The 1941 incorporation date stands as canonical.

### TEN canonical Coach colors in 1981 (with hangtag photos)

Page 3 of the catalog displays **ten leather hangtags** as a color reference, with the caption:
> "Every Coach® Bag carries its own little leather Coach tag on a beaded brass chain. The ten tags pictured above represent the ten Coach® Bag colors now in production."

The ten 1981 colors (with positions on the page):
| Color | Notes |
|-------|-------|
| **Saddle** | Light brown |
| **Navy** | Dark blue |
| **Mocha** | Medium brown |
| **Red** | Saturated red |
| **British Tan** | Coach default warm brown |
| **Putty** | Light beige |
| **Sage** | NEW to our archive — green-grey |
| **Burgundy** | Deep wine |
| **Black** | |
| **Tabac** | Tobacco brown |

**Mahogany is NOT in the 1981 palette** — `colors/mahogany.md` should be flagged as post-1981 introduction.
**Hunter Green is NOT in the 1981 palette** — `colors/hunter-green.md` should be flagged as post-1981 (consistent with our existing era_code: E3-E4 framing).
**Sage** is a documented 1981 color not previously in our archive — needs `colors/sage.md` entry.
**Cream / Bordeaux** not listed; possibly later additions or shoehorn-equivalents (Mocha ≈ Mahogany?).

### Hangtags in 1981 — rectangular with brass beaded chain

The catalog hangtag photographs show **rectangular leather hangtags** stamped "COACH LEATHERWARE" with a beaded brass chain. Not yet the rounded "beanbag" form documented in `authentication/hangtags.md` for later eras. The transition from rectangular to rounded happened post-1981 — not yet pinned.

### Musette Bag (Style 9625) — primary-confirmed 1981

> "N° 9625 Musette Bag — $162. Largest of Coach® Bags, this one is fancied by models, dancers, travelers and photographers. Two large outside pockets and an extra one inside. Solid brass stirrup buckles and turnlocks. Black, Mocha, Tabac, British Tan, Saddle."

- Style **9625**
- Dimensions **16"×11"×5"** (largest Coach bag in 1981)
- Price **$162**
- Colors: Black, Mocha, Tabac, British Tan, Saddle

`models/musette.md` is primary-confirmed for 1981 production. Cashin attribution remains tentative pending pre-1974 catalog evidence. The 1981 production at $162 ("Largest of Coach® Bags") frames it as a flagship NYC-era piece.

### Crescent (Style 9235) — primary-confirmed 1981

> "N° 9235 Crescent — $82. This body hugging Coach® Bag is especially contoured for a snug fit. Shoulder strap is 1¼" wide and adjusts with a pair of matched, solid brass stirrup buckles. The industrial turnlock closure is also made of solid brass. There's an extra compartment inside. Available in all ten Coach® Colors shown on page three."

- Same Style 9235 as in Fall 1975 — model unchanged
- Price **$82**
- Dimensions 10"×10"×3"
- "Industrial turnlock" wording — primary confirmation of "industrial" as Coach's own descriptor

**Reconciliation with `models/crescent-saddle-bag.md`**: research/D's "Crescent Saddle Bag" entry was based on dimensions 10.25"×9.5"×2.875" from Yourgreatfinds. The 1981 catalog Crescent (9235) is 10"×10"×3" — close but not identical. Possible: Crescent and Crescent Saddle Bag are distinct models, OR Yourgreatfinds measured an example with slight variance. Flag for reconciliation.

### Pony Express Pouch (Style 9670) — NEW to archive

> "N° 9670 Pony Express Pouch — $114. Well-organized pouch keeps everything in its place. Two roomy outside pockets; an extra compartment inside. Solid brass stirrup buckles and turnlocks. Black, Mocha, Tabac, British Tan, Burgundy, Navy."

- Style **9670**
- Dimensions **11"×8"×3"**
- Price **$114**
- Colors: 6 of the 10 (excluded Saddle, Putty, Sage, Red)

NOT in our existing archive. Needs `models/pony-express-pouch.md` entry.

### Basic Bag (Style 9455) — same as 1975 Zippered Clutch

> "N° 9455 Basic Bag — $66. The Coach® Bag that started a look. Soft, perfectly proportioned clutch swings off the hip on detachable shoulder straps. Does double duty at night. Inside pocket. Solid brass industrial zipper with wrist strap pull. All ten Coach® Colors shown on page three."

- Style **9455** — same number as 1975's "Zippered Clutch"
- Dimensions 11"×7"×2"
- Price **$66**
- All ten 1981 colors

**Reconciliation**: Coach renamed Style 9455 from "Zippered Clutch" (1975) to "Basic Bag" (1981). Same physical product. The "Basic Bag" name is the brand-canonical name.

The catalog copy "The Coach® Bag that started a look" suggests Style 9455 is one of Coach's foundational silhouettes — possibly a Cashin-era origin. Worth investigating against pre-1974 catalogs.

### "Solid brass" as brand vocabulary

The 1981 catalog uses **"solid brass"** repeatedly: solid brass stirrup buckles, solid brass turnlocks, solid brass industrial zipper. This corroborates `authentication/hardware.md`'s emphasis on solid brass as the Coach standard.

### "THE FACTORY" book — separate Coach publication

The 1981 catalog mentions:
> "N° 1035 'The Factory' — $2. Over 100 striking photographs taken in the Coach Factory have been published in a beautiful 76-page library edition entitled: 'THE FACTORY — Portrait of a Leathergoods Factory in Downtown New York City.' A copy of this handsome photographic essay can be ordered for a nominal sum to cover the cost of shipping."

So Coach published a **76-page photographic book about its factory** as a separate item. NEW to our archive — would be an extraordinary primary source if findable. Style number `1035` distinguishes it from the bag catalog numbering. Worth tracking down via library catalogs or rare-book channels.

### Workforce 1981

> "Some 125 exceptionally talented men and women, working mostly with their hands, are turning out the Coach® Bags and Belts for which this factory is famous."

125 workforce in 1981 — up from "six people" original workshop (per the same page). The S-1 reports 1985 sales of $19M, so this 125-person factory predates the major Sara Lee growth.

### Building: 12-story factory, three blocks west of Macy's

> "Today it occupies two floors of a twelve story factory building, just three blocks west of Macy's."

516 W 34th St is geometrically "three blocks west of Macy's" (Macy's Herald Square at 34th and Broadway, 516 W 34th is between 10th and 11th Aves). The 12-story building detail places Coach in a multi-tenant industrial loft building rather than a standalone factory.

## Initial findings — Fall 1975 catalog (pages 1–8)

This catalog is the **first post-Cashin year** — Cashin's contract ended 1974. The catalog documents:

### "35 years" of Coach — primary-confirmed 1940 founding

The Fall 1975 catalog's "A Word about Our Factory" page opens:
> "In the 35 years we have been manufacturing quality Leather Accessories here in New York, we have grown from a small workshop employing six people to one of the largest and best known leather-goods Factories in the city."

1975 − 35 = **1940**. Coach itself, in 1975 catalog copy, dated its operational start to 1940 — one year before the 1941 incorporation date in the 2003 10-K and 2000 S-1.

**Resolution status**: `disputed`. Both dates are primary-sourced from Coach itself, but address different things — operational start (1940) vs corporate-incorporation date (1941). The 1960 catalog's "over two decades" framing now also fits cleanly: 1960 − 1940 = 20 years exactly.

### Glove-tanned cowhide language already canonical in 1975

The catalog's "A Word about Our Leather" page reads:
> "All COACH bags are made out of a completely natural, uncorrected, full-grain Glove Tanned Cowhide. This remarkable leather — essentially the same leather that is used in the making of Baseball Gloves — has been cured in our own formulation of special tanning extracts in order to render it soft and supple to the touch."

This is the same canonical language Coach used in the 2003 10-K ("classic, high-quality leather goods constructed from 'glove-tanned' leather"). The language was set by 1975.

The catalog also explicitly states:
> "COACH bags are soft and unconstructed. They can be hugged, squeezed and squashed."
> "With normal abuse the leather will gradually darken, ultimately taking on a most pleasing over-all burnished appearance."

This corroborates `authentication/leather.md`'s drum-dyed-through and patina diagnostics, and its "edge: raw or burnished" framing.

### Factory: 516 W 34th St., NYC — confirmed 1975

The catalog footer reads:
> "Factory & Showroom — 516 West 34th St., New York City, (212) 594-1850 — Just off 10th Avenue"

This is the same address as the 2003 10-K corporate HQ. Coach occupied 516 W 34th St continuously from at least 1975 through 2003+ (and 2000 S-1).

The catalog adds: "Having completed our fourth major move a few years ago, we now occupy two entire floors — each a city block in length." So **by ~1973 (a few years before 1975), Coach had moved four times**, with 516 W 34th being the fourth/current factory. The previous three locations are unspecified.

### "Two entire floors — each a city block in length"

The 2003 10-K reports 160,000 sq ft NYC corporate; the 2000 S-1 reports 140,000 sq ft. 1975 catalog describes 516 W 34th as "two entire floors — each a city block in length" — substantially larger than the 2000 footprint. Plausibly: in 1975 the building housed both factory + corporate; by 2000 only corporate remained (manufacturing had moved to Carlstadt, Medley FL, Lares PR, etc.).

### Production: hand-work + machine sewing in 1975

> "There is some machinery in evidence here and there, but for the most part, the work is all done by hand."

The catalog lists production roles: "Cutters, Scivers, Cementers, Folders, Framers, Assemblers and Sewing Machine Operators."

This corroborates `authentication/stitching.md`'s framing that NYC-era Coach used **mixed hand and machine stitching** — the catalog itself names sewing-machine operators alongside hand-work emphasis. The 1960 catalog's saddle-stitch language combined with this 1975 framing suggests:
- 1960: hand-saddle-stitched on small leather goods
- 1975: machine-sewing-machine-operators present, but "most" work hand-done
- Post-1975: gradual transition to more machine work

### Style number transition: 22-25XXX (1960) → 9XXX (1975)

By Fall 1975, all bags use **9XXX** style numbers (one residual `8517` Swinger). The transition from the 1960 `22-25XXX-NNN` format to the 9XXX format happened sometime in the 1962-1974 period, almost certainly coinciding with the women's-handbag line launch under Cashin.

The Swinger (8517) is interesting: 8517 is in the 8000-9000 range, suggesting it predates the broader 9000-series rollout. Possibly 8517 is a Cashin-era style number from the 1960s and the bag remained in catalog into 1975 (one year past her departure).

### "Ruff-Out" suede variants — entirely new finding

Many bags shipped in two finishes:
- Standard (smooth glove-tanned cowhide)
- **"Ruff-Out"** (suede / reverse-grain finish)

Each finish gets its own style number, with the Ruff-Out variant typically +3 from the standard (e.g., 9085 standard, 9088 Ruff-Out; 9170/9178; 9205/9208; 9425/9428; 9440/9448; 9455/9458). This style-number-pair-by-finish convention is **not documented in any of our existing materials** and significantly affects authentication of period bags.

### Logo design

The horse-and-carriage device is visible on the catalog hangtag in Fall 1975 (the photographed bag's beanbag tag clearly shows the device). Same as the 1960 catalog. Logo continuity 1960 → 1975 confirmed.

## Initial findings — 1960 Coach Wallets catalog (pages 1–5)

This is the most consequential single document in the archive, because
it primary-sources the **pre-Cahn Coach era** for the first time.

### "Over two decades" framing — Coach may predate 1941

The 1960 catalog cover copy reads:
> "Creators of hand-fashioned leather goods for over two decades, our
> skilled artisans maintain the strictest quality standards of their
> craft."

In 1960, "over two decades" places Coach's founding at or before 1940.
The Coach 2003 10-K and Coach 2000 S-1 both give **1941** as the
founding year. Possible reconciliations:

1. The "over two decades" copy is rounded marketing language — "20+
   years" since 1941 = 19 years would round up colloquially to "two
   decades" by 1960.
2. Coach's actual operational origin predates the 1941 incorporation
   date — perhaps the workshop existed informally in 1939 or 1940
   before formal incorporation.
3. The Gail Leather Products entity dates to before 1941, and the
   1941 figure refers to a corporate restructuring (e.g., "founded
   1941" = first formal handbag-line introduction, not first-day-of-
   business).

This is a `disputed` open finding. The 1941 date is primary-sourced
from Coach itself (10-K and S-1) so it stays canonical, but the 1960
"over two decades" copy notes a marketing framing that softens the
1941 sharpness.

### COACH LEATHERWARE wordmark in use by 1960

The catalog cover prominently displays:
> COACH LEATHERWARE — "The Finest - for only a Little More"

Plus a horse-and-carriage logo (the Coach hangtag-and-stationery
device). This means **the COACH LEATHERWARE wordmark was in commercial
use by 1960**, predating the USPTO trademark filing (1982) and the
trademark's "First Use in Commerce: September 1973" claim.

Reconciliation with the TSDR finding: the registered trademark Reg
1242098 covers IC 018 (handbags) and IC 025 (belts). The 1973
first-use date is **specific to those goods classes**. Coach used
the COACH LEATHERWARE wordmark on wallets / small leather goods (a
different class) decades earlier; the 1973 date marks the first
commercial use in the women's-handbag category — consistent with
Cashin's 1962 hire, the 1960s product launches, and the Cashin Carry
(1962) maturing into a multi-year handbag line by 1973.

### Pre-Cashin style number format

The 1960 catalog uses the format **`22-25XXX-NNN`**:
- `22-` = constant prefix (likely product-category marker; "22"
  appears across all wallet/SLG entries)
- `25XXX` = sequential five-digit base (range 25506 to 25704+ in this
  catalog)
- `-NNN` = price-tier suffix (matches retail price * 100, e.g.,
  -750 = $7.50, -375 = $3.75)

This is **completely different** from the post-1980 style-number
format documented in `authentication/serial-numbers.md` (the 9XXX
four-digit format). The transition from `22-25XXX-NNN` to `9XXX`
remains an open research item — likely happened in the 1970s when
the numbered creed was introduced.

### Pre-Cashin materials documented

Materials in 1960 production are broader than the post-1962
glove-tanned-cowhide era:
- **Top Grain Cowhide**
- **Hand-Grained English Morocco**
- **Duo-Tone Calf-finished Cowhide**
- **Roser Polished Pigskin**
- **Polished Cowhide**
- **Polished Pigskin**

The "glove-tanned cowhide" identity that Coach is known for came
later (consistent with FundingUniverse's "by the 1960s" framing and
Maharam's coffee-glove leather origin story for Miles Cahn). In
1960, Coach was already established but operated across several
leather and pigskin types.

### Construction features named "traditional with COACH" (1960)

Inset text on the catalog states:
> "Silk lined billfolds, secret pockets, spare key and chargeplate
> holders, removable key assemblies and other up to the minute
> features are traditional with COACH, and therefore receive no
> special mention."

So in 1960, Coach's traditional features included:
- Silk-lined billfolds
- Secret pockets
- Spare key holders
- Chargeplate holders (credit-card pre-cursor)
- Removable key assemblies

### Saddle-stitching named explicitly (Mark 111, 1960)

> "Mark 111 — Hand turned **Saddle Stitched** stud box..."

This is a primary-source confirmation that **saddle stitching was
an authentic Coach construction method in 1960**, contrary to
research/E §7's framing that "NYC-era bags use machine lock-stitch
(despite folklore)."

The reconciliation: pre-1961 small-leather-goods (wallets, stud
boxes, key cases) were saddle-stitched. The post-1962 women's
handbag line under Cashin and Coach's later production used machine
lock-stitch on most seams. Both can be correct depending on era and
product category.

`research/E-authentication.md` §7 needs amendment: the saddle-stitch
folklore IS correct for pre-1961 Coach SLGs.

### Logo design — horse-and-carriage device confirmed pre-1961

The 1960 catalog cover prominently displays the horse-and-carriage
device alongside the COACH LEATHERWARE wordmark. This means the
horse-and-carriage logo predates Cashin and the Cahn purchase. It is
not a Cashin design contribution.
