## 2. Pre-Coach career (1930s–1962)

- **Theatre/film background** seeded her industrial-utility design
  vocabulary. She designed for ~60 films at 20th Century Fox.
- **Adler & Adler sportswear** — first sportswear collection with her
  name on the label. Coined the modern usage of "layering" in 1950.
- **Philip Sills** collaboration brought her to leather work; this was
  the immediate precursor to the Coach engagement. Sills was a leather
  importer and craftsman; Cashin's leather work for Sills (coats,
  jackets) directly informed the bags she would design for Coach.

## 3. Coach tenure (1962–1974)

### Contractual status
- Contracted designer, **never an employee** of Gail Leather / Coach.
  Internally referred to as "the Bonnie Cashin account" (C8).
- Her UCLA papers contain her contractual paperwork (C7), which would
  pin down terms; not yet retrieved here.

### Design vocabulary at Coach
| Element | Cashin attribution | Confidence | Sources |
|---------|-------------------|------------|---------|
| Brass turnlock hardware introduced **1964** at Coach | Inspired by 1940s convertible-car turnlocks she owned; introduced 1964 (two years after her 1962 hire); used **non-exclusively** across Cashin's collections for Coach, Sills, Meyers, Crescendoe-Superb, HBA, and D. Klein | confirmed | C1, C5, vintagefashionguild-cashin (new source — H stream) |
| "Cashin Carry" tote silhouette | Designed for Coach Fall 1962 collection | confirmed | C8 |
| Side pockets on bags | Cashin contribution | likely | C8 |
| Kissing-lock coin purse | Cashin signature | likely | C9 (verify in C5/C7) |
| Brighter color palette | Cashin contribution | likely | C8 |
| Industrial hardware aesthetic generally | Hers across entire career, not just Coach | confirmed | C1, C5 |

### Note on the turnlock
- Introduction **1964**, not 1962. The Cashin Carry tote (Fall 1962)
  predates the turnlock by two years; early Cashin Carry totes used
  different closures (kiss-lock or open-top variants documented).
- The fastener was **not Coach-exclusive**. Per Vintage Fashion Guild
  / dealer reference, the same turnlock appeared on Cashin's
  collections for Philip Sills, Meyers, Crescendoe-Superb, HBA, and
  D. Klein. Coach is most associated with it because of longevity
  and brand visibility, not exclusivity.

The "1940s convertible turnlock" origin story is widely repeated and
appears to trace to Cashin herself in interviews. C1 attributes the
inspiration; C5 (Stephanie Lake's archive) is the most authoritative
source on this and should be checked against original interview audio
or transcripts in the UCLA papers.

## 5. The over-attribution problem

The vintage-Coach community frequently calls bags introduced after
1974 "Cashin designs." They are not. Cashin's contract ended in 1974;
bags introduced 1975 onward were by Coach's in-house team or other
contracted designers. Examples of likely over-attribution to flag in
the archive:

- Court bag (introduction date needs verification; if post-1974, it
  is not Cashin's work even if it uses Cashin-era vocabulary).
- Willis (1980s introduction per Tier 3 sources — definitively
  post-Cashin).
- Station bag (need date).
- Stewardess / Legacy (need date).

The archive's per-model authentication block must distinguish:
1. **Designed by Cashin** (1962–1974, with primary documentation).
2. **In production during Cashin era** (overlapping but not necessarily
   designed by her).
3. **Still produced after Cashin left** (Cashin-era models that
   continued; she should not be credited for bags newly introduced
   post-1974 even if they use her vocabulary).
4. **Cashin-influenced** (post-1974 in-house bags using turnlock,
   side pocket, etc. — pay tribute, not authorship).

## 6. Post-Coach (1974–2000)

- Continued independent design work.
- Retired from fashion 1985 (C1).
- Established the Innovative Design Fund and the James Michelin
  Distinguished Visitors Program at Cal Tech via her estate.
- Long Island Community Foundation manages a Bonnie Cashin Fund (C6).
