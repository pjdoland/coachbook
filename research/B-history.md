# Research stream B — Coach company history & timeline

Compiled 2026-05-01 from public web sources. Every event tagged with a
confidence level and at least one citation. Where multiple sources
disagree, both are recorded and the entry is marked `disputed`.

**Important corrections to earlier plan drafts:**
- Original company name was **Gail Leather Products**, not "Manhattan
  Leather Bags." (W. Sources A1, A2)
- Cahns **purchased** Gail Leather in 1961; they did not found the company
  in 1941. Coach predates the Cahns by ~5 years.
- Bonnie Cashin papers are at **UCLA**, not LACMA (multiple drafts had
  this wrong).
- Lew Frankfort became **president** in 1985 (under Sara Lee), then
  **chairman/CEO in 1995**, not 1985 as one source wording suggested.

## Timeline

### 1941 — Coach founded as Gail Leather Products
- Confidence: confirmed
- Sources: A1, A2, A4, A8
- Detail: Family-run workshop in a Manhattan loft, six leatherworkers,
  produced small leather goods (men's wallets, billfolds) by hand.
  Founders not the Cahns.
- Open: founders' names not located in free sources; should be in
  Coach's own S-1 (A9).

### 1946 — Miles Cahn joins Gail Leather Products
- Confidence: confirmed
- Sources: A1, A4
- Detail: Miles and Lillian Cahn knowledgeable about leather business;
  joined the contractor as employees, not owners.

### 1950 — Miles Cahn begins managing factory operations
- Confidence: likely
- Sources: A1
- Detail: Single source; verify against A9 if possible.

### c. 1960 — Coach brand of cowhide purses introduced
- Confidence: likely
- Sources: A1
- Detail: Per FundingUniverse, the "Coach" brand of cowhide handbags
  was introduced as a signature product around 1960 — predating the
  Cahn purchase. This is important: Coach-branded handbags existed
  before Bonnie Cashin's 1962 hiring.

### 1961 — Miles and Lillian Cahn purchase Gail Leather Products
- Confidence: confirmed
- Sources: A1, A2, A4
- Detail: Leveraged buyout from the original owners. Marks the
  transition to family ownership.

### 1962 — Bonnie Cashin hired as designer
- Confidence: confirmed
- Sources: A4, A6, A8
- Detail: Hired at Lillian Cahn's suggestion to start a women's handbag
  line. Cashin was a contracted designer, never a Gail Leather or
  Coach employee. Internally referred to as "the Bonnie Cashin
  account." First "Cashin Carry" collection launched Fall 1962.

### 1962–1974 — Bonnie Cashin tenure
- Confidence: confirmed
- Sources: A4, A6, A8
- Detail: Twelve-year design partnership. Cashin introduced side
  pockets, kissing-lock coin purse, brighter colors, and the "Cashin
  Carry" tote silhouette (Fall 1962). Brass turnlock hardware came
  later — see 1964 entry.

### 1964 — Brass turnlock introduced
- Confidence: likely (Tier 2/3 source; primary patent or catalog not
  yet pulled)
- Sources: Vintage Fashion Guild (vintagefashionguild-cashin), new
  source under H stream
- Detail: Cashin introduced the brass turnlock/toggle fastening in
  1964, two years after her 1962 hire. The same fastener appeared
  non-exclusively across her collections for multiple manufacturers
  including Coach, Philip Sills, Meyers, Crescendoe-Superb, HBA, and
  D. Klein. Coach is most associated with it because of longevity
  and brand visibility, not exclusivity.

### Late 1970s / early 1980s — mail-order business launched; first specialty stores opened
- Confidence: likely
- Sources: A1
- Detail: Date imprecise in source; need to refine.

### 1979 — Lew Frankfort joins Coach
- Confidence: confirmed
- Sources: A3 (Lew Frankfort biographical)
- Detail: Joined as VP of New Business Development.

### July 1985 — Cahns sell Coach to Sara Lee Corporation
- Confidence: confirmed
- Sources: A1, A2, A4, **coach-10k-fiscal-2003** (PRIMARY)
- Detail: Specific to month (July 1985) per the 2003 10-K primary
  source. Reported sale price ~$30 million. Sara Lee acquired the
  factory, six boutiques, and the Madison Avenue flagship. Cahns
  reportedly sold to focus on Coach Farm goat dairy in Gallatinville
  NY (started 1983).

### July 1985 — Lew Frankfort named president of Coach
- Confidence: confirmed
- Sources: A1, A3, **coach-10k-fiscal-2003** (PRIMARY)
- Detail: Specific to month (July 1985) per primary 10-K. Frankfort
  took the president role under Sara Lee ownership in the same month
  Sara Lee acquired Coach. He had joined Coach in 1979 as VP New
  Business Development. (NOT CEO; that came in 1995, when he became
  Chairman and CEO of Coach as a Sara Lee subsidiary; he became CEO
  of Coach Inc. when the public company was incorporated June 1, 2000.)

### Early 1986 — Coach expands in department stores
- Confidence: likely
- Sources: A1
- Detail: New boutiques in Macy's and Bamberger's; mall stores
  multi-state.

### 1987 — Madison Avenue flagship reopens; workforce doubles
- Confidence: likely
- Sources: A1

### 1988 — Manufacturing expansion and international debut
- Confidence: likely
- Sources: A1
- Detail: New Miami-area manufacturing facility; first international
  boutiques in England and Japan; silk scarves introduced (later
  discontinued).

### 1989 — Sales reach $100 million; international expansion continues
- Confidence: likely
- Sources: A1
- Detail: Freestanding London store on Sloane Street; first
  Continental European store in Stuttgart, Germany.

### 1990 — Trade-dress infringement ruling
- Confidence: likely
- Sources: A1
- Detail: Federal court awards Coach damages for trade-dress
  infringement against competitors. Specific case citation not in
  source — should be findable on PACER or court records.

### May 1991 — Annual sales reach $150 million
- Confidence: likely
- Sources: A1
- Detail: Coach for Business stores open (two locations).

### Early 1992 — Worldwide store count: 53
- Confidence: likely
- Sources: A1
- Detail: Sheridan and Camden collections introduced.

### Fall 1992 — Leather outerwear and fabric luggage launched
- Confidence: likely
- Sources: A1

### Spring 1993 — Manhattan collection launches
- Confidence: likely
- Sources: A1
- Detail: First major use of bright colors.

### Late 1994 — Two-level New York flagship opens
- Confidence: likely
- Sources: A1
- Detail: Soho collection introduced 1994 per Coach.com Soho product
  page (Tier 1 brand source).

### 1995 — Frankfort becomes Chairman and CEO; Sonoma collection
- Confidence: confirmed
- Sources: A1, A3
- Detail: Frankfort's title shift from president to chairman/CEO.
  First freestanding Japan store opens. Sonoma collection launches.
- Note: One Encyclopedia.com snippet conflated 1985 (president) and
  1995 (CEO) into "1985 through 2014" — disputed wording, but the
  more detailed sources agree on the 1995 CEO date.

### Fiscal 1996 — Pacific Rim flagship opens (Waikiki)
- Confidence: likely
- Sources: A1

### December 1996 — Reed Krakoff joins Coach
- Confidence: confirmed
- Sources: **coach-10k-fiscal-2003** (PRIMARY)
- Detail: Krakoff joined Coach in **December 1996** as Senior Vice
  President and Executive Creative Director. Promoted to President,
  Executive Creative Director in September 1999. Prior background:
  Polo/Ralph Lauren Senior Designer (Jul 1988 – Apr 1992); Tommy
  Hilfiger Head Designer Sportswear (Apr 1992 – Jan 1993); Tommy
  Hilfiger SVP Marketing/Design/Communications (Jan 1993 – Dec 1996).
  Education: Parsons (A.A.S. Fashion Design), Tufts (B.A. Economics
  and Art History).

### 1997 — First licensing deal: Movado for watches
- Confidence: likely
- Sources: A1

### Fiscal 1998 — Motorola leather phone case licensing
- Confidence: likely
- Sources: A1

### 1999 — Baker Furniture licensing; coach.com launches (October)
- Confidence: likely
- Sources: A1

### June 1, 2000 — Coach Inc. (Maryland) incorporated
- Confidence: confirmed
- Sources: **coach-10k-fiscal-2003** (PRIMARY)
- Detail: The public-company parent Coach Inc. was incorporated as a
  Maryland corporation on June 1, 2000. Distinct from the historical
  Coach business (Gail Leather Products and successors). Frankfort's
  Coach Inc. CEO/Chairman tenure dates from this incorporation date.

### October 2000 — Coach IPO
- Confidence: confirmed
- Sources: A1, **coach-10k-fiscal-2003** (PRIMARY)
- Detail: Per the 2003 10-K: "In October 2000, Coach was listed on the
  New York Stock Exchange and sold **17.0 million shares** of stock in
  an initial public offering." This resolves the disputed
  17%/19.5%/19.9% percentage figures from secondary sources — the
  primary stat is share count. Other sources report ~$118M raised at
  $16/share; the IPO date was October 5, 2000 per WWD coverage of
  pricing. Ticker COH on NYSE.

### April 2001 — Sara Lee final divestiture of Coach
- Confidence: confirmed
- Sources: A1, **coach-10k-fiscal-2003** (PRIMARY)
- Detail: Per the 2003 10-K: "In April 2001, Sara Lee ended its
  ownership with a distribution of its remaining shares in Coach via
  an exchange offer." Coach Inc. becomes fully independent.

### July 1999 — Jacksonville consolidation
- Confidence: confirmed
- Sources: **coach-10k-fiscal-2003** (PRIMARY)
- Detail: "In July 1999, Coach consolidated its worldwide warehousing,
  distribution and repair functions into one location in Jacksonville,
  Florida" — a 560,000 sq ft facility. This date is the **primary
  anchor for the Coach Repair operation moving to Jacksonville**;
  prior to July 1999, Coach Repair was distributed across multiple
  US locations.

### April 2002 — Lares, Puerto Rico facility closes
- Confidence: confirmed
- Sources: **coach-10k-fiscal-2003** (PRIMARY)
- Detail: Coach ceased operations at its Lares, Puerto Rico
  manufacturing facility in April 2002. This was Coach's **last
  Coach-operated manufacturing facility**; from this point forward,
  all production is by independent contractors. The Puerto Rico
  facility's start date is not in this 10-K — research item.

### 2001 — Signature collection (Double C logo) introduced
- Confidence: confirmed
- Sources: A1, A4
- Detail: Marks the visual brand pivot away from solid-leather
  identity toward logo monogram. End-of-vintage marker for this
  archive.

### November 2001 — First jewelry collection
- Confidence: likely
- Sources: A1
- Detail: Joint venture with Carolee Designs, Inc.

## Disputes / unresolved

### Frankfort role title timing
- Some sources merge "president 1985" and "CEO 1995" into a single
  "since 1985" claim. Resolution: A1 distinguishes; treat as A1's
  reading until contradicted by primary.

### "Manhattan Leather Bags" name
- Several blog/community sources call the original company "Manhattan
  Leather Bags." Authoritative trade sources (A1, A2, A4) call it
  "Gail Leather Products." Resolution: use Gail Leather Products as
  canonical; note "Manhattan Leather Bags" as a community misnomer.

### Original 1941 founders
- No free source names them. They are not the Cahns. Pursue via the
  Coach S-1 prospectus or NYTimes archive.

### Cashin Carry exact debut date
- "Fall 1962" widely cited; need a primary catalog or press source
  for the exact month.
