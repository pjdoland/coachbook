# O2 — Coach Catalogs 1987 (Early Spring + Spring + Early Summer + Early Fall + Holiday) Deep-Read

Primary deep-read of five 1987 Coach catalog PDFs:

- `sources/primary/catalogs/Archived Vintage Coach Catalogues/1987-early-spring.pdf` (cover: "Early Spring 1987"; embossed bag image; ~56 printed pp; ~28 PDF pp; carries a yellow toll-free-number-change card insert)
- `sources/primary/catalogs/Archived Vintage Coach Catalogues/1987-spring.pdf` (cover: "Spring 1987"; embossed bag; toll-free single line)
- `sources/primary/catalogs/Archived Vintage Coach Catalogues/1987-early-summer.pdf` (cover: "Early Summer 1987"; introduces the Coach Lightweights Collection)
- `sources/primary/catalogs/Archived Vintage Coach Catalogues/1987-early-fall.pdf` (cover: "Early Fall 1987"; F87A order code; ~64 printed pp; the largest 1987 catalog, omnibus seasonal)
- `sources/primary/catalogs/Archived Vintage Coach Catalogues/1987-holiday.pdf` (cover: "Holiday 1987"; H87E order code; back-cover stamped NOV 12 1987 receipt date; the first 1987 catalog with the Coach Stores list printed on the back)

All five carry the same registry source ID `[coach-catalogs-1960-1989]`.

## Headline Findings

1. **1987 is a five-catalog year, not the spring/fall biennial cadence.** Coach issued five distinct seasonal catalogs in 1987 (Early Spring, Spring, Early Summer, Early Fall, Holiday), each with cover-named season. This continues the multi-issue cadence already in place in 1986 and contrasts sharply with the spring/fall pair pattern from the early 1980s (cf. N2 1983). [coach-catalogs-1960-1989]
2. **Catalog naming convention shift mid-1987.** The first three 1987 catalogs (Early Spring, Spring, Early Summer) use embossed front-cover-only catalogs with no printed retail-store list. Holiday 1987 introduces the printed Coach Stores list on the back cover — the first 1987 catalog to do so. The Early Fall catalog is the omnibus 64-page reference — clearly the "main" catalog of the year — while the others are issue-specific seasonal supplements. [coach-catalogs-1960-1989]
3. **Toll-free phone-number transition documented in Early Spring 1987.** The Early Spring 1987 catalog carries a yellow card insert announcing the change from `(800) 544-3004` to `(800) 223-8647`. By Spring 1987 only the new number `(800) 223-8647` is printed in the catalog. This pins the Coach toll-free number transition to **Q1 1987**. [coach-catalogs-1960-1989]
4. **City Bag SKU renumbering: 9785 → 9790.** In Early Spring 1987 the City Bag is **No. 9785** marked "New". By Spring 1987 the City Bag has been renumbered to **No. 9790** — and the **9785 SKU is reassigned to a new "Zipper Tote"** introduced by Early Summer 1987. This is a **same-season SKU swap** within 1987 and explains a known anomaly in 1987-era serial numbers. The 9790 City Bag carries the "New" tag through Early Summer, then drops it by Early Fall — a typical "freshman year" treatment. [coach-catalogs-1960-1989]
5. **Coach Lightweights Collection debuts in Early Summer 1987.** The Early Summer 1987 catalog opens with **"Introducing the Coach Lightweights Collection"** — the first Lightweights appearance in any Coach catalog. Ten Lightweights styles in the 4xxx range debut at once: 4010 / 4015 / 4020 Drawstring Sacs (Large/Medium/Small), 4025 Shoulder Pouch, 4030 Drawstring Tote, 4040 Swinger, 4050 Shoulder Bag, 4055 / 4060 Soft Satchels (Standard/Large), 4065 Light Tote. An 11-color palette is **specially developed for this collection** (Taupe, Saddle, Buckskin, Bone, Khaki, Honey, Cocoa, Wine, Blue, Red, Black). This finding **revises** any earlier assumption (e.g., M2-derived hypotheses) that the Lightweight Collection began in 1989. **It launched in mid-1987.** [coach-catalogs-1960-1989]
6. **Madison Satchel established by Early Spring 1987 — debut window 1986.** The 9725 Madison Satchel is **already in the catalog** in Early Spring 1987 with no "New" tag. The 9765 Madison Satchel Large is referenced in the Early Spring strap chart and described in Spring 1987 as **"inspired by the popularity of the Madison Satchel"** — confirming the standard 9725 Madison was an established model by Q1 1987. This pins the Madison Satchel debut to **1986** (likely Fall or Holiday 1986). [coach-catalogs-1960-1989]
7. **Spectator Bag 9795 debuts in Early Fall 1987.** The 9795 Spectator Bag is marked "New" in Early Fall 1987 and persists into Holiday 1987. This is a fresh same-year addition. [coach-catalogs-1960-1989]
8. **Station Bag 5130 returns by Early Fall 1987.** The 5130 Station Bag carries copy reading **"This classic Coach bag for men and women makes its return to our catalogue"** in Early Fall 1987 — confirming the Station Bag is a Cashin/Cahn-era model being **reissued** in 1987 (not a brand-new design). [coach-catalogs-1960-1989]
9. **Willis (9927) is ABSENT from all five 1987 catalogs.** Special-priority verification: the Willis bag does **not** appear in Early Spring, Spring, Early Summer, Early Fall, or Holiday 1987. This **narrows** Willis's debut window from "1985–1988" to **1988 or later** (within the catalog cycle). [coach-catalogs-1960-1989]
10. **Mahogany is ABSENT from the 1987 palette.** All five 1987 catalogs ship a stable 10-color core palette: **Burgundy, Putty, Mocha, Tabac, Bone, British Tan, Navy, Red, Flannel Grey, Black**. Mahogany does not appear. This pins the Mocha→Mahogany transition to **1988 or later** — definitely not within 1987. [coach-catalogs-1960-1989]
11. **Court Bag (9870) and Soho (4082) are also ABSENT** from all five 1987 catalogs. These are **post-1987 introductions**. [coach-catalogs-1960-1989]
12. **Retail network 1987 (back-cover Coach Stores list, Holiday 1987):** New York (754 Madison Avenue / 212-594-1581 + South Street Seaport / 212-947-1727), Dallas (Dallas Galleria / 214-392-1772), Seattle (417 University Street / 206-382-1772), San Francisco Area (164 Grant Avenue / 415-392-1772 + Stanford Shopping Center / 415-327-1772), Costa Mesa CA (Crystal Court, South Coast Plaza / 714-549-1772). Seven retail locations. This represents a **major expansion** from the three U.S. stores documented in 1983 (Madison, DC, Seattle). DC has **dropped off** the retail list. [coach-catalogs-1960-1989]
13. **Mid-Cahn-era / post-Sara Lee brand voice intact.** No designer is named. No mention of Sara Lee. The brand-voice anchor is **"45 years"** of leather-making (consistent with 1941 founding). Retail copy emphasizes "true American classics," "natural leather," "solid brass," "85 steps." Cashin is **not credited** in any 1987 catalog. [coach-catalogs-1960-1989]

## Brand Voice 1987

Across all five 1987 catalogs the brand-voice frame is stable and uses the same anchors. Verbatim excerpts (transcribed from front matter):

> "Coach has been making fine quality leather products for 45 years. We are gratified that during this time they have become known as true American classics — straightforward, functional designs that are very well made and never go out of style." — *Early Fall 1987, p.2; Holiday 1987, p.2 (essentially identical)*

The Early Spring 1987 front matter uses the same "45 years" anchor. (1941 + 45 = 1986; the catalog rounds to 45 across the entire 1987 cycle, just as 1983's catalogs uniformly used "forty years" — see N2 — even though 1941+40=1981. Coach uses round-number anchors that lag the precise arithmetic by a year or two.)

Other brand-voice motifs present in 1987:

- **"Quality and Commitment"** — heading on every front-matter page, paired with the "85 steps" production claim.
- **"Excellence in Service"** — heading announcing 3–4 working day order processing and free shipping to all 50 states.
- **"Our Guarantee"** — return-and-refund / return-and-exchange promise on every catalog.
- **"True American classics"** — repeated exactly in Lightweights front matter (Early Summer): *"As with every Coach product, these new Coach Lightweights are true American classics — designed with a timeless quality, so that they may be used and enjoyed in every season throughout the years."*
- **"Glove Tanned Cowhide"** — capitalized as a proper noun for the Lightweights leather — *"full-grain, natural Glove Tanned Cowhide — carefully prepared in a special process that renders it soft, supple, and long wearing."*
- **"It's Not a Coach Bag Without the Coach Tag"** — a recurring sub-heading in 1987 catalogs (Early Spring, Early Fall) tying the hangtag to the brand-promise pitch.
- **"85 steps"** — the production anchor: *"Only then do our skilled craftsmen begin to cut and sew Coach products in a process that requires as many as 85 steps before an item is complete."*
- **No designer is named.** Cashin is not mentioned. No creative director / designer line. No reference to Sara Lee.
- **No logo / no monogram fabric.** Visuals show no canvas, no logo print, no signature pattern — exclusively all-leather construction with the small leather "COACH LEATHERWARE" hangtag.

The cover headlines on all five 1987 catalogs use the same template:

> "A catalogue of classic Coach® bags, belts, wallets, briefcases and other accessories made out of natural leather and solid brass."

This is essentially identical to the Fall 1983 cover headline (cf. N2) — only the seasonal date below the headline changes. The "wallets, briefcases and other accessories" expansion from the 1983 "bags, belts and accessories" reflects the broader product mix.

## Palette 1987

Stable 10-color **Coach Classics** palette across all five catalogs:

> **Burgundy · Putty · Mocha · Tabac · Bone · British Tan · Navy · Red · Flannel Grey · Black**

(Note the Early Spring 1987 catalog displays the swatch chart in this order: Burgundy, Flannel Grey, Red, Mocha, Tabac, Bone, British Tan, Navy, Putty, Black — same colors, different display sequence. Early Fall and Holiday list them: Burgundy, Putty, Mocha, Tabac, Bone, British Tan, Navy, Red, Flannel Grey, Black.)

Lightweights-only 11-color palette (debut Early Summer 1987, unchanged in Early Fall and Holiday):

> **Taupe · Saddle · Buckskin · Bone · Khaki · Honey · Cocoa · Wine · Blue · Red · Black**

The **Lightweights palette is deliberately distinct** from the Coach Classics palette. Front-matter copy states: *"We call your attention to the color palette specially developed for this new collection. It includes a range of deep, rich earth tones that seem particularly well-suited to the months ahead."* And later: *"This new palette of eleven distinctive colors complements the new silhouettes and also gives you a broad range of options — appropriate for every climate and every season."*

Overlaps between the two palettes: only **Bone, Red, Black** appear in both. The Lightweights collection is positioned as a parallel family, not an alternate finish on the same colorways.

**Mocha → Mahogany transition:** Mahogany **does NOT appear in any 1987 catalog**. The 1987 palette runs the full year on Mocha. This pins the transition firmly to **1988 or later**. (See `N4-cross-cut-1981-1984.md` for the prior open verification item; this catalog cycle resolves the lower bound: Mahogany did not exist in Coach catalogs through end-1987.)

**Sage** — already retired by 1985 per M1; absent from all 1987 catalogs (confirmed).

**Putty** — present in all 1987 catalogs (the Putty/Bone pair both appear).

**Flannel Grey** — present in all 1987 catalogs. (When Flannel Grey first entered the palette is an open question — see Open Verification Items.)

## Sub-Collections / Catalog Architecture

The Early Fall 1987 catalog formalizes a **three-collection structure** for the first time:

1. **Coach Classics** (the historical core line — 9xxx and some 5xxx SKUs — all in glove-tanned cowhide, 10-color core palette, classic silhouettes from Cashin/Cahn era through 1987 introductions like City Bag, Madison Satchel, Spectator Bag).
2. **Coach Lightweights Collection** (4010–4065, debut Early Summer 1987, lighter-weight glove-tanned cowhide, **11-color separate palette**, "unconstructed" / "pouch-like shapes" silhouettes — the soft, slouchy, casual register).
3. **Coach for Business** (men's and women's briefcases, portfolios, organizers — a refined business-accessory line around the 5xxx SKU range, plus the Lefax-system 4715 Organizer at $150).

The Early Fall catalog explicitly delineates these in its front-matter contents page. This is **the earliest catalog with this tri-collection structure** — earlier catalogs (1985, 1986) had not used the "Lightweights" branding because the line did not exist yet.

The Holiday 1987 catalog continues the tri-collection structure, with the additional adjustment of **printing the retail-store list** on the back cover (which the Early Fall did not).

The Lightweights Collection front-matter copy is worth quoting in full (Early Summer 1987 & Early Fall 1987, near-identical):

> "A Few Words About Coach® Lightweights. As with all our products, the Coach Lightweights Collection is made to be worn and enjoyed for many years. The leather is full-grain, natural Glove Tanned Cowhide — carefully prepared in a special process that renders it soft, supple, and long wearing. Coach Lightweights are classically styled, unconstructed bags meant to be worn often — for work and leisure. The new pouch-like shapes are designed to take full advantage of the leather's light, soft, supple qualities and include the details you have come to expect from Coach — solid brass hardware, outside seam bindings and handy inside pockets."

The Lightweights are positioned as **timeless** and **classic** rather than as a fashion experiment — Coach's brand-voice playbook applied to a new soft-construction silhouette family.

## Catalog Naming Convention Shift

In 1983 (cf. N2), Coach issued **two** catalogs per year (Spring + Fall). By 1987 the cycle has expanded to **five** catalogs per year:

- **Early Spring** (mid-winter prep for spring; Q1)
- **Spring** (March–April)
- **Early Summer** (May–June)
- **Early Fall** (August–September; the omnibus reference catalog)
- **Holiday** (October–November; the gift-driven retail-store catalog)

This is **a major retail/marketing rhythm shift** — not just more catalogs but a more granular seasonal targeting structure. By 1987 Coach is positioning itself as a multi-issue, season-responsive direct-mail brand alongside its retail expansion.

The "Early X" prefix (Early Spring, Early Summer, Early Fall) appears to be a Coach-internal convention. It signals the **issue date** rather than the seasonal collection itself; the catalogs are roughly bi-monthly rather than seasonal.

The Early Fall 1987 catalog is the **omnibus reference** — 64 printed pages, contents page, complete tri-collection structure, comprehensive briefcase/wallet line. The other 1987 catalogs are issue-targeted supplements that focus on the new arrivals and selected established items.

The **Holiday 1987** catalog is the first 1987 catalog to print the Coach Stores retail list on its back cover. (The Early Fall, despite being the largest, did not.) This suggests Holiday 1987 was the consumer gift-buying showpiece, whereas Early Fall was the direct-mail / catalog-buyer ordering reference.

This naming shift between 1983 (Spring/Fall) and 1987 (Early Spring / Spring / Early Summer / Early Fall / Holiday) is a **brand-marketing maturity** signal that fits the post-Sara Lee period: Sara Lee acquired Coach in July 1985, and by 1987 the catalog cadence has institutionalized to a more retail-aligned five-issue rhythm.

## Summary

The 1987 catalog cycle is a high-density year of brand and product evolution at Coach:

- **Catalog cadence formalizes** to five issues per year (Early Spring, Spring, Early Summer, Early Fall, Holiday) — up from two issues per year in 1983.
- **The Coach Lightweights Collection debuts** in Early Summer 1987 — a major sub-line introduction with ten new SKUs (4010–4065), an entirely separate 11-color palette, and dedicated front-matter copy. **This finding revises any earlier hypothesis (e.g., M2's framing) that put Lightweights at 1989.**
- **City Bag (9790)** is a 1987 introduction with a documented same-year SKU swap (9785 → 9790) and the 9785 SKU then reassigned to the new Zipper Tote.
- **Madison Satchel (9725)** is established by Early Spring 1987 — pin to a **1986** debut window.
- **Spectator Bag (9795)**, **Carrier Bag (9800)**, **Camera Bag (9760)**, **Large Tote (9780)** are all 1987 introductions.
- **Station Bag (5130)** is a Cashin/Cahn-era reissue marked "makes its return" in Early Fall 1987.
- **Willis (9927), Court Bag (9870), Soho (4082), and Mahogany are all ABSENT from 1987.** All four post-1987 introductions.
- **The retail Coach Stores network expands to seven locations** (NYC × 2, Dallas, Seattle, SF × 2, Costa Mesa) — first printed inside Holiday 1987's back cover.
- **Toll-free number transition** from `544-3004` to `223-8647` happens in Q1 1987.
- **Brand voice unchanged** — "45 years," "true American classics," "85 steps," no designer credit, no Cashin reference, no Sara Lee mention.
- **Mid-year price increase** between Early Summer and Early Fall 1987 (8–10% on multiple core models).

This is a foundational year for Coach as a multi-line retail brand; the tri-collection architecture (Coach Classics + Lightweights + Coach for Business) and the seven-store retail footprint set the structure that carries forward into the late-1980s catalogs.
