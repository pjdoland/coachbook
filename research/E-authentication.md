## 1. Creed evolution by era

| Era | Approximate years | Creed presence | Diagnostic wording |
|-----|-------------------|----------------|--------------------|
| Pre-creed | early 1960s | Small metal interior tag only | No leather creed (E1) |
| Stamped-leather only | late 1960s–early 1970s | Leather stamp inside, no creed patch and no number | No serial (E1) |
| Numbered creed, US-made | mid-1970s–1994 | Creed patch with serial | "COACH LEATHERWARE / Made in United States" — note the small-caps "LEATHERWARE" beneath "COACH" (E1, E2) |
| Numbered creed, transitional | 1994–~1996 | New letter-number serial introduced | Wording overlap; some still "LEATHERWARE" (E1) |
| Offshore production begins | 1994–2001 | Creed continues | "Made in Costa Rica," "Made in Hungary," "Made in Turkey," "Made in Dominican Republic," "Made in Mexico," "Made in Italy" (Italy used for Madison/Gramercy exotic lines) (E1, E12) |
| Modern (post-vintage) | 2000s+ | Creed continues, branding updates | "Handcrafted in China" common (E1) |

### Key textual diagnostic
- **"LEATHERWARE" stamped in tiny letters beneath "COACH" on the creed
  indicates pre-1994 manufacture** (E1). This is the single most
  cited textual anchor for dating.

### NYC factory
- Manufactured in New York City until **circa 1980** (multiple Tier 3
  sources). After that, US production moved to other US factories,
  including a Miami-area facility opened 1988 (per FundingUniverse,
  research file B). "MADE IN NEW YORK CITY, U.S.A." wording appears
  on the earliest numbered creeds.

## 2. Serial / style number formats

### Pre-1994: random unique serial
- Format: digit-only, formats like `xxx-xxxx` (1970s) and `xxxx-xxx`
  (1980s) (E3).
- These are **unique serial numbers**, not style numbers. They cannot
  be decoded for style or factory. (E3, E5)
- Marked `tentative` — the dash position differing between 70s and
  80s is repeated across Tier 3 sources without primary citation.

### Post-1994: letter-number style code
Per E1 (the most specific Tier 3 source):
- **Pattern**: `[A-M][year-digit][factory-letter]-[style-number]`
- **First character (letter)**: month code, A=Jan through M=Dec,
  **skipping I**.
  - A=Jan, B=Feb, C=Mar, D=Apr, E=May, F=Jun, G=Jul, H=Aug, J=Sep,
    K=Oct, L=Nov, M=Dec.
- **Second character**: year code. 4=1994, then cycles 5,6,7,8,9,0,
  1,2,3 = 1995–2003. Then double digits (04=2004 onward).
- **Third character (letter)**: factory code. Coach never publicly
  released this list. E12 cites `P` = Costa Rica.
- **Last 4–5 digits**: style number.
- **Worked example (E1)**: `B6D-9927` = February 2006, Willis bag
  (style 9927).

### CRITICAL AMBIGUITY (flagged disputed)
The E1 example reads `B6D-9927` as Feb **2006**, but under the
single-digit year scheme the same code resolves equally to Feb **1996**.
The post-2003 transition to double-digit years (04, 05…) means a
single-digit "6" in the year position is unambiguous only if other
context (creed wording, lining, hardware) places the bag in one
decade or the other.

This decoder cannot stand alone. Always cross-check year against:
- Creed wording (LEATHERWARE present → pre-1994)
- Lining material and color
- Hardware stamping
- Country of origin (Costa Rica started ~1994; "Made in U.S.A."
  consistent with single-digit 1990s reading)

### Style-number stability
- Per E1: `9927` = Willis. This style number was reused on the
  Willis silhouette over multiple years.
- General principle: a style number identifies a model. A creed
  bearing only a style number does not date the bag.

### Bags without serials
- Pre-1980s bags often had no serial.
- Some pebbled-leather 1990s bags had no serial (E3).
- Small accessories (key fobs, coin purses, mini bags) often shipped
  without serials throughout. Absence of serial alone is not a fake
  tell.

## 4. Hardware

### Brass turnlock — diagnostic
- Solid brass on NYC-era and most pre-2000 bags.
- **Magnet test**: brass is non-magnetic; a magnet that sticks
  indicates plated steel (post-vintage or fake).
- "COACH" stamping appears on later turnlocks; absence does not by
  itself indicate inauthenticity (Cashin-era pieces often unstamped).

### Dogleash clip
- Spring-loaded oval clip; "COACH" stamped on the body in late E3
  onward.
- Casting seams and uneven oval geometry are fake tells.

### Need primary verification
- USPTO patent search for Coach turnlock — searches surfaced USPTO
  landing pages but no specific patent number located in this pass.

## 5. Zipper makers

- **Talon**: pre-mid-1970s common (folklore — needs confirmation
  via dated bag inspections).
- **YKK**: dominant from late 1970s onward.
- **Coats, Ideal, Conmar**: occasional, era-specific.

All `tentative`. The most reliable way to ground these claims is
inspection of dated catalog imagery or bags with independent date
provenance (e.g., bags photographed in dated PurseForum threads or
museum holdings).

---

## 6. Lining

- **Unlined** is correct for many NYC-era leather bags — an unlined
  Court or Willis is **not** a fake tell.
- Lined models use cotton twill, grosgrain, or branded jacquard
  (later eras).
- Heat-cut polyester linings are a fake tell on bags claiming
  pre-1995 manufacture.

---

## 7. Construction

- **Glove-tanned cowhide** is the NYC-era standard.
- Edges are typically **raw or burnished, not painted**. Painted
  glossy edges on a "1980s" bag is a strong fake or reproduction
  signal.
- Stitching: machine lock-stitch on most seams (despite "saddle-
  stitched" folklore). Hand-finishing on specific accents.

## 8. Bag-without-numbers / metal-tag-only era

E1 documents an early diagnostic: **"leather stamp inside but no
creed is actually the same style…placed late 1960s, early 1970s."**
And earlier: **"1960s — original NYC factory — small metal interior
tag only."**

This places three sub-eras within Cashin's tenure:
- E2a (early 1962–mid-60s): metal interior tag only.
- E2b (mid-60s–late 60s): leather stamp inside, no creed patch.
- E2c (late 60s–early 70s): leather stamp continues, transition to
  creed patch.

Confidence: tentative. This taxonomy comes from a single Tier 3
source and needs corroboration.

## 9. The 2014 inflection (out of vintage scope but relevant)

- **Mid-2014: Coach stops putting serial numbers on creeds** (per E8).
  Modern bags use a separate "Made In" sewn-in tag.
- This is post-vintage but matters because community authentication
  guidance written after 2014 sometimes confuses the post-2014 tag
  system with the pre-2014 creed serial system.

## 11. Provisional decision-tree (for Phase 1 publication, with all caveats)

Given a vintage-Coach candidate:

1. **Is there a leather creed?**
   - No, but small metal interior tag → likely E2a (early 1960s).
   - No creed, no tag, just a leather stamp → likely E2b–E2c (late 60s–
     early 70s).
   - Yes, leather creed with serial → continue.

2. **Does the creed read "COACH LEATHERWARE" with small "LEATHERWARE"?**
   - Yes → pre-1994 (E3 era).
   - No (just "COACH") → 1994 or later.

3. **Country of origin in creed text?**
   - "Made in United States" + LEATHERWARE → 1980–1994.
   - "Made in New York City, U.S.A." → 1970s–c. 1980.
   - "Made in Costa Rica" / "Hungary" / "Turkey" / "Dominican Republic"
     → 1994–early 2000s.
   - "Made in Italy" → exotic line or Madison/Gramercy.
   - "Handcrafted in China" → 2000s+, post-vintage.

4. **Serial format?**
   - All digits, e.g. `xxxx-xxx` → pre-1994.
   - Letter-digit-letter-dash-digits, e.g. `B6D-9927` → 1994 onward.
     Year is single-digit (1994–2003) or double-digit (2004+).

5. **Hardware**: brass non-magnetic, "COACH" stamping (later only).

6. **Construction**: raw/burnished edges, no painted edges.

7. **Smell**: glove-tanned has a distinct leather smell; acetone or
   plastic odor is a fake signal.

Each branch must be cross-checked; no single check is sufficient.
