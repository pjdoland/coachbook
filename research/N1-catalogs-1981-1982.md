# Research stream N1 — Deep readings of the 1981–1982 catalogs

Compiled 2026-05-02. Companion to `research/M-catalogs-deep-readings.md`.
Primary-source readings of three Coach catalogs from the **pre-Sara Lee
NYC peak**: 1981 Fall, 1982 Spring/Summer, 1982 Fall — the **40-year
anniversary era** when the company was still owned by Miles and
Lillian Cahn. The archive's primary 1980s data previously started at
1985; this stream pushes the primary-source baseline back four years
into the heart of the Cahn-era catalog program.

Files read:

- `1981-fall.pdf` — 19 PDF pages; printed pages 1–28 with mid-catalog
  belt/billfold pages 18–20 and 22–27 missing from scan
- `1982-spring-summer.pdf` — 16 PDF pages; printed pages 1–20, with
  printed pages 15–18 (belts) missing from scan
- `1982-fall.pdf` — 15 PDF pages; printed pages span 1–22+, scan
  starts on printed page 5 (Duffle Sac); belt pages and apparent
  introductory copy not in scan

---

## Headline findings

1. **The "since 1941" / "small family owned" voice was already in
   place by 1981 Fall.** The back-cover copy on `1981-fall.pdf` page
   28 reads almost verbatim what Coach was still printing in Spring
   1985: "Coach Bags are made by a small, privately held company
   that has been making leather bags, belts and billfolds since
   1941. It is a family-owned business deeply committed to the
   excellence of its products and the quality of its service." This
   voice was therefore **established as canonical Coach copy at least
   four years before the Sara Lee acquisition** and was preserved
   verbatim — not authored — by the post-acquisition team
   [coach-catalogs-1960-1989].

2. **The 40-year anniversary is acknowledged subtly, not loudly.**
   The 1981 Fall catalog never says "40 years" or "1941" on its
   cover; the only anniversary anchor is a factory-page line: "The
   Coach Factory was first organized **nearly forty years ago**,
   starting out as a small leathergoods workshop employing only six
   people." (1981-fall p2). The Spring/Summer 1982 catalog updates
   this to "**over forty years ago**" (1982-spring-summer p2). There
   is **no explicit "40th Anniversary" cover badge, no commemorative
   issue, no anniversary edition** — the milestone is treated with
   the same understatement that defines the brand voice
   [coach-catalogs-1960-1989].

3. **The 10-color palette is locked in by 1981 Fall.** The same ten
   colors appear on the leather-tag chart in all three catalogs (1981
   Fall p3, 1982 Spring/Summer p3, 1982 Fall p4): **Saddle, Navy,
   Mocha, Red, British Tan, Putty, Sage, Burgundy, Black, Tabac**.
   This is a four-year-stable palette: the same ten still anchors
   Spring 1985, where Bone is added and Sage is on its way out
   [coach-catalogs-1960-1989].

4. **No toll-free number, no retail store list, no Paris store.**
   Both 1981–1982 catalogs use a mail-order model: "Coach Leatherware,
   516 West 34th Street, New York City 10001." There is **no 800
   number, no list of Coach-owned stores, no Madison Avenue, no
   Rue Jacob**. This dates Coach's transition to a retail/store
   network as a phenomenon **after 1982** — the 6-store network
   documented in 1985 (including Paris) was built between 1982 and
   1985 [coach-catalogs-1960-1989].

5. **Sub-collection branding does not exist yet.** No "Classic
   Collection," no "Lightweights," no "Spectator," no "Continental,"
   no "Madison." Every bag is just a Coach Bag. The catalog
   architecture is: Bags → Briefcases/Portfolios → Belts → Wallets/
   Bag Fittings. The collection-diversification era of 1989+ is a
   completely different organizational model
   [coach-catalogs-1960-1989].

6. **Cashin's silhouettes still dominate the 1981–1982 catalog**,
   ~7 years after her contract ended. **At least seven Cashin-era
   models** appear by name and number across these catalogs: Dinky
   Bag (9375), Compact Pouch (9620), Saddle Pouch (9585/9590/9595),
   Duffle Sac (9085), Musette Bag (9625), Pony Express Pouch (9670),
   and Stewardess Bag (9525). Cashin herself is **never named**
   [coach-catalogs-1960-1989].

7. **The Watermelon Satchel (9441) is described as "this early
   Coach classic."** This phrasing — used by Coach itself — confirms
   that by 1981 Coach already framed certain models as heritage
   classics. The bag combines a kiss-lock outside coin purse with a
   zippered satchel body — a **kiss-lock closure on a 9XXX-prefix
   bag** that has been understudied in the archive
   [coach-catalogs-1960-1989].

8. **The Skinny Tote (9546) is a kiss-lock model too**, with an
   "outside coin purse [that] is leather-covered, leather-lined" — a
   second 9XXX bag with a sister kiss-lock element. Both are present
   in 1981 Fall and 1982 Spring/Summer, then **the Skinny Tote
   silently disappears in the 1982 Fall scan** (color list contracts
   from "Black, Mocha, Tabac, Burgundy, Navy, Putty" — Saddle dropped
   for Fall 1982) [coach-catalogs-1960-1989].

9. **The Brief Bag (5080), Handle Portfolio (5050), and Portfolio
   (5040) are present in all three catalogs.** This gives a **5XXX
   prefix anchor for legal-size briefcases** four years before the
   1985 catalog confirmed the same numbers. The 5XXX prefix does
   double-duty: 5010/5020/5030 are the wetpacks (toiletry kits in
   British Tan only), 5040–5080 are professional carry
   [coach-catalogs-1960-1989].

10. **Catalog redesign happens between Spring/Summer 1982 and Fall
    1982.** The 1981 Fall and 1982 Spring/Summer catalogs share a
    layout system: cover-with-hero-bag-illustration, page 2 "Coach
    factory in downtown NYC" essay, page 3 ten-color tag grid, then
    bags presented two-up with thumbnail dimension panels. **Fall
    1982 breaks this model**: the cover is a brown linen-textured
    blind-emboss with no bag image, the bags get full-page hero
    photos, and the pacing is slower (one or two bags per page).
    This is the **visual transition into the look that Spring 1985
    will preserve** [coach-catalogs-1960-1989].

11. **The Crescent Bag (9235) appears in glossy color photography
    on the 1982 Spring/Summer cover** — the first time in this
    archive's catalog corpus that a Coach catalog leads with a tipped-in
    color photograph rather than a pencil illustration. The bag
    pictured is in British Tan with a buckle/turnlock combo strap
    [coach-catalogs-1960-1989].

12. **Resolved/narrowed open questions:**
    - **Court Bag (9870):** **NOT in 1981–1982 catalogs.** Confirms
      Court Bag origin remains Fall 1989 [coach-catalogs-1960-1989].
    - **Willis (9927):** **NOT in 1981–1982 catalogs.** Archive's
      "early 1980s" tentative dating is **too early** — Willis must
      be **1983 or later**. The 9927 number sits in a number range
      not yet allocated in 1981–1982 [coach-catalogs-1960-1989].
    - **Soho Bag (4082):** **NOT in 1981–1982 catalogs** — confirmed
      not present, consistent with Fall 1989 / Holiday 1991 narrowed
      window [coach-catalogs-1960-1989].
    - **Lightweight Collection:** **DOES NOT EXIST** in 1981–1982 in
      any form — no name, no styles. The Lightweight 4XXX prefix
      does not appear at all. (Note: the Watermelon Satchel is 9441,
      so the 4XXX prefix is likely just unused space, not "borrowed
      from Lightweights")  [coach-catalogs-1960-1989].
    - **Madison Collection:** **DOES NOT EXIST** in 1981–1982.
      Confirmed [coach-catalogs-1960-1989].

## Catalog 1: Fall 1981

Catalog identifier: `1981-fall.pdf`. Printed catalog pages 1–28 (back
cover copy reads "Prices valid through December 31, 1981. Coach
Leatherware, 516 West 34th Street, New York City 10001.").

### Cover (printed page 1)

Light grey card stock, framed pencil illustration of two
top-handle satchels (likely Slim Satchel Standard 9430 and
Slim Satchel Compact 9425) — both in dark leather with solid
brass dog-leash hangtags. Header text:

> **Coach® Leatherware**
> A CATALOGUE OF CLASSIC COACH® BAGS AND BELTS MADE OUT OF NATURAL
> LEATHER AND SOLID BRASS.

Top-right corner of cover: "1981 - FALL".

No bag-on-the-arm fashion shot, no model, no anniversary badge,
no "40 years" cover line. The brand voice is **understated and
trade-catalog-like**, more like a small leathergoods house than a
fashion brand [coach-catalogs-1960-1989].

### Brand voice — Fall 1981

The brand-voice page is the back cover (printed page 28),
illustrated with what appears to be a Convertible Clutch (9635)
in mid-tone leather. Headline: "**A few of the things you should
know about Coach® Bags**." Six paragraphs of body copy:

> "Coach® Bags are basic, unlined leather bags that are very well
> made out of very good leather. They never go out of style, and,
> under normal conditions, can be worn and enjoyed for many years."

> "Coach® Bags are made by a small, privately held company that has
> been making leather bags, belts and billfolds **since 1941**. It
> is a family-owned business deeply committed to the excellence of
> its products and the quality of its service."

> "Coach® Bags are made in a wide range of classic, instantly
> recognizable models. Many of them were first introduced **almost
> fifteen years ago** and are still in production today. There are
> small, medium and large sizes, and most of them are available in
> ten colors."

> "Coach® Bags are made out of a completely natural, uncorrected,
> full grain leather that has been cured in a special formulation
> of tanning extracts to render it soft and supple to the touch.
> Great care is taken not to cover over the natural markings of
> this leather with paints, sealers, or artificial finishes, and to
> use only clear aniline colors that enhance rather than conceal
> these beautiful markings."

> "Coach® Bags are virtually indestructible. There are no synthetic
> lining materials to tear, pull out or come apart. The buckles are
> cast in solid brass. The seams are sewn with 3-ply high tenacity
> nylon — double stitched where necessary — and can withstand over
> 100 lbs. of stress."

> "Coach® Bags are 'one-of-a-kind' bags, each one identified at the
> factory with its own registration number stamped right into the
> leather. Because it is made out of a completely 'naked' leather,
> each Coach® Bag will develop its own distinctive patina, changing
> gradually in appearance and growing darker in coloration as it is
> worn."

> "Coach® Bags are soft, supple, unconstructed bags designed to be
> worn casually and often — at work and at play. True American
> Classics, they look good with everything and can be worn and
> enjoyed any time, anywhere. Like a favorite pair of blue jeans,
> they seem to improve with wear, taking on additional character
> as they are broken in."

Footer: "© 1981 Coach Leatherware Company, Inc." and "Prices valid
through December 31, 1981. Coach Leatherware, 516 West 34th Street,
New York City 10001" [coach-catalogs-1960-1989].

The phrase "**True American Classics**" is used here. The
**"naked leather"** framing is explicit. The "**blue jeans**"
analogy — a brand-voice signature — is in place by 1981. The
"**100 lbs. of stress**" durability claim is here. **All of the
Spring 1985 brand-voice canonical claims are present in 1981 Fall
in the same words** [coach-catalogs-1960-1989].

The line "first introduced **almost fifteen years ago**" places
the model lineup's establishment around **~1966–1967**, which
aligns with the Cashin design-relationship period.

### Factory copy — Fall 1981

Printed page 2, illustrated with a black-and-white photograph of
hands cutting leather with shears. Headline: "**The Coach factory
in downtown New York City**." Body:

> "The Coach Factory was first organized **nearly forty years
> ago**, starting out as a small leathergoods workshop employing
> only six people. Today it occupies **two floors of a twelve story
> factory building**, just three blocks west of Macy's."

> "Here, in the heart of downtown New York City, **some 125
> exceptionally talented men and women**, working mostly with their
> hands, are turning out the Coach® Bags and Belts for which this
> factory is famous. Some of these people have been working here
> as long as ten, fifteen, and even twenty years. Drawn from the
> many separate ethnic communities that make up the New York
> metropolitan area, they represent many races, creeds, and
> nationalities, rich in heritage and skills."

> "If Coach products have enjoyed a measure of recognition and
> success, it is in no small part because they are made in this
> great City by a unique work force whose skills, energy, and
> quick intelligence make it possible — even in these times — to
> turn out a good product at a fair price."

A boxed call-out promotes a separate publication: "**No 1035 'The
Factory' . . . . . $2.** Over 100 striking photographs taken in the
Coach Factory have been published in a beautiful 76-page library
edition entitled: 'THE FACTORY — Portrait of a Leathergoods Factory
in Downtown New York City.'"

This implies a **separate Coach photographic monograph existed by
1981**, sold through the catalog for $2 (postage). Publication
identifier "No 1035." Worth investigating as a primary source —
not in current archive [coach-catalogs-1960-1989].

## Catalog 2: Spring/Summer 1982

Catalog identifier: `1982-spring-summer.pdf`. Printed catalog pages
1–20 (back cover: "Prices valid through June 30, 1982. Coach
Leatherware, 516 West 34th Street, New York City 10001.").

### Cover (printed page 1)

Cream/buff card stock with **a single tipped-in color illustration
of a pouch bag in British Tan** (likely a Compact Pouch 9620 or
similar small flap-pouch) hanging from a hand at hip — solid brass
hangtag visible. Header: "**Spring/Summer 1982**" (top center).
Below the illustration:

> **Coach® Leatherware**
> A CATALOGUE OF CLASSIC COACH® BAGS AND BELTS MADE OUT OF NATURAL
> LEATHER AND SOLID BRASS.

This is the **first** Coach catalog in the archive's view to
break out of black-and-white pencil illustration on the cover.
The interior bag pages, however, are still pencil illustrations
in the same two-up-with-thumbnail layout as 1981 Fall
[coach-catalogs-1960-1989].

### Brand voice — Spring/Summer 1982

Same back-cover copy as 1981 Fall, **printed verbatim**, with the
same "© 1981 Coach Leatherware Company, Inc." copyright line
(re-used from prior year). Footer changes only the date:
"Prices valid through June 30, 1982." Bag illustrated on back is
again the Convertible Clutch (9635) [coach-catalogs-1960-1989].

### Factory copy — Spring/Summer 1982

Printed page 2. Same headline ("The Coach factory in downtown New
York City") and same shears photo. Body copy is **near-verbatim
from 1981 Fall**, but with two telling updates:

> "The Coach Factory was first organized **over forty years ago**,
> starting out as a small leathergoods workshop employing only
> six people. Today it occupies **three floors** in a factory
> building, **located just a few blocks west of Macy's**."

> "Here, in the heart of downtown New York City, **some 150
> exceptionally talented men and women**, working mostly with
> their hands, are turning out the Coach® Bags and Belts for
> which this factory is famous."

**Two changes from 1981 Fall:**
- "nearly forty years ago" → "**over forty years ago**" (timestamp
  advance)
- "two floors of a twelve story factory building" → "**three
  floors**"
- "**125** exceptionally talented men and women" → "**150**"

This is **primary-source evidence of Coach factory growth between
fall 1981 and spring 1982**: workforce expanded ~20% (125 → 150),
factory footprint expanded by one floor. The "nearly" → "over"
forty wording confirms 1981 ⇒ 1982 timestamp transition over the
1941 anniversary. The same "$2 photographic monograph No 1035 The
Factory" call-out repeats [coach-catalogs-1960-1989].

### Color tag chart — Spring/Summer 1982 (printed page 3)

**Identical** to 1981 Fall: ten tags in same colors and same
arrangement (Saddle/Navy on top row, Mocha/Red, British Tan/Putty,
Sage/Burgundy, Black/Tabac on bottom). Same caption text including
the "set of leather swatches can be had for the asking" line
[coach-catalogs-1960-1989].

## Catalog 3: Fall 1982

Catalog identifier: `1982-fall.pdf`. Printed catalog pages 1–22+
(scan stops on a page 22 wallet spread).

### Cover (printed page 1)

**Brown linen-textured card stock with blind-emboss only — no

This is a **major typographic and design departure** from the 1981

The interior also redesigns:

  enlarged to dominate the page.

  list, and dimensions in three short lines.

  smaller items (wallets, clutches) retain a multi-up grid.

### Brand voice — Fall 1982

The scanned PDF (15 pages) does **not include the back-cover

page** (PDF page 13 = printed page 22):

> "**Here Are A Few Classic Bag Fittings Especially Designed to

> Tanned Cowhide that we use to make our bags and belts. Like the
> bags and belts, they respond very well to wear, taking on a rich
> patina as they are worn."

> "In order to make these small pieces out of our leather, it is
> necessary to split it down to a lighter weight."

> "This is accomplished by running each part through a splitting

This is a notable primary-source paragraph because it:

1. **Names the leather as "Glove Tanned Cowhide"** — a specific
   tannage descriptor used here in 1982 alongside the looser
   "natural leather" language. The "Glove Tanned" label predates
   the post-1985 marketing emphasis on "**Glove Tanned Leather**"
   and confirms it as **Cahn-era nomenclature** rather than Sara
   Lee invention [coach-catalogs-1960-1989].

2. **Confirms the splitting operation** for small leathergoods
   (the leather is "split down to a lighter weight" to make
   wallets and small fittings — a manufacturing detail rarely
   surfaced in catalogs).

3. **"For several years now"** establishes the wallet/fitting
   sub-line as **already ongoing by Fall 1982** — these aren't new
   1982 introductions, they predate the catalog.

4. Implies a **Consumer Service Department** at the Factory
   handling direct mail orders. No 800-number, no retail store
   list — the customer-service apparatus is by mail/letter
   directly to West 34th Street.

## Brand voice across 1981–1982 — verbatim canonical claims

The 1981 Fall back-cover essay is the canonical Cahn-era brand
voice statement. **Every claim is preserved verbatim through 1982
Spring/Summer**, with the Fall 1982 Bag Fittings copy adding
manufacturing detail. Verbatim sentences worth preserving:

**Identity / heritage:**

> "Coach® Bags are made by a small, privately held company that
> has been making leather bags, belts and billfolds since 1941."
> [1981 Fall p28; 1982 Spring/Summer p20]

> "It is a family-owned business deeply committed to the
> excellence of its products and the quality of its service."
> [both; preserved verbatim 1985 Spring]

> "**True American Classics**, they look good with everything and
> can be worn and enjoyed any time, anywhere."
> [1981 Fall p28; 1982 Spring/Summer p20]

> "Like a favorite pair of **blue jeans**, they seem to improve
> with wear, taking on additional character as they are broken
> in." [both]

**Material claims:**

> "Coach® Bags are made out of a completely **natural,
> uncorrected, full grain leather** that has been cured in a
> special formulation of tanning extracts to render it soft and
> supple to the touch." [both]

> "Great care is taken not to cover over the natural markings of
> this leather with paints, sealers, or artificial finishes, and
> to use only **clear aniline colors**." [both]

> "**Glove Tanned Cowhide** that we use to make our bags and
> belts." [1982 Fall p22 — first occurrence of "Glove Tanned" in
> the catalog corpus]

**Construction claims:**

> "The buckles are cast in **solid brass**. The seams are sewn
> with **3-ply high tenacity nylon — double stitched** where
> necessary — and can withstand **over 100 lbs. of stress**."
> [both]

> "**Coach® Bags are virtually indestructible.** There are no
> synthetic lining materials to tear, pull out or come apart."
> [both]

**Patina / aesthetics:**

> "Each Coach® Bag will develop its own distinctive **patina**,
> changing gradually in appearance and growing darker in
> coloration as it is worn." [both]

> "Coach® Bags are made out of a completely **'naked' leather**."
> [both — the "naked leather" framing predates Sara Lee]

**Heritage / classic-status:**

> "Coach® Bags are made in a wide range of **classic, instantly
> recognizable models**. Many of them were first introduced
> **almost fifteen years ago** and are still in production today."
> [1981 Fall p28]

> "This early Coach classic combines best features of a zippered
> satchel and an open tote." [1981 Fall p10 / 1982 Spring/Summer
> p12, describing the Watermelon Satchel — primary source for
> Coach calling its own bag "an early Coach classic" by 1981]

**Anti-counterfeit framing:**

> Not present in the 1981–1982 catalogs the way it is in 1985.
> The 1981–1982 catalogs assert "each one identified at the

> Without The Coach® Tag"** anti-counterfeit page that the 1985

> [coach-catalogs-1960-1989].

**Voice not yet present in 1981–1982:**

- "**No unnecessary ornamentation**" — not in these catalogs.
- "**Baseball glove leather**" — not in these catalogs (the 1982

  hardware is described as "solid brass" repeatedly but the
  alliterative phrase isn't used).

  designer is named.

## Designer attributions

**None.** No designer is named in any of the three catalogs.

The Coach factory copy emphasizes the workforce ("125 / 150
exceptionally talented men and women, working mostly with their
hands"), the heritage (1941, "almost fifteen years ago"), and
the materials/construction. There is **no creative-director
credit, no designer name, no credit line for the catalog
photography or illustration**. The "© 1981 Coach Leatherware
Company, Inc." copyright is the only authorship statement.

This is consistent with the 1985 catalogs, which also do not
credit individual designers. The **named-designer practice** at
Coach appears to begin no earlier than the late 1980s and is not
visible in primary catalog evidence until much later — likely the
1990s [coach-catalogs-1960-1989].

debut per `research/M2` [coach-catalogs-1960-1989].

### Toll-free / 800 number

**Confirmed: no 800 number in 1981 Fall, 1982 Spring/Summer, or
the visible pages of 1982 Fall.** Coach's transition to phone
ordering happened between Fall 1982 and Winter 1985 (when 800-
262-2422 / 800-544-3004 were both documented per `research/M1`).

### Coach Color Palette card / swatch card

**Yes — but free, not for sale.** The 1981 Fall, 1982 S/S, and
1982 Fall color tag pages all offer "**An actual set of leather
swatches can be had for the asking**." This is a **free swatch
service** in 1981–1982. The 1985 Coach Color Palette card sold
through the catalog was therefore a **commercialization** of an
already-existing free service [coach-catalogs-1960-1989].

### "Glove Tanned" naming

**First catalog occurrence in Fall 1982** ("the same Glove Tanned
Cowhide that we use to make our bags and belts" — 1982 Fall p22).
This precedes Sara Lee by three years and confirms "Glove
Tanned" as **Cahn-era nomenclature** [coach-catalogs-1960-1989].

### "Naked leather" framing

**First documented in 1981 Fall** ("each Coach® Bag will develop
its own distinctive patina, changing gradually in appearance and
growing darker in coloration as it is worn... Because it is made
out of a completely 'naked' leather"). Preserved verbatim in 1982
Spring/Summer and 1985 Spring [coach-catalogs-1960-1989].

### "Almost fifteen years ago" anchor

The 1981 Fall back-cover statement says: "Many of them were first
introduced **almost fifteen years ago** and are still in
production today." This puts the model lineup's founding window
around **1966–1967** — the Cashin period. By Spring 1985 this
copy is updated; the precise wording change should be cross-checked
against `research/M1` [coach-catalogs-1960-1989].

## Open verification items

1. **1981 Fall printed pages 18–20 and 22–27 not in scan.** These
   would contain the **belts**, **billfolds**, **order form**, and
   any **store list / retail directory** (if one existed). Recovery
   of these pages would close gaps in the 1981 belt/wallet record.

2. **1982 Spring/Summer printed pages 15–18 not in scan.** These
   are the belt pages for 1982 Spring/Summer. The visible
   billfolds are on page 19. Belt model numbers and prices for
   1982 are unknown from this stream.

3. **1982 Fall scan starts on printed page 5 (Duffle Sac).**
   Pages 1 (cover) and 2–3 are likely the title page, factory
   essay, and either the color tag chart or the workforce update —
   not visible in scan. The workforce/floor count for Fall 1982
   is therefore unknown.

4. **9235 Crescent, 9670 Pony Express, 9441 Watermelon Satchel,
   8920 Courier Pouch all NOT VISIBLE in 1982 Fall scan.** The
   bag pages visible in the Fall 1982 scan account for ~22 models
   but at least 4 prior-catalog models are missing from view. It
   is not yet clear whether they were dropped or are just on
   unscanned pages. **Cross-check against 1983 catalogs.**

5. **The "Coach Color Palette card" question** is partially
   answered (1981–1982 offered free swatches by mail) but the
   commercial Coach Color Palette card sold through the catalog
   may have been introduced between 1983 and 1985. Cross-check
   1983–1984 catalogs.

6. **The "Glove Tanned" terminology** — first catalog occurrence
   in Fall 1982 Bag Fittings copy. Earlier usage (1981 Fall and
   prior) only says "natural leather." Confirm whether any
   pre-1982 advertising or PR uses the phrase
   [coach-catalogs-1960-1989].

7. **Saddle's contraction in Fall 1982** is striking and worth
   further investigation. Saddle is "All ten" on the tag chart
   and on most bag color lists in 1981 Fall and 1982
   Spring/Summer, but Fall 1982 sees Saddle dropped from at least
   12 model color lists. This may reflect a **mid-1982 supply
   issue**, a **decision to retire Saddle** (later reversed?), or
   a **catalog-editing simplification**. Cross-check 1983 Spring
   to determine which.

8. **Sage's continued presence in Fall 1982 Bag Fittings** but
   absence from most Fall 1982 bag color lists may indicate Sage
   was **already in soft retirement on bags but kept on small
   leathergoods**. Cross-check 1983 catalogs.

9. **Saddle Pouch Small (9595) appears retired in Fall 1982.**
   This is a specific model retirement to flag. Cross-check Fall
   1982 / Spring 1983 to confirm permanent retirement vs. seasonal
   omission.

10. **The "No 1035 The Factory" book** ($2 in 1981 Fall and 1982
    S/S, 76 pages, 100+ photos) is an **important secondary
    primary source** if it can be tracked down. Worth searching
    library catalogs (Library of Congress, NYPL, FIT, Parsons
    archives) for "The Factory: Portrait of a Leathergoods
    Factory in Downtown New York City" published by Coach
    Leatherware Co., circa 1981 [coach-catalogs-1960-1989].

11. **No Cahn-family signature or named-letter from Lillian or
    Miles Cahn** appears in any of the three catalogs. This
    contrasts with later Lewis Frankfort–era catalogs that
    sometimes carry signed letters. Cross-check earlier
    1970s mini-catalogs to see if a Cahn signature ever appeared
    in print.

12. **The 1982 Spring/Summer cover bag identity.** The
    illustrated bag on the cover is described above as "likely a
    Compact Pouch (9620)" but the angle and detail of the
    illustration could match other small flap-pouch styles.
    Worth a closer look.
