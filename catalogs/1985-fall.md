The 1985 Fall catalog window is the first Sara Lee–era catalog. Sara Lee
Corporation acquired Coach in July 1985, and Lew Frankfort was appointed
president of Coach in the same month per the fiscal-2003 10-K primary
source [coach-10k-fiscal-2003]. Fall 1985 is therefore the earliest
catalog produced under the new ownership and the first opportunity to
document any visible brand-direction shift away from the Cahn-era house
voice.

## What to look for when a scan is retrieved

- Cover and inside-cover models — likely the continuing classic-NYC
  canon (Court, Willis, Madison, Station, Mailbox, Penny, Bucket); a
  Sara Lee–era cover redesign would be a notable signal in itself.
- Creed wording — the most important single data point in this catalog.
  Compare the creed text shown on bags here against pre-July-1985
  catalogs to determine whether Sara Lee changed the
  `COACH LEATHERWARE / EST. 1941 / NEW YORK CITY` wording or the
  serial-number format. Pre-Sara-Lee creed text is the baseline.
- House voice in catalog copy — does the Fall 1985 catalog read like
  the Cahn-era catalogs, or does it show early signs of the broader
  marketing reach Sara Lee would build out (department-store boutique
  expansion began early 1986 per FundingUniverse)
  [fundinguniverse-coach].
- Madison Avenue flagship references — the flagship continued under
  Sara Lee and would reopen in 1987 [fundinguniverse-coach]; any 1985
  flagship copy is the pre-renovation baseline.
- "Made in U.S.A." stamping on every shown bag — this is the
  pre-offshoring baseline; offshoring did not begin meaningfully
  until the 1990s.
- Style-number formats — the pre-1994 `xxxx-xxx` family is expected
  throughout.

## Models likely documented

- Classic-NYC canon: Court, Willis, Stewardess, Madison variants,
  Station, Mailbox, Penny, Bucket — likely the bulk of pages, since
  the Sara Lee–era collection-launch pattern (Sheridan, Camden,
  Manhattan, Soho, Sonoma) does not begin until the early 1990s
  [fundinguniverse-coach].
- Cashin-vocabulary carryovers (turnlock-closure bags) continuing in
  the line, though Cashin's own contract ended in 1974 and any bag
  introduced post-1974 is not a Cashin design regardless of hardware
  vocabulary.
