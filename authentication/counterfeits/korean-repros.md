# Korean repros

## Lead

Korean reproductions of vintage Coach are a documented counterfeit
family from the 1990s, distinguished by a small set of consistent
diagnostic tells [vintagecoachbags-authenticity, purseforum-authenticate-coach].
They are notably less sophisticated than later Chinese fakes but
still appear regularly in resale. All claims on this page are
`tentative`: the family is widely cited across Tier 3 community
authentication sources but has no Tier 1 or Tier 2 corroboration —
no SEC filing, brand statement, or trade-press article documents the
1990s Korean-repro stream.

## Era of production

Primarily 1990s; some examples persist in resale today. Korean repros
generally target the most-sought NYC-era models — Willis, Court, and
Station Bag — because those silhouettes carried the highest resale
demand during the period the repros were produced
[purseforum-authenticate-coach]. Korea is **not** in the fiscal-2003
Coach manufacturing-country list (China, Costa Rica, Mexico, India,
Italy, Spain, Hungary, Turkey) [coach-10k-fiscal-2003], so any creed
or origin tag indicating Korean manufacture is a fake tell on its
face — Coach did not produce in Korea.

## Diagnostic tells

A Korean repro typically carries multiple of these tells. Each one
echoes a documented authentic-bag construction anchor from
`research/E-authentication.md` or the cross-family signal list in
`authentication/counterfeits/index.md`; no single tell is sufficient,
but the combination is diagnostic.

- **Edge paint present on a "vintage" creed era** — the strongest
  single tell. Authentic NYC-era Coach edges are raw or burnished,
  not glossy painted [valentinasvault-creed, bagforum-creed-history].
  A glossy painted edge on a bag whose creed claims pre-1995 manufacture
  is a strong fake or reproduction signal.
- **Magnetic hardware** — the turnlock or dogleash sticks to a magnet.
  Authentic brass turnlocks of the LEATHERWARE era are non-magnetic;
  a magnet that sticks indicates plated steel
  [vintagecoachbags-authenticity, purseforum-authenticate-coach].
  Korean repros commonly used plated steel for cost.
- **Wrong creed kerning** — letterspacing on "COACH LEATHERWARE" is
  visibly different from authentic; "1941" numerals are commonly
  fatter or sans-serif on the repros [bagforum-creed-history,
  valentinasvault-creed].
- **Heat-cut polyester lining** on a model that should be unlined or
  have cotton twill. Korean repros often add a synthetic lining for
  "completeness" — but unlined is correct for many NYC-era leather
  bags, and an unlined Court or Willis is **not** itself a fake tell
  [vintagecoachie, vintagecoachbags-authenticity].
- **Lighter weight** — the bag feels noticeably lighter than authentic
  of the same dimensions, reflecting thinner leather and plated-steel
  hardware [purseforum-authenticate-coach].

## What they did right

Listed so an authentic bag is not dismissed by accident on a feature
that the repros also got right:

- Silhouette is generally accurate to the targeted model.
- Color names sometimes match Coach's palette.
- Style number on the creed often matches the model — for example,
  `9927` for Willis fakes [coachbagrehab-dating].

A correct silhouette, plausible color name, and matching style number
are therefore not authentication signals on their own; they are the
baseline the repros cleared.

## Replacement-component caveat

A magnetic dogleash or replaced lining on an otherwise-authentic bag
could indicate Coach Repair restoration rather than a Korean repro.
Coach Repair (consolidated to Jacksonville, Florida in July 1999
[coach-10k-fiscal-2003]) sometimes replaced creeds, turnlocks,
dogleash clips, and linings during legitimate restoration. Cross-check
creed integrity, edge treatment, and weight before classifying as
fake — a single era-mismatched component is a question, not a
verdict (see `authentication/counterfeits/index.md` "Replacement-
component caveat" and the high-folklore-zone rule in `STYLE.md` §4.5).

## Detection at resale

What to ask the seller for, before bidding or buying:

- Clear photos of the creed with a ruler in frame for scale.
- Turnlock front and back.
- Edge close-ups along the gusset and strap.
- Lining (full and any pocket details).
- Country-of-origin tag, whether stamped into the creed or sewn
  into a seam.

If any of these are refused or unclear, walk away. The submission
photo set in `authentication/counterfeits/index.md` "Submission
workflow" is the canonical request list for any suspected fake.
