# Counterfeit catalog

## Lead

Counterfeit vintage Coach falls into identifiable "families" — Korean
repros from the 1990s, Turkish repros, modern Chinese fakes, and
Frankenbags assembled from real parts plus replacement creeds. Each
family carries signature tells. This page is the framework index;
individual family pages live under `/authentication/counterfeits/<family-slug>.md`.

## Counterfeit families

Each family below is a stub. Diagnostic detail, photo references, and
year-first-observed will land on the dedicated family pages. Every
specific-tell claim here echoes a documented signal from
`research/E-authentication.md` or the cross-reference pages
(`creed-formats.md`, `serial-numbers.md`, `country-of-origin.md`); no
new tells are introduced on this index.

### Korean repros (1990s)

A 1990s reproduction wave commonly cited in Tier 3 community-authentication
guidance. Typical tells echo the construction-anchor list in
`research/E-authentication.md` §7 and the creed-wording anchors in
`creed-formats.md`: **painted glossy edges** on a bag claiming pre-1995
manufacture, **magnetic hardware** (real Coach turnlocks of the era are
solid brass and non-magnetic), and **wrong creed kerning** or stroke
weight on the "COACH LEATHERWARE" wordmark. Dedicated `korean-repros.md`
page forthcoming.

### Turkish repros

Turkey was a legitimate Coach manufacturing country from the
mid-1990s — confirmed in the fiscal-2003 sourcing list
[coach-10k-fiscal-2003] and cited from approximately 1996 onward in
Tier 3 sources [coachbagrehab-dating]. A separate Turkish *reproduction*
stream circulates in community-authentication discussion, complicating
the read on any "Made in Turkey" creed: a legitimate Turkish-made
Coach and a Turkish counterfeit can both surface at resale. Dedicated
`turkish-repros.md` page forthcoming with the diagnostic boundary
between the two.

### Modern Chinese fakes (2000s+)

The most common counterfeit family today, with multiple sub-types. The
recurring signal across sub-types is **era-impossible country-of-origin
combinations** — most diagnostically, "Made in China" on a bag whose
creed still carries the small-caps "LEATHERWARE" wordmark. China-made
Coach is post-vintage and post-LEATHERWARE per
`country-of-origin.md`; the combination is era-impossible.
Dedicated `modern-chinese-fakes.md` page forthcoming with the sub-type
breakdown.

### Frankenbags

Bags assembled from a mixture of authentic Coach components and
replacement parts — most often a replacement creed, sometimes a
swapped turnlock, dogleash clip, or hangtag. Particularly hard to
detect because some components really are authentic; the bag's
leather, hardware, and stitching may pass every individual check
while the creed carries era-impossible wording or a serial format
that contradicts the rest of the bag. Dedicated `frankenbags.md`
page forthcoming.

## Era-inconsistency tells

These cross-family signals come from `country-of-origin.md` and
`research/E-authentication.md`. Any one of them flags a bag as a
counterfeit, a Frankenbag, or a misrepresented item — pending the
replacement-component caveat below.

- **"Made in China" with LEATHERWARE creed.** China production
  postdates the LEATHERWARE wordmark window. Strong fake tell
  [coach-10k-fiscal-2003, coachbagrehab-dating].
- **Offshore origin (Costa Rica / Hungary / Turkey / Dominican Republic
  / Mexico) on a creed that reads "Made in New York City, U.S.A."**
  NYC-only wording cannot coexist with offshore origin
  [coachbagrehab-dating, vintagecoachie].
- **"Handcrafted in China" with the small-caps LEATHERWARE wordmark
  present.** Era-impossible [coach-10k-fiscal-2003, coachbagrehab-dating].
- **Offshore origin marking on a bag with a stamped-leather-only
  interior and no creed at all** (the late-1960s–early-1970s E2b–E2c
  style). Almost certainly a replacement creed or a fake
  [coachbagrehab-dating].
- **Letter-number serial (e.g., `B6D-9927`) paired with
  "Made in New York City, U.S.A." creed wording.** The letter-number
  format begins in 1994; NYC wording ends approximately 1980. The
  combination is impossible [coachbagrehab-dating, vintagecoachie].
- **Painted glossy edges on a bag with a pre-1995 creed.** Authentic
  vintage edges are raw or burnished, not painted
  [valentinasvault-creed, bagforum-creed-history].
- **Magnetic turnlock on a bag with a LEATHERWARE creed.** Authentic
  brass turnlocks of the LEATHERWARE era are non-magnetic
  [vintagecoachbags-authenticity, purseforum-authenticate-coach].
- **Zipper maker postdating the creed era** — see
  `research/E-authentication.md` §5 for the maker-by-era timeline.

## Replacement-component caveat

A bag with a "wrong era" component is **not automatically a fake.**
Coach Repair (consolidated to **Jacksonville, Florida in July 1999**
per the fiscal-2003 10-K, [coach-10k-fiscal-2003]) sometimes replaced
creeds, turnlocks, and dogleash clips during legitimate restoration.
A genuinely authentic bag can carry a Coach-Repair-installed
replacement that reads as era-inconsistent.

This is one reason Frankenbag detection is hard: the line between
"authentic bag with Coach-installed replacement" and "Frankenbag
assembled at resale from real parts" is not always visible from the
bag alone. Provenance, restoration paperwork, and the consistency of
the *non-replaced* components carry the weight in close calls. The
high-folklore-zone rule from `STYLE.md` §4.5 — *"original" hardware
vs replacement* — applies here: a single era-mismatched component is
a question, not a verdict.

## Authenticator's quick triage

In order, on a candidate bag:

1. **Magnet to the turnlock.** Brass is non-magnetic; a stick is a
   strong fake or post-2000 plated-steel signal.
2. **Read the creed wording.** Small-caps "LEATHERWARE" beneath
   "COACH" → pre-1994. Just "COACH" → 1994+. Country-of-origin line
   must be consistent with the wording era (see `creed-formats.md`).
3. **Check serial format against creed era.** Digit-only `xxxx-xxx`
   or `xxx-xxxx` → pre-1994. Letter-digit-letter-dash-digits
   (`B6D-9927`) → 1994+. A mismatch between serial format and creed
   wording is a Frankenbag or fake signal (see `serial-numbers.md`).
4. **Inspect edge treatment.** Raw or burnished edges on pre-1995
   bags; glossy painted edges are a red flag.
5. **Check zipper maker against era.** Talon dominant pre-mid-1970s,
   YKK dominant late 1970s onward (per `research/E-authentication.md`
   §5). A zipper maker postdating the creed era is suspect.

No single check is sufficient. The decision tree in `creed-formats.md`
is the long-form companion to this triage list.

## Submission workflow

Readers submitting suspected fakes should provide the **canonical
photo set**: full bag (front, back, both sides); creed close-up with
a ruler in frame for scale; turnlock front and back; zipper pull and
zipper-tape stamp; lining (full and any pocket details);
country-of-origin tag (whether stamped into the creed or sewn into
a seam); hangtag (both sides); base and feet. Submissions land in
`/authentication/submissions/` as drafts. Once verified or
classified, notable examples are promoted to reference status on the
relevant family page.
