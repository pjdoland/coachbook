# Turkish repros

## Lead

"Turkish repros" are counterfeit Coach bags manufactured in Turkey,
not to be confused with the legitimate Turkish-made Coach production
that ran from approximately 1996 onward [coachbagrehab-dating,
coach-10k-fiscal-2003]. The diagnostic profile centers on subtle
wrong-grain leather, generic edge treatment, and a creed stamp whose
impression depth is shallower than authentic. All claims on this page
are `tentative`: the family is described across Tier 3 community
authentication sources but has no Tier 1 or Tier 2 corroboration of
the *counterfeit* stream specifically — the Tier 1 source confirms
only the legitimate manufacturing channel.

## Era of production

Turkish counterfeits circulate roughly from the late 1990s onward,
overlapping the legitimate Turkish-made Coach window
[purseforum-authenticate-coach]. Authentic Turkish Coach production
is documented from approximately 1996 in Tier 3 sources
[coachbagrehab-dating] and confirmed in the fiscal-2003 sourcing list
[coach-10k-fiscal-2003], which names Turkey alongside China, Costa
Rica, Mexico, India, Italy, Spain, and Hungary. The counterfeits
typically target the same offshore-era models a legitimate "Made in
Turkey" Coach would carry — handbags from Coach's mid-to-late 1990s
catalog — which is precisely what makes them harder to triage at
resale than a Korean repro of an NYC-era bag.

## Diagnostic tells

A Turkish repro typically carries multiple of these tells. Each one
echoes a documented authentic-bag construction anchor from
`research/E-authentication.md` or the cross-family signal list in
`authentication/counterfeits/index.md`; no single tell is sufficient,
but the combination is diagnostic.

- **Wrong-grain leather** — the pebbling or smooth-finish grain
  pattern reads as subtly off compared to authentic glove-tanned
  cowhide. The grain is often more uniform (mechanically embossed)
  than the natural variation on authentic hides
  [vintagecoachbags-authenticity, valentinasvault-creed]. Authentic
  glove-tanned leather is the NYC-era and offshore-era standard per
  `research/E-authentication.md` §7.
- **Generic edge treatment** — edges are neither cleanly burnished
  nor properly painted to era-appropriate Coach standards. The
  finish is often a thin, uneven coating that wears unevenly within
  the first year of use [valentinasvault-creed,
  bagforum-creed-history]. Authentic offshore-era Coach edges remain
  consistent with the construction anchor in
  `research/E-authentication.md` §7.
- **Shallow creed-stamp depth** — the creed impression is visibly
  less deep than authentic, with letters that sit nearly flush with
  the leather surface rather than pressed in
  [valentinasvault-creed, bagforum-creed-history]. Authentic creed
  patches carry a firm, even impression depth across the wordmark.
- **Wrong creed kerning** — letterspacing on "COACH" or
  "COACH LEATHERWARE" deviates from authentic; "Made in Turkey"
  text is sometimes set in a typeface that does not match the rest
  of the creed [bagforum-creed-history, valentinasvault-creed].
- **Magnetic hardware** — the turnlock or dogleash sticks to a
  magnet. Authentic brass turnlocks of the LEATHERWARE era are
  non-magnetic; a magnet that sticks indicates plated steel
  [vintagecoachbags-authenticity, purseforum-authenticate-coach].
- **Era-impossible serial-creed combinations** — a "Made in Turkey"
  creed paired with a digit-only pre-1994 serial (e.g., `xxxx-xxx`)
  is era-impossible: Turkish production began approximately 1996,
  well after the 1994 transition to letter-number serials
  [coachbagrehab-dating, coach-10k-fiscal-2003]. Equally, a
  "Made in Turkey" creed paired with the small-caps "LEATHERWARE"
  wordmark is suspect; LEATHERWARE wording fades through the 1994
  transition and the country line generally aligns with the
  post-LEATHERWARE creed.
- **Lighter weight and thinner leather** — the bag feels noticeably
  lighter than an authentic Turkish-made Coach of the same model
  [purseforum-authenticate-coach].

## What they did right

Listed so an authentic Turkish-made Coach is not dismissed by accident
on a feature that the repros also got right:

- "Made in Turkey" stamping is itself legitimate — Turkey appears
  in the fiscal-2003 manufacturing-country list
  [coach-10k-fiscal-2003]. Origin marking alone does not flag a fake.
- Silhouette is generally accurate to the targeted offshore-era
  model.
- Style number on the creed often matches the model
  [coachbagrehab-dating].
- Color names sometimes match Coach's offshore-era palette.

A "Made in Turkey" creed, correct silhouette, plausible color name,
and matching style number are therefore not authentication signals on
their own; they are the baseline both authentic Turkish Coach and the
repros clear.

## Turkey-as-authentic vs Turkey-as-fake

This distinction matters for resale-shopper triage and is the central
reading challenge for any "Made in Turkey" Coach:

- **Both exist.** Coach contracted with Turkish manufacturers from
  approximately 1996 through the early 2000s
  [coachbagrehab-dating, coach-10k-fiscal-2003]. Turkish counterfeits
  also circulated in the same period [purseforum-authenticate-coach].
  The country marking alone does not determine authenticity in
  either direction.
- **The era-consistency cross-check determines fake**, not the
  country marking. A "Made in Turkey" creed must be paired with a
  letter-number serial format (post-1994), a creed without the
  small-caps "LEATHERWARE" wordmark (post-LEATHERWARE), and
  hardware, edge, and grain consistent with mid-to-late 1990s
  authentic Coach. Any one of these in disagreement with the country
  line points to a counterfeit or a Frankenbag — see the
  era-inconsistency tells in `authentication/counterfeits/index.md`
  and the decision tree in `creed-formats.md`.
- **The folklore trap.** A community claim that "Made in Turkey
  means fake" is wrong; it conflates the existence of the
  counterfeit stream with the country marking itself. Per the
  high-folklore-zone rule in `STYLE.md` §4.3, blanket country claims
  fail; per-bag evidence only.

## Replacement-component caveat

A magnetic dogleash, replaced lining, or replacement creed on an
otherwise-authentic bag could indicate Coach Repair restoration
rather than a Turkish repro. Coach Repair (consolidated to
Jacksonville, Florida in July 1999 [coach-10k-fiscal-2003])
sometimes replaced creeds, turnlocks, dogleash clips, and linings
during legitimate restoration. A genuinely authentic Turkish-made
Coach can carry a Coach-Repair-installed replacement that itself
reads as era-inconsistent. Cross-check creed-stamp depth, leather
grain, and edge treatment on the *non-replaced* components before
classifying as fake — a single era-mismatched component is a
question, not a verdict (see
`authentication/counterfeits/index.md` "Replacement-component
caveat" and the high-folklore-zone rule in `STYLE.md` §4.5).

## Detection at resale

What to ask the seller for, before bidding or buying:

- Clear photos of the creed with a ruler in frame for scale, with
  raking light to show stamp impression depth.
- Turnlock front and back.
- Edge close-ups along the gusset and strap.
- Leather close-ups showing grain pattern and natural variation.
- Country-of-origin tag, whether stamped into the creed or sewn
  into a seam.
- Serial format — the letter-number combination must be readable
  and consistent with a post-1996 Turkish-production date.

If any of these are refused or unclear, walk away. The submission
photo set in `authentication/counterfeits/index.md` "Submission
workflow" is the canonical request list for any suspected fake.
