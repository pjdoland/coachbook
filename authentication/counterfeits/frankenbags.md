# Frankenbags

## Lead

Frankenbags are the hardest counterfeit family to detect because they
incorporate authentic Coach components — most often a real bag body
fitted with a replacement creed, but also real hardware mounted on a
fake body, an authentic body recolored to a more desirable shade, or a
hybrid stitched together from real-but-damaged parts. The leather,
hardware, or stitching may pass individual checks while the assembled
bag is not what the seller represents. Every claim on this page is
`tentative`: Frankenbag construction patterns are widely cited across
Tier 3 community-authentication discussion but have no Tier 1 or Tier
2 corroboration.

## Common Frankenbag patterns

- **Replacement creed on a real body** — a counterfeiter peels off a
  worn or non-style-matching creed and stitches in a replacement,
  often citing a more desirable model or earlier era, onto an
  otherwise authentic bag body [bagforum-creed-history,
  valentinasvault-creed].
- **Real hardware on a fake body** — turnlock and dogleash salvaged
  from a damaged authentic bag and mounted on a leather body of
  unknown origin [vintagecoachbags-authenticity,
  purseforum-authenticate-coach]. The brass passes the magnet test
  because it really is brass; the leather and construction do not
  pass the rest of the checks.
- **Recolored body** — an authentic body in an undesirable color is
  stripped and re-dyed to a more sought-after color (e.g., Black to
  Hunter Green) [purseforum-authenticate-coach].
- **Hybrid construction** — front of one bag merged with back of
  another to "fix" a damaged authentic, presented as a single intact
  bag [purseforum-authenticate-coach].

## Diagnostic tells

Frankenbags are harder than other families because individual checks
from `research/E-authentication.md` and the cross-family signal list in
`authentication/counterfeits/index.md` may pass on the authentic
component while failing on the replacement. Look for inconsistencies
*between* parts of the bag, not just at any one part.

- **Stitch pitch mismatch between creed and body** — the creed
  stitches do not align with the body's stitch holes, indicating the
  creed was added or replaced after original manufacture
  [valentinasvault-creed, bagforum-creed-history].
- **Glue residue around creed edges** — visible under UV inspection
  (see `authentication/toolkit.md`); authentic creeds are stitched,
  not glued [valentinasvault-creed].
- **Cut edge of leather does not match the surface color** — on a
  recolored body, the original drum-dyed-through color breaks through
  at edges, scuffs, and seam lines [purseforum-authenticate-coach].
- **Hardware mounting holes do not match Coach's standard rivet
  positions** for the model — extra holes, filled holes, or holes
  offset from the documented model geometry indicate hardware was
  moved [vintagecoachbags-authenticity, purseforum-authenticate-coach].
- **Stitch thread color or weight differs** between the creed area
  and the rest of the body [bagforum-creed-history].
- **Aging mismatch** — the creed shows different patina, edge wear,
  or staining than the body it sits on [valentinasvault-creed,
  purseforum-authenticate-coach].

## The hard problem

A Frankenbag with a 70%-authentic body still has authentic Coach
material in it. The collector question is not "is any part of this
bag real?" — it usually is — but whether the assembled bag is what
the seller represents. Usually it isn't, and the seller may not even
know: a Frankenbag can move through several owners between assembly
and the listing under inspection [purseforum-authenticate-coach].

## Coach Repair caveat

Coach Repair is critical here. Coach consolidated worldwide
warehousing, distribution, and repair into Jacksonville, Florida in
**July 1999** per the fiscal-2003 10-K [coach-10k-fiscal-2003], and
the program **legitimately** replaced creeds, hardware, and hangtags
on bags submitted by their original owners. A bag with a
Coach-Repair-installed replacement creed is not a Frankenbag — it is
an authentically-restored Coach. The high-folklore-zone rule from
`STYLE.md` §4.5 ("'original' hardware vs replacement") applies
directly: a single replaced component is a question, not a verdict.

Distinguishing a legitimate Coach Repair restoration from a
Frankenbag requires:

- **Provenance documentation** — original owner story, repair
  receipts, dated correspondence with Coach Repair.
- **Internal consistency of replaced parts** — Coach Repair drew
  from era-appropriate inventory, so a Coach-Repair replacement creed
  on a 1985 body is itself an era-appropriate creed. Frankenbags do
  not consistently match era: a Frankenbag often pairs a
  more-desirable era's creed wording with a body whose construction,
  edges, or hardware belong to a different era
  [bagforum-creed-history, coachbagrehab-dating].
- **Era consistency between the replaced part and the body** — a
  Coach-Repair-installed creed should be era-consistent with the body
  (or at minimum era-plausible given the repair date); a Frankenbag
  creed is typically chosen for desirability, not era fit.

## Detection at resale

Ask the seller explicitly: **"Has this bag been restored?"**

- If yes, ask for repair documentation (receipts, owner history).
  An authentically-restored Coach with paperwork is a different
  market category from a Frankenbag, and a different price.
- If no, but creed or hardware shows replacement signs (stitch pitch
  mismatch, glue residue, aging mismatch, off-position rivet holes),
  increase suspicion materially. Either the seller does not know the
  bag's history or is misrepresenting it.

The submission photo set in
`authentication/counterfeits/index.md` "Submission workflow" is the
canonical request list for any suspected Frankenbag, with two
additions worth requesting: edge close-ups along multiple seams
(recolor detection) and oblique-angle creed photos under raking light
(stitch-hole and glue-residue detection).
