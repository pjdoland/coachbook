## Lead

Coach bags from before the numbered-creed era (mid-1970s onward) lack the
most common authentication surfaces — no creed patch, no serial number, and
no "Made in" wording on the leather. Authentication for these bags relies on
construction details, hardware vocabulary, and provenance rather than on a
single textual anchor. This page documents the three sub-eras within the
pre-creed period (E2a, E2b, E2c), all of which fall inside Bonnie Cashin's
1962–1974 contract with Coach.

The full sub-era taxonomy below is `tentative` — it traces to a single
Tier 3 source (`coachbagrehab-dating`, propagated in `research/E-authentication.md`
§8) and has not yet been corroborated against a primary catalog or the
Cashin papers.

## E2a — early-to-mid 1960s: small interior metal tag only

- Tag is brass, stamped "COACH" or similar. **No serial number** appears
  on the tag [coachbagrehab-dating].
- **No leather creed patch.** The leather creed had not yet been
  introduced [coachbagrehab-dating, vintagecoachie].
- **No country-of-origin marking on the leather.** Manufacture was in
  New York City throughout this period, but the bag itself does not say
  so.
- This is the era of Cashin's earliest Coach work — roughly 1962–1965.
  The Cashin Carry tote (Fall 1962 inaugural collection) falls here
  [wwd-coach-early-years].
- **The brass turnlock did NOT exist yet.** The turnlock was introduced
  at Coach in **1964**, two years after Cashin's hire [vintagefashionguild-cashin].
  Closures on E2a bags include kiss-lock frames, open-top totes,
  occasional magnetic snaps, and wraparound tabs.

Confidence: tentative. The metal-tag-only diagnostic comes from a single
Tier 3 source.

## E2b — mid-to-late 1960s: leather stamp inside, no creed patch

- "COACH" stamp pressed directly into the leather lining (where present)
  or into an interior pocket [coachbagrehab-dating].
- Still **no serial number** and still **no "Made in" wording**
  [coachbagrehab-dating].
- Brass turnlock from **1964** onward becomes standard hardware on
  applicable silhouettes [vintagefashionguild-cashin].
- **Striped fabric lining** is a Cashin signature in this period and is
  often cited in community guides as a Cashin marker [vintagecoachie].
- This is peak Cashin-vocabulary era at Coach: side pockets, kiss-lock
  coin-purse interior surprises, and brighter colors all date to this
  window [wwd-coach-early-years].

Confidence: tentative.

## E2c — late 1960s to early 1970s: leather stamp continues, transition to creed patch

- Some examples carry the leather stamp from E2b; some carry an early
  creed patch with no serial number [coachbagrehab-dating].
- The sub-era boundary is fluid; the specific year of transition is **not
  pinned down** in any free source located.
- The Soft Sac was introduced in 1970 per `vintagecoachie`, signalling a
  shift toward the standard glove-tanned aesthetic that defined the
  numbered-creed era [vintagecoachie].
- This sub-era ends roughly with the introduction of the numbered creed
  patch in the mid-1970s. Cashin's contract ended in 1974, so the closing
  edge of E2c overlaps with her departure [wwd-coach-early-years].

Confidence: tentative.

## Authentication discipline for pre-creed bags

Without a creed and serial to read, authentication leans heavily on
construction, hardware, and provenance. Each of the following is
necessary but not sufficient on its own.

- **Provenance is critical.** Without a creed or serial, dated
  photographs, original receipts, or a credible chain of custody
  substantially raise authentication confidence. Their absence does not
  prove a fake but raises the verification bar.
- **Construction-level signals.** Edge treatment (raw or burnished, never
  painted on a true pre-creed bag), drum-dyed-through cut edge,
  glove-tanned-leather smell, and the specific stitching pattern for the
  model — machine lock-stitch on most seams, hand-finishing on accents
  (despite "saddle-stitched" folklore) [coachbagrehab-dating].
- **Hardware vocabulary.** Turnlock construction (solid brass,
  non-magnetic — magnet test), dogleash clip details, kiss-lock frame
  quality, brass weight. "COACH" stamping on the turnlock is a *later*
  addition; absence of the stamp is **not** a fake tell on a Cashin-era
  piece [coachbagrehab-dating, vintagefashionguild-cashin].
- **Cashin-vocabulary signals.** Striped fabric lining, side pockets,
  brighter colors, and a kiss-lock coin-purse interior surprise all
  suggest the E2b period. They do **not** prove Cashin authorship: per
  `vintagefashionguild-cashin`, the same design vocabulary (turnlock
  included) appeared on Cashin's contemporaneous collections for Philip
  Sills, Meyers, Crescendoe-Superb, HBA, and D. Klein. A bag with all
  the Cashin signals can authentically be a Coach bag, a Sills bag, or
  one of several others.

## Common authentication mistakes for pre-creed bags

- **Concluding a no-creed bag is fake because it has no creed.** The
  creed patch did not exist for the period these bags were made.
- **Crediting any pre-1974 bag as a "Cashin" without primary evidence.**
  Cashin's contract covered 1962–1974, but multiple in-house and
  contracted designers also worked at Coach during the period. A
  Cashin-era bag is not necessarily a Cashin-designed bag. Primary
  documentation (the `ucla-cashin-papers` Coach contract and design
  illustrations, or a dated 1962–1974 Coach catalog) is required to
  attribute a specific bag to her.
- **Reading metal interior tag absence as fake on a true E2a bag.**
  Metal tags can detach over six decades of use; a missing tag is not
  by itself a fake tell.

## What would upgrade these claims to confirmed

The pre-creed taxonomy currently rests on a single Tier 3 source. The
following would corroborate or correct it:

- A **pre-1974 Coach catalog page** showing a model with its interior
  construction visible — the most direct way to anchor leather-stamp
  vs. metal-tag chronology.
- The **UCLA Cashin papers** (`ucla-cashin-papers`) — design illustrations,
  the Coach contract, and contemporaneous press materials.
- **Dated press photos** of carriers from the 1962–1974 period.
- **Stephanie Lake's Cashin biography** (`lake-cashin-2016`) — chapters
  on Cashin's Coach work.

## Open verification items

1. The E2a / E2b / E2c sub-era boundaries trace to a single Tier 3
   source (`coachbagrehab-dating`). No primary or Tier 2 corroboration
   has been located.
2. The exact transition year between E2c (leather stamp, no creed) and
   the numbered-creed era is not documented in any free source examined
   so far. "Mid-1970s" is the best available approximation.
3. Whether E2a metal tags carried any text beyond "COACH" (e.g., a
   factory mark or designer mark) is not documented.
4. The chronology of the early-creed-patch-without-serial sub-format
   inside E2c — how long it lasted, on which models — is not documented.
5. Whether striped fabric lining is exclusively a Cashin-period feature
   at Coach, or whether it persisted beyond 1974, has not been
   independently verified.
