## Lead

Vintage Coach hardware is solid brass; the magnet test is the single fastest
authentication check. Hardware diagnostics combine material (brass =
non-magnetic), construction (turnlock rivet count, dogleash spring, casting
quality), and stamping ("COACH" stamp presence by era). No single check
settles a bag — hardware reads alongside creed, lining, and country of
origin.

## Brass test

Pre-2000 Coach hardware is solid brass throughout: turnlock face and bar,
dogleash clip body, D-rings, buckles, feet, and rivet heads
[coachbagrehab-dating, vintagecoachie, vintagecoachbags-authenticity].

- **Magnet sticks → not brass → likely fake or post-vintage.** Brass is
  non-magnetic; a magnet that grabs the turnlock or dogleash points to
  plated steel, which is either a counterfeit substitute or a much later
  piece [vintagecoachbags-authenticity, thriftyrunner-authenticating].
- **Patina** on real brass develops a warm honey-to-amber tone over years
  of contact and oxidation; it is not uniform across a piece and tends to
  be deepest at handled spots (turnlock face, dogleash thumb tab).
- **Plated-steel tells**: silver or grey underlayer revealed where the
  surface has worn through (corners, edges of the turnlock face, the
  dogleash thumb tab), and weight that feels light for the size.

## Turnlock

The brass turnlock was introduced in **1964** by Bonnie Cashin — two years
into her Coach tenure, not at her 1962 hire — inspired by the closure on
her 1940s convertible automobile [vintagefashionguild-cashin]. The fastener
was **not Coach-exclusive**: the same turnlock appeared on Cashin's
collections for Philip Sills, Meyers, Crescendoe-Superb, HBA, and D. Klein
[vintagefashionguild-cashin]. Coach is most associated with it because of
longevity and brand visibility, not because it owned the design.

Construction:

- Two-piece face (the fixed plate riveted to the bag) plus a rotating bar
  that pivots around a central post.
- Riveted through a brass back plate on the interior side; rivet count and
  head shape are model-specific and should match catalog references for
  the model in question.
- The rotating bar is **cast**, with a smooth profile and rounded edges on
  authentic pieces. **Stamped-out** bars (flat, sharp-edged, sheet-metal
  feel) are a strong fake tell [vintagecoachbags-authenticity].

Stamping:

- "COACH" stamping appears on **later** turnlocks — typically the back
  plate or the bar — and is **not** present on Cashin-era pieces.
- Absence of a "COACH" stamp is therefore not by itself a fake tell on a
  bag plausibly placed in E2 (1962–1974) or early E3 (1975–1984)
  [coachbagrehab-dating, purseforum-authenticate-coach].
- Wrong rivet counts and stamped-out (rather than cast) bars are stronger
  signals than stamping presence/absence.

## Dogleash clip

Spring-loaded oval clip used as the strap-attachment hardware on most
NYC-era Coach bags with detachable straps. Authentic dogleash clips:

- Solid brass, with a smooth spring action — the thumb tab depresses
  cleanly without grinding, and the gate snaps closed without lag.
- Even oval geometry; no visible casting seams down the long axis.
- "COACH" stamped on the body in late E3 onward (roughly the 1980s)
  [purseforum-vintage-date-codes, vintagecoachbags-authenticity]. As with
  turnlocks, absence on earlier pieces is expected, not suspicious.

Counterfeit tells:

- **Stiffer spring** that requires more thumb pressure than the smooth
  authentic action.
- **Casting seams** visible along the oval body — authentic pieces are
  finished smooth.
- **Uneven oval** geometry; one end fatter than the other, or the gate
  not seating flush when closed.
- **Wrong mounting hardware** at the clip's base — stamped sheet metal
  where the original is cast brass, or rivets that don't match the rest
  of the bag's hardware.

## D-rings, buckles, feet

The supporting hardware follows the same brass-and-cast rule as turnlocks
and dogleash clips:

- **D-rings**: solid brass, even thickness around the ring, no plating
  wear-through revealing a base metal.
- **Buckles**: solid brass on belts and adjustable straps; tongue and
  frame both cast, no stamped-sheet construction.
- **Feet** (where present, e.g., on briefcase silhouettes): solid brass
  domes or low cylinders, riveted through the base; no plating wear.

Per-model rivet counts, foot placement, and D-ring positions vary; record
them on each `models/<slug>.md` page rather than asserting a global rule
here.

## Springlock closure (Fall 1990+ diagnostic)

**Update 2026-05-02 (research stream O3).** The **Springlock
closure** is a Coach mechanism documented as a **Fall 1990 design
innovation** [coach-catalogs-1960-1989]. First implementations:

- **9853 Binocular Bag** (Fall 1990)
- **9886 Avenue Bag** (Fall 1990)
- **9826 Mini Belt Bag** (slightly later in the 1990 sequence)

By Holiday 1991 (per M's main file), the Springlock appears on
additional models including the Court Bag (9870). Authentication
implications:
- **A Coach bag with a Springlock closure should NOT predate Fall
  1990.** Pre-1990 bags use turnlock, magnetic snap, zipper, or
  drawstring closures.
- **Pre-1990 production date + Springlock closure = wrong on its
  face.**
- The Springlock joins the brass turnlock (Cashin 1964 patent) as
  a Coach signature mechanism. Both are solid brass, non-magnetic.
- Magnet test still applies — Springlock should fail the magnet
  test (brass).

## Late-1990s nickel-hardware shift (E5 diagnostic)

**Update 2026-05-02 (research stream M4).** "Brass through and through"
holds for E1–E4 (1941–1994) but breaks down in two specific ways
within the late-NYC / Krakoff transition (E4-E5 boundary):

1. **Coach Neo Collection (1998)** — bags carry a silver/nickel
   **"COACH NEW YORK" metal plaque** on the bag front. This is the
   **first metal exterior brand-plaque on a Coach bag, predating
   Signature C (2001) by ~3 years** [coach-catalogs-1960-1989]. The
   reframe: the "first metal logo" pivot in Coach's visible-branding
   history is Coach Neo's nickel plaque, not the Signature C jacquard.

2. **Music-named Classic styles (1998 Holiday Preview)** — Cha Cha,
   Mambo, Disco, Tango, Swing styles within the heritage Classic line
   shipped with **NICKEL hardware** [coach-catalogs-1960-1989]. This
   is nickel **inside** the heritage line (not a separate modernist
   sub-collection), making it the first documented break from "brass
   through and through" within the Classics.

3. **Tribeca Collection (Spring Preview 1998)** — embossed "COACH"
   wordmark at the **base of the bag** [coach-catalogs-1960-1989].
   First systematic exterior wordmark on a Coach handbag.

For authentication purposes:
- A "1998 Coach Neo" with all-brass hardware (no nickel plaque) is
  wrong on its face.
- A music-named Classic (Cha Cha / Mambo / Disco / Tango / Swing) is
  expected to fail the magnet test by design — the nickel hardware
  is the original spec.
- An exterior "COACH" wordmark on a bag claiming pre-1998 manufacture
  is a strong red flag (no documented vintage NYC-era exterior
  wordmark survives the Tribeca debut).

## Replacement-component caveat

Coach's repair operation — consolidated in **Jacksonville, Florida from
July 1999** [coach-10k-fiscal-2003] — installed replacement turnlocks,
dogleash clips, and other hardware during legitimate restoration work
through the Coach Repair / Factory Restoration program.

A bag with a "wrong era" hardware component is therefore **not
automatically a fake**. It may be:

1. A counterfeit (the most common case for a bag with multiple wrong
   tells stacked together).
2. An authentic bag that went through Coach Repair and received a
   later-era replacement part — still authentic, just no longer all
   original.
3. A "Frankenbag" assembled from real parts plus replacements outside
   the Coach Repair channel.

Reading hardware in isolation without checking the creed, lining,
construction, and provenance will misclassify case (2) as case (1).

## Open verification items

1. **USPTO design patent on the turnlock.** A 1960s design patent under
   Cashin's name or assigned to Coach Leatherware / Gail Leather Products
   is the canonical primary source for the turnlock's introduction date,
   geometry, and ownership chain [uspto-patents]. Not yet pulled. Per
   `RETRIEVAL.md` Operation 3.
2. **USPTO patent on the dogleash clip variant** Coach used. Existence
   not yet confirmed.
3. **Per-model rivet counts** documented from dated catalog imagery —
   the strongest single fake tell once cataloged.
