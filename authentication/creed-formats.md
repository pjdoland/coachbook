## Lead

The leather creed patch is the single most diagnostic element of vintage Coach
authentication. Its wording evolved through identifiable eras — from no creed
at all in the earliest years, through the numbered "COACH LEATHERWARE" stamp
of the NYC and Carlstadt period, to the simplified "COACH" mark that appears
once offshore production begins in the mid-1990s. Used carefully, creed
wording alone narrows a bag's origin to a window of roughly a decade.

## Era-by-era wording

### E1 — Pre-Cashin (1941–1961)

No leather creed patch. Per E1 (coachbagrehab-dating), the earliest Coach-
branded goods carried a **small metal interior tag only** — no creed, no
serial. Leather stamps inside the bag are also documented for this and the
adjacent period; absence of a creed on a bag from this era is expected, not a
fake tell.

Confidence: tentative. Single Tier 3 source.

### E2 — Cashin (1962–1974)

Cashin-era creed evolution divides into three sub-eras (per E1, propagated
in research/E §8). All three are tentative — the taxonomy comes from a
single Tier 3 source.

- **E2a (early 1962 – mid-1960s)**: small metal interior tag only; carries
  forward from E1.
- **E2b (mid-1960s – late 1960s)**: leather stamp inside the bag; **no
  creed patch and no serial number**. E1 quotes: "leather stamp inside but
  no creed is actually the same style…placed late 1960s, early 1970s."
- **E2c (late 1960s – early 1970s)**: leather stamp continues; transition
  to a creed patch begins toward the end of the period.

Confidence: tentative.

### E3 — Early NYC (1975–1984)

Numbered creed patches appear. The wording most often documented for this
window is **"COACH LEATHERWARE"** with a country-of-origin line beneath.
Two location strings appear, in rough chronological order:

- **"Made in New York City, U.S.A."** — appears on the earliest numbered
  creeds, consistent with NYC manufacturing through approximately 1980
  (multiple Tier 3 sources, summarized in research/E §1).
- **"Made in United States"** — appears as US production diversified to
  other US plants after roughly 1980.

Serial format in this era is digit-only, commonly written as `xxx-xxxx`
(1970s) or `xxxx-xxx` (1980s). These are **unique serial numbers, not
style numbers**, and are not decodable for factory or year on their own
(E3, E5).

Confidence: tentative. The 1970s/1980s dash-position split is repeated
across Tier 3 sources without primary citation.

### E4 — Late NYC / Carlstadt (1985–1994)

The diagnostic wording for this era is **"COACH" with "LEATHERWARE"
stamped in small caps directly beneath**, plus **"Made in United States"**.
This is the single most-cited textual anchor for dating vintage Coach
(E1, E2, E11, E13). Bags from the Sara Lee acquisition (1985) through the
mid-1990s offshoring transition typically show this format.

Serial format remains digit-only (commonly `xxxx-xxx`) through roughly
1994.

Confidence: tentative — but more frequently corroborated across Tier 3
sources than other claims on this page.

### E5 — Transition (1995–2002)

Three things change roughly together in the mid-1990s:

1. **"LEATHERWARE" disappears** from the creed; the mark becomes "COACH"
   only.
2. **The serial format shifts** from a digit-only unique serial to a
   letter-number style code, e.g. `B6D-9927` (month letter, year digit,
   factory letter, dash, style number). See `serial-numbers.md` (planned)
   for the full decoder and the 1996/2006 single-digit-year ambiguity.
3. **Country of origin diversifies.** Creeds carry "Made in Costa Rica,"
   "Made in Hungary," "Made in Turkey," "Made in Dominican Republic,"
   "Made in Mexico," and "Made in Italy" (Italy used for Madison /
   Gramercy exotic lines). Costa Rica is associated with factory letter
   "P" per E12 (tentative).

By the 2000s the "Handcrafted in China" wording becomes common (E1) —
post-vintage for this archive.

Confidence: tentative. Country dates are repeated across Tier 3 sources;
Tier 1 anchoring (Coach S-1 prospectus, dated catalogs) is pending.

## Key textual anchor

> **"LEATHERWARE" stamped in tiny letters beneath "COACH" on the creed
> indicates pre-1994 manufacture.** (E1, also corroborated in E2, E11, E13.)

This is the single most-cited textual anchor for dating a vintage Coach
bag. It is necessary but not sufficient: cross-check against country of
origin, serial format, hardware, and lining before declaring an era.

## Decision tree

Adapted from research/E §11. Each branch must be cross-checked against
hardware, lining, and construction; no single check is sufficient.

1. **Is there a leather creed patch?**
   - No, but a small metal interior tag → likely E2a (early 1960s).
   - No creed, no metal tag, just a leather stamp inside → likely E2b
     or early E2c (late 1960s – early 1970s).
   - Yes, leather creed with serial → continue.

2. **Does the creed read "COACH LEATHERWARE" with "LEATHERWARE" in small
   letters under "COACH"?**
   - Yes → pre-1994 (E3 or E4).
   - No, just "COACH" with no "LEATHERWARE" → 1994 or later (E5).

3. **What does the country-of-origin line say?**
   - "Made in New York City, U.S.A." + LEATHERWARE → 1970s through
     roughly 1980 (E3).
   - "Made in United States" + LEATHERWARE → roughly 1980–1994 (late
     E3 into E4).
   - "Made in Costa Rica" / "Hungary" / "Turkey" / "Dominican Republic"
     / "Mexico" → 1994 through early 2000s (E5).
   - "Made in Italy" → exotic-skin or Madison / Gramercy line; cross-
     check with model.
   - "Handcrafted in China" → 2000s onward; post-vintage for this
     archive.

4. **What does the serial look like?**
   - All digits, e.g. `xxxx-xxx` or `xxx-xxxx` → pre-1994.
   - Letter-digit-letter-dash-digits, e.g. `B6D-9927` → 1994 onward.
     Year is single-digit (1994–2003) or double-digit (2004+); single-
     digit codes are ambiguous between 1990s and 2000s without
     contextual cross-checks (creed wording, country of origin,
     hardware, lining).
   - No serial at all → either pre-numbered era (E1, E2a, E2b) or a
     small accessory / select 1990s pebbled-leather model that
     legitimately shipped without one. Absence is not by itself a
     fake tell.

5. **Hardware**: brass turnlock should be non-magnetic; "COACH"
   stamping appears on later turnlocks but is not present on earlier
   Cashin-era pieces.

6. **Construction**: edges should be raw or burnished, not painted, on
   pre-1995 bags.

## Caveats

Every claim on this page is currently `tentative`. All cited sources are
Tier 3 (community blogs, dealer guides, and forum threads). No Tier 1
(primary) or Tier 2 (reputable secondary) source on creed-wording
chronology has yet been located.

Specifically:

- The Cashin-era E2a / E2b / E2c sub-division comes from a single Tier 3
  source (E1) and has not been corroborated.
- The 1980 NYC-to-other-US-plants pivot is repeated across Tier 3
  sources without a primary date anchor.
- Country-of-origin dates (Costa Rica from ~1994, Turkey from ~1996,
  etc.) are community consensus, not catalog-anchored.
- The "LEATHERWARE → COACH only" transition is described as occurring
  around 1994 alongside the serial-format change, but the exact cutoff
  bag and date are not documented in any primary source we have located.

The next step is **primary anchoring**: matching specific creed wording
to dated catalog imagery (Coach Pinterest "From the Archives," PurseForum
member-uploaded catalog scans) and to bags with independent date
provenance. Until that is done, no claim on this page should be promoted
to `confirmed` or even `likely`. See research/E §10 for the open
research items.

The 2014 inflection (Coach stops putting serial numbers on creeds; "Made
In" moves to a sewn-in tag, per E8) is post-vintage but worth noting
because authentication guidance written after 2014 sometimes confuses the
post-2014 tag system with the pre-2014 creed serial system.
