# Coach Repair and Factory Restoration

## Lead

For most of the company's history Coach ran an in-house repair program
that returned vintage bags to customers with replacement components —
turnlocks, dogleash clips, beanbag hangtags, creed patches, zippers,
refinished edges. **Bags with mismatched-era hardware are therefore
not automatically counterfeit.** They may be authentic bags that
went through Coach Repair and came back with later-era replacement
parts. This page exists so the rest of the archive can stop repeating
the caveat: link here.

## What Coach Repair routinely replaced

| Component | Replacement behavior |
|---|---|
| Turnlocks | Swapped to whatever era's turnlock was current at the time of repair. |
| Dogleash clips | Same — current-era replacement, not period-original. |
| Beanbag hangtags | Restored bags shipped out with new hangtags as a matter of routine. |
| Zippers | Replaced when worn or broken; the replacement reflects the repair date, not the bag's manufacture date. |
| Creed patches | Replaced on heavily-restored bags; era-mismatched creed on an otherwise consistent bag may indicate a service replacement. |
| Edge finish | Refinished as part of restoration; a refinished edge alone is not disqualifying. |

## Confirmed dates

- **July 1999** — Coach consolidated worldwide warehousing,
  distribution, and **repair** functions into a 560,000 sq ft facility
  in Jacksonville, Florida [coach-10k-fiscal-2003]. From that point
  on, restored bags returning to customers shipped from Jacksonville.

## Open research

- **Program start date** — when Coach formally launched a repair
  program. Pre-1999 facility locations are not in the research files.
- **Program end date** — Coach Repair as the original program (later
  re-branded as part of Coach Service / Coach Restoration) wound down
  but the precise dates are not in the research files.
- **Documentation of replacement-part inventory** — period service
  manuals, parts lists, or customer-service guidance would let us
  date specific replacement components more precisely.

## How to use this page

When you authenticate a vintage Coach bag and find a single component
out of era — wrong-era turnlock, beanbag hangtag younger than the
creed, mismatched zipper — **don't conclude counterfeit on that
component alone.** It may be:

1. A genuine era-mismatch indicating a counterfeit (other tells will
   stack with it — wrong creed wording, wrong stitching, wrong lining).
2. An authentic bag that went through Coach Repair and received a
   later-era replacement part. Still authentic; just no longer all
   original.
3. A "Frankenbag" assembled from real parts plus parts replaced
   outside the Coach Repair channel.

Reading any single component in isolation will misclassify case (2)
as case (1). Always combine: creed wording, lining, construction,
provenance, and the overall era-consistency of the rest of the bag.
