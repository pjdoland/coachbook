## Lead

Zippers are date-coded surrogates: a bag's zipper maker should be era-consistent
with its creed. A YKK zipper on a bag claiming 1960s manufacture is suspect.
Talon dominates Coach zippers from the pre-mid-1970s window; YKK takes over
from the late 1970s onward. Used carefully, zipper maker narrows a bag's era
to roughly a decade and corroborates (or contradicts) the creed.

## Maker timeline

All entries `tentative`. Era windows below are community-derived and need
primary anchoring against dated catalog imagery or bags with independent
date provenance.

- **Talon** — pre-mid-1970s common on Coach (folklore consensus, needs
  primary confirmation; see research/E §5). Tooth profile and slider stamps
  are the main diagnostic. Talon zippers persist on some 1970s pieces past
  the YKK transition.
- **YKK** — dominant from late 1970s onward. YKK has a published date-stamp
  system on some sliders; decoding chart belongs in a separate research
  item (open verification below).
- **Coats** — occasional, era-specific. Document per-model when seen.
- **Ideal** — occasional, era-specific. Document per-model when seen.
- **Conmar** — very early; rare on Coach.

## Pulls

- **Material**: solid brass tab on E2–E4 bags; non-magnetic, consistent
  with the brass-hardware standard documented in `authentication/hardware.md`
  (planned).
- **Stamping**: "COACH" stamped on later examples. Earlier Cashin-era pulls
  are typically unstamped; absence is not by itself a fake tell.
- **Shape**: pull shapes vary by model — lozenge, square-ended, leather-
  tabbed. Per-model pull shape goes in each `models/<slug>.md` entry.

## Era-inconsistency tell

> "A bag whose zipper maker postdates the creed-format era is suspect."
> (research/E §5, summarizing the cross-check between zipper maker and creed
> wording.)

A YKK zipper on a creed reading "COACH LEATHERWARE / Made in New York City,
U.S.A." is the canonical mismatch: the creed places the bag in the 1970s
through roughly 1980 (E3), while YKK dominance starts in the late 1970s and
ramps through the 1980s. A Talon zipper on an offshore-creed bag (E5,
"Made in Costa Rica" etc.) is the inverse mismatch.

Zipper-maker mismatch alone does not prove fakery; see the replacement
caveat below.

## Replacement-zipper caveat

Coach Repair replaced zippers as part of legitimate restoration —
see [Coach Repair and Factory Restoration](../coach-repair/) for the
canonical caveat. A wrong-era zipper on an otherwise consistent bag
may indicate a service swap, not a counterfeit. Combine with other
signals before rejecting a bag on zipper evidence alone:
- creed wording and serial format
- hardware stamping and brass response to a magnet
- lining, edge treatment, and stitching
- country-of-origin line

A replacement-zipper bag with consistent creed, brass turnlock, glove-
tanned leather, and raw-or-burnished edges is plausibly authentic and
serviced. A wrong-era zipper combined with painted edges and magnetic
hardware is not.

## Open verification items

1. **Primary inspection of dated bags or catalog imagery** to ground each
   maker's actual usage window at Coach. Current windows are community
   folklore (research/E §5); a single dated catalog photograph showing a
   visible slider stamp would anchor the Talon-to-YKK transition.
2. **YKK date-stamp decoding chart**: YKK published a slider date-stamp
   system; capture the canonical decoder in a separate research item and
   link from this page.
3. **Coats / Ideal / Conmar usage windows at Coach**: each currently a
   one-line claim with no primary citation. Worth confirming against
   PurseForum dated-bag threads (`purseforum-vintage-date-codes`) and
   member-uploaded catalog scans (`purseforum-vintage-catalogs`,
   browser-only).
4. **Per-model zipper-maker logs**: as model entries are written, record
   the zipper maker(s) seen on dated examples in the `authentication:`
   block. Aggregate logs will refine the era windows above.
