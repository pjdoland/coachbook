# Serial number formats

Coach serial numbers serve **different purposes in different eras**.
Before 1994 they were random unique serial numbers stamped into the
creed and could not be decoded for date, factory, or style. From 1994
onward Coach replaced them with a structured letter-number style code
that encodes month, year, factory, and style number. The two systems
share the creed location and a superficially similar look, but no
decoder works across both.

The single most important consequence: **a Coach style number alone
never dates a bag.** Style numbers were reused across years on the
same silhouette (`9927` = Willis across multiple seasons), and the
post-1994 letter-number code carries the dating information — not the
trailing four-to-five digits everyone reaches for first.

All sources currently underpinning this page are Tier 3 (community
blogs, dealer guides, PurseForum threads). No Tier 1 catalog,
patent, or filing has been pulled to anchor the decoder. Treat every
specific claim below as `tentative` until a dated catalog page with a
visible creed is matched against the decoder for an unambiguous year.

---

## Pre-1994: random unique serials

Before 1994, the creed (where present) carried a digit-only serial
that was unique to the bag — not a style number, not an encoded date.
It cannot be decoded.

Two sub-formats are repeated across community sources:

- **`xxx-xxxx`** — three digits, dash, four digits — described as the
  1970s pattern.
- **`xxxx-xxx`** — four digits, dash, three digits — described as the
  1980s pattern.

Both formats are reported by lovetoknow-coach-serials and
essexfashionhouse-serials without primary citation. The dash-position
flip between decades is plausible but **flagged tentative**. A dated
catalog or museum-cataloged bag with a visible serial would confirm
or refute it.

What these serials do *not* tell you:
- Year of manufacture.
- Factory.
- Model (style number was tracked separately on the order form, not
  always on the bag itself).

What they can tell you, indirectly:
- Combined with **creed wording** (small "LEATHERWARE" beneath
  "COACH" → pre-1994), they confirm an era rather than narrowing
  within it.
- Combined with **country of origin** stamped into the creed, they
  rule out post-offshoring manufacture.

## Bags without serials

Several legitimate categories shipped without any serial number.
**Absence of a serial is not evidence of fakery in these cases.**

- **Pre-1980s bags**, especially Cashin-era pieces with only an
  interior leather stamp or a small metal interior tag.
- **Small accessories** — key fobs, kissing-lock coin purses, mini
  bags — often shipped without serials throughout Coach's vintage
  era.
- **Some 1990s pebbled-leather bags** per lovetoknow-coach-serials.

A bag that *should* have a serial for its era but does not (e.g., a
classic 1980s glove-tanned satchel with a creed but a blank space
where the serial should be) is a different signal — possible
replacement creed, possible counterfeit. Era expectations have to
come first.

## Post-1994: letter-number style code

From 1994 onward, the creed serial follows a structured pattern that
encodes manufacturing month, year, factory, and style number. Per
coachbagrehab-dating (the most specific Tier 3 source):

### Pattern

```
[A-M][year-digit][factory-letter]-[style-number]
```

Worked example from coachbagrehab-dating: `B6D-9927`.

### Position 1 — month letter

The alphabet from A to M, **skipping I** (to avoid confusion with
the digit 1):

| Letter | Month | Letter | Month |
|--------|-------|--------|-------|
| A | January | G | July |
| B | February | H | August |
| C | March | J | September |
| D | April | K | October |
| E | May | L | November |
| F | June | M | December |

### Position 2 — year code

Coach used a single-digit year code from 1994 through 2003, then
switched to double digits:

| Year code | Year |
|-----------|------|
| 4 | 1994 |
| 5 | 1995 |
| 6 | 1996 |
| 7 | 1997 |
| 8 | 1998 |
| 9 | 1999 |
| 0 | 2000 |
| 1 | 2001 |
| 2 | 2002 |
| 3 | 2003 |
| 04 | 2004 |
| 05 | 2005 |
| ... | ... |

The single-digit-to-double-digit transition is what creates the core
ambiguity flagged below.

### Position 3 — factory letter

A single letter encoding the manufacturing facility. **Coach has
never publicly released the full factory-code map.** The community
has pieced together fragments from dated bags, but only one letter
is widely repeated across independent sources:

- **P = Costa Rica** (per purseforum-costa-rica)

Other letters appear in community posts but without consistent
attribution. Treat factory-letter readings other than `P` as
unconfirmed.

### Position 4 — style number

Four or five digits identifying the model. The same style number was
**reused across years** for the same silhouette — `9927` is Willis
whether the creed reads `B6D-9927` or `K8C-9927`. The style number
is therefore a model identifier, not a date.

## CRITICAL AMBIGUITY: single-digit year codes

The single-digit year code from 1994–2003 collides with later years
when read in isolation. **The same code resolves to two different
decades.**

> `B6D-9927` reads as **February 1996, factory D, Willis** *or*
> **February 2006, factory D, Willis** — depending only on which
> decade you assume.

The decoder cannot resolve this on its own. The single-digit "6" in
the year position is unambiguous **only if other context places the
bag firmly in one decade or the other.** Cross-checks that resolve
it:

1. **Creed wording.** If the creed reads "COACH LEATHERWARE" with
   the small-caps "LEATHERWARE" beneath "COACH", the bag is pre-1994
   — which already contradicts a 1994-onward letter-number serial,
   so this is a sanity check more than a tiebreaker. For 1994–~1996
   transitional pieces, "LEATHERWARE" wording **plus** a letter-
   number serial pushes the year toward the earlier reading.
2. **Country of origin in the creed.** Costa Rica production began
   ~1994 and ran through ~2001. A `P` factory letter combined with
   a single-digit year overwhelmingly reads as the 1990s. "Made in
   China" stamps appeared in the 2000s and push toward the later
   reading.
3. **Hardware.** Solid brass turnlocks (non-magnetic, heavy) align
   with 1990s production; lighter plated hardware leans 2000s.
4. **Lining and construction.** Cotton twill or branded jacquard
   linings shifted across the decade; an unlined glove-tanned
   construction reads 1990s.

**The decoder should never be cited as a year on its own.** Every
published decoder example (including `B6D-9927`) needs its decade
disambiguated by an independent diagnostic.

## Worked example

From coachbagrehab-dating, presented with both possible readings:

**`B6D-9927`**

| Position | Value | Meaning |
|----------|-------|---------|
| `B` | month | February |
| `6` | year | 1996 *or* 2006 |
| `D` | factory | (unmapped — community guides do not consistently identify D) |
| `9927` | style | Willis |

Reading one — **February 1996, Willis** — is consistent with peak
US glove-tanned production, "LEATHERWARE" creed wording in the
transitional period, US-made country of origin.

Reading two — **February 2006, Willis** — places the bag in
post-vintage offshore production. By 2006 the Willis silhouette had
been reissued; creed wording, lining, and country of origin would
all have shifted.

The cited example does not specify which is correct. Without a
photograph of the full creed and an independent date anchor, the
decoder alone cannot decide.

### Worked resolution: a concrete buyer's check

Suppose a listing photo shows `B6D-9927` and you need to settle the
1996-vs-2006 ambiguity before bidding. Walk the cross-checks in
order until one fires:

1. **Read the country line on the creed.** If it says **"Made in
   United States"** or **"Made in U.S.A."**, the bag is 1996 — Coach
   ended significant US production in 1994 and the only US-made
   bags shipping in 2006 came from the Lares, Puerto Rico facility,
   which closed April 2002 [coach-10k-fiscal-2003]. A 2006 Willis
   would say "Made in [offshore country]," not U.S.A.
2. **Check the wordmark.** If "LEATHERWARE" is stamped in small caps
   under "COACH", the bag pre-dates the late-1994 wordmark change
   in nearly every case. A 1996 transitional bag might still carry
   the older wordmark; a 2006 bag would not.
3. **Check the leather edges.** Raw or burnished glove-tanned edges
   read 1990s. Painted-glossy edges on a "Coach" Willis read 2006+
   or fake.
4. **Magnet-test the turnlock.** Solid brass (non-magnetic) is
   consistent with both decades, so this rules out fakes but doesn't
   resolve the ambiguity by itself.
5. **Check the lining.** Unlined glove-tanned leather inside the
   bag reads 1990s; cotton twill or jacquard lining reads 2000s.

If steps 1–3 agree on a decade, you have a confident reading.
If they disagree, the bag is either a Frankenbag, a fake, or
serviced through Coach Repair — post the canonical photo set to
PurseForum's "Authenticate This COACH" thread before bidding.

**Never publish a date from the serial code alone.** The decoder
narrows era, then cross-checks finish the date.

## The 2014 inflection (out of vintage scope)

Per purseforum-authenticate-coach: **mid-2014, Coach stopped
putting serial numbers on creeds.** Modern Coach uses a separate
sewn-in "Made In" tag with a different identifier scheme. This is
post-vintage and out of scope for the archive, but it matters for
two reasons:

1. Authentication guidance written after 2014 sometimes conflates
   the new sewn-tag system with the pre-2014 creed serial system.
2. A creed-stamped serial confirms pre-2014 manufacture; a bag
   with no creed serial but a separate sewn tag is post-2014, not
   a counterfeit.

## Open verification items

The serial decoder is folklore-prone. Community sources mostly
agree, but no Tier 1 source on serial-number decoding has been
located. To anchor this page:

1. **A dated catalog page with a creed visible.** Matching a
   specific creed (with full letter-number serial) to a catalog
   year would resolve the single-digit-year ambiguity for at least
   one example and validate the month-letter and year-digit tables.
2. **The PurseForum vintage-date-codes thread (purseforum-vintage-
   date-codes).** Extract the original post and any pinned
   authoritative reply for canonical decoder reproduction; check
   whether any member has posted dated-catalog evidence.
3. **A primary-source citation for the factory-code letter map.**
   `P = Costa Rica` is the only widely-repeated assignment;
   community lists for other letters are inconsistent.
4. **A bag with independent date provenance** — e.g., gifted with
   receipt, photographed on a dated magazine cover, held in a
   museum collection with acquisition date — and a clear creed
   photograph.

Until at least the first item is satisfied, every numeric claim on
this page remains `tentative`.
