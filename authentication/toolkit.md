## Lead

Authenticating vintage Coach is mostly a matter of careful inspection — a few
cheap tools dramatically improve the signal-to-noise of that inspection. The
diagnostic value lives in informed examination of the bag's structure
(creed, hardware, stitching, leather), not in any single test. This page
lists what to use and why; the entire kit assembles for under $50.

## Tools

### 10× jeweler's loupe

The single most useful tool. Magnified inspection of the creed reveals
counterfeit kerning, fatter strokes, and serif substitutions that are
invisible at arm's length. Also use it to read stitch lock-offs (clean
backstitch on authentic bags vs loose tail threads on fakes), embossing
depth, and hardware casting seams. Pairs well with a phone macro lens for
documenting findings.

### Digital calipers (mm)

Measure leather thickness and hardware dimensions; record per-model on
the `authentication:` block of each model entry. Counterfeits are commonly
made from thinner leather than authentic glove-tanned cowhide, and
hardware dimensions drift on cast reproductions. Without numbers,
"feels thin" is not a defensible authentication call.

### Kitchen scale (1 g resolution)

Hardware weight is a brass proxy. Solid brass weighs noticeably more than
plated steel of the same dimensions. A turnlock or dogleash clip that
feels light for its size is suspect. Combine with a magnet for
triangulation — the two tests cross-check each other.

### Strong rare-earth magnet

Brass is non-magnetic. A magnet that sticks to the turnlock, dogleash,
D-rings, or feet is a high-confidence fake signal — *or* a Coach Repair /
Factory Restoration steel replacement on an otherwise authentic bag.
See `authentication/hardware.md` for the replacement-hardware caveat
before declaring a bag fake on a magnet test alone.

### 365 nm UV flashlight

Modern adhesives often fluoresce; original Coach glues do not. UV
inspection of the creed perimeter and lining seams surfaces post-
restoration repairs and creed swaps that are otherwise invisible. A
fluorescing creed edge on a "1980s" bag means somebody has been in there.

### Color-corrected daylight bulb

For photographing bags for second opinions. Eliminates the warm/cool
color cast that makes leather look wrong in iPhone photos under
incandescent or fluorescent light. Critical when posting to community
authenticators who are evaluating leather color and patina.

### Reference ruler in every photo (cm and inches)

Provides scale for second-opinion authenticators. PurseForum's
"Authenticate This Coach" thread (`purseforum-authenticate-coach`) and
similar community resources require a ruler in creed close-ups; without
it, dimensional checks against a known authentic example are impossible.

### Soft brush + microfiber cloth

Physical inspection without damaging leather. Surface dust and grime hide
embossing detail, edge treatment, and hardware patina. Clean before
inspecting; never inspect through a dirty surface.

### Phone camera

12 MP or better is fine. The loupe pairs well with a phone macro lens for
shareable creed close-ups. A dedicated camera is not required for
authentication work.

## Cost

The entire toolkit assembles for under $50. The jeweler's loupe is the
single most expensive item at roughly $15–25 for an optical-grade 10×
triplet; the rest are commodity items.

## What you don't need

Several products are marketed as authentication aids but add no
diagnostic value beyond what informed inspection already provides:

- **Chemical solvent / leather authenticity pens** — these test for one
  property (typically dye uptake or coating) that has no reliable
  correlation with Coach authenticity. They damage the leather they touch.
- **"Authenticator" apps that scan a creed photo** — none of the publicly
  available ones have a documented training set on dated vintage Coach,
  and the false-confidence rate is high.
- **Fluorescence kits beyond a single 365 nm flashlight** — the marginal
  value of additional wavelengths on leather goods is low.

The diagnostic value is in informed inspection of the bag's structure —
creed wording (`authentication/creed-formats.md`), serial format
(`authentication/serial-numbers.md`), hardware (`authentication/hardware.md`),
and construction — not in chemistry.
