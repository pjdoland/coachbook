1. **Add reference photos to every authentication page — the site is currently text-only.**
   The `images/` directory is empty and `public/` only contains a favicon. Pages on creed-formats, hardware, lining, hangtags, zippers, and stitching all describe diagnostic features in prose without showing them. Yourgreatfinds and even random eBay seller listicles beat this site on first impression because they at least show a creed photo. Practical fix: even sourced/credited PurseForum thread links or Coach Originals product photos embedded with a clear caption would massively raise utility. Right now a buyer who has never seen a "small-caps LEATHERWARE beneath COACH" cannot use `/authentication/creed-formats/` to make the call. **Rationale: Photos-needed.**

2. **Lead the wizard with the magnet test, not the creed.**
   In the field — at a thrift store, an estate sale, with a seller standing there — I am holding a fridge magnet to the turnlock before I even unzip the bag. The wizard's first question ("Is there a leather creed patch?") forces the user into a low-light bag interior before the fastest, highest-signal check has been performed. The site's own counterfeits/index.md "Authenticator's quick triage" correctly puts magnet first, then creed. The wizard should match that order. Move hardware to Step 1; bag without magnet response = early branch. **Rationale: Wizard-flow.**

3. **The "Made in Italy" wizard option is too narrow and will mislead users.**
   Current sub-text says: "Exotic-skin or Madison/Gramercy line; line-specific, not era-specific." In practice I see "Made in Italy" creeds on small leather goods (wallets, key fobs), some 1990s Madison styles, and Krakoff-era pieces well outside Madison/Gramercy. The wizard makes the user feel they got an unusual bag when they probably have an SLG. Either expand the sub-text to mention SLGs and 1990s SKU sets, or bounce them to a dedicated `/authentication/italy-production/` stub. **Rationale: Authentication.**

4. **The serial-format wizard step omits the very common "I cannot read the serial" branch.**
   Real-world creeds are often worn smooth, ink-faded, or partially obscured by oil from decades of handling. The wizard offers four serial options but no "cannot read" branch — users will guess, which corrupts the era-consistency check downstream. Add: "Serial is present but unreadable / partially worn — skip and rely on creed wording, country, hardware." Maps to "no flag added; era-context dependent." **Rationale: Wizard-flow.**

5. **The wizard verdict logic treats one red flag as "possibly Coach Repair" — too generous given Frankenbag prevalence.**
   The verdict for `flags === 1` says "single red flag is consistent with a replacement component (Coach Repair work) on an otherwise authentic bag — not by itself a fake tell." In my experience, the single most common single-flag pattern in resale today is a replaced creed on a body — Frankenbag territory, not Coach Repair. The Frankenbags page itself acknowledges this. The wizard should soften toward "ambiguous — could be Coach Repair, could be a Frankenbag — re-check the unflagged dimensions twice and demand provenance for a creed swap." Right now the wizard sounds like a defense lawyer for the seller. **Rationale: Authentication.**

6. **The "Made in U.S.A. with LEATHERWARE absent" combination is missing from era-impossibility checks.**
   `country-of-origin.md` flags this combination in prose ("'Made in U.S.A.' with a post-LEATHERWARE creed (just 'COACH'), pre-2014 serial format, on a model with no documented late-US-production run — suspect"), but the wizard's `isInconsistent` function only catches pre-vs-post 1994 era mixing. It will pass a bag that has just-COACH wording with "Made in U.S.A." through to `likely-authentic` if other answers align. That's a real fake pattern circulating now. Add this combination to the inconsistency detector explicitly. **Rationale: Authentication.**

7. **Cashin-era / pre-creed authentication is the weakest section and the one collectors pay most for.**
   `pre-creed-era.md` documents E2a/E2b/E2c sub-eras as `tentative` from a single Tier 3 source, and the wizard's `verdict-no-creed` punts users to "post to PurseForum." For a $400-$2,500 Cashin-era bag, that is unhelpful. What's missing: the specific tells that separate a real Cashin-era Coach from a Cashin-era Sills/Meyers/Crescendoe-Superb piece (since Cashin used the same vocabulary across all of them). What hardware was Coach-stamped vs unstamped on which years? Which stripe-lining colorways are Coach-only vs cross-licensed? The Vintage Fashion Guild Cashin entry at least addresses this. **Rationale: Model-coverage / Authentication.**

8. **The "blue-and-white ticking pochette lining" claim needs a photo or it will be ignored.**
   Buried in `lining.md` — and it is one of the most useful era-specific tells for small leather goods that I see misidentified constantly. Two lines of prose will not save it from being overlooked. Without an image, users cannot match what they are looking at to "blue-and-white ticking." Same applies to "striped Cashin-era lining" — those are concrete visual diagnostics that disappear into bullet points. **Rationale: Photos-needed.**

9. **The "Coach LEATHERWARE creed kerning" tell in the Korean repros page is not actionable without a comparison image.**
   `korean-repros.md` cites "wrong creed kerning — letterspacing on COACH LEATHERWARE is visibly different from authentic; '1941' numerals are commonly fatter or sans-serif on the repros." That is exactly the kind of claim that needs side-by-side authentic-vs-fake creed photos to be useful. Without that, no one looking at a single creed knows what "wrong kerning" means. This is the highest-value photo opportunity on the site. **Rationale: Photos-needed.**

10. **The decision tree omits the "1994 transitional" reality where bags genuinely mix old and new diagnostics.**
    Bags from roughly 1993-1996 actually do show mixed-era characteristics — LEATHERWARE wording lingering on early Costa Rica creeds, digit-only serials on early offshore bags, brass hardware persisting on the first letter-number-serialed bags. The wizard's strict `isInconsistent` rule will flag legitimate transitional bags as fake. `creed-formats.md` mentions "transitional 1994–1996 pieces sometimes retain it" but the diagnostic flow doesn't account for it. Add a "transitional 1993-1996" tolerance: LEATHERWARE-wordmark + early-offshore + letter-number serial = plausible, not impossible. **Rationale: Folklore-correction.**

11. **The "single-digit-year ambiguity" warning is good — but the worked example resolves nothing.**
    `serial-numbers.md` correctly flags that `B6D-9927` reads as either Feb 1996 or Feb 2006. The page then walks through both readings and concludes the decoder cannot decide. That's responsible but unhelpful for a user holding a bag. What collectors actually do: read the creed wording (LEATHERWARE? = pre-1994 Willis run continuing through transition); look at the lining (unlined = 1990s; jacquard = 2000s); look at the dogleash for the COACH stamp (smaller and less crisp on later production). Add an explicit "how to disambiguate in 30 seconds" worked walkthrough showing each cross-check resolving in real time. **Rationale: Dating.**

12. **Missing model: the Station Bag.**
    Station Bag (style 9525) is one of the most-faked NYC-era models alongside Willis and Court — Korean repros explicitly target it (your own `korean-repros.md` lists "Willis, Court, and Station Bag" as repro targets). It is cited five times in the authentication corpus but has no model entry in `models/`. A buyer searching for "Coach Station Bag authentication" lands nowhere. Either add a stub `models/station.md` (you have the slug "station" referenced from Willis), or fix the orphan reference. **Rationale: Model-coverage.**

13. **No coverage of the City Bag (style 4130) or Ergo (1997).**
    Ergo is mentioned as the "first Krakoff Coach silhouette" on the Coach Neo page but has no entry. City Bag 4130 is one of the most-resold Carlstadt-era satchels and is absent. Bonnie Tote is listed as a model file, which is great — but Ergo and the various Manhattan Lightweights styles deserve dedicated entries since they are routinely encountered at resale. The Bleecker entry is excellent depth-wise; that level of rigor on Ergo would close a real gap. **Rationale: Model-coverage.**

14. **The "saddle-stitched vs lock-stitched" correction in `stitching.md` is correct but buries the lede.**
    The correction is gold — community wisdom is wrong here, and you call it out. But the page leads with a long methodological discussion before saying "vintage Coach is **machine lock-stitched, not saddle-stitched**." A buyer doing a 60-second sanity check will skip the page. Move the correction to the lead in bold, with the "12 SPI documented in 1985 catalogs" anchor right beneath it as the practical check. The hand-folded seam-binding 1989 quote is the strongest brand-voice anchor I have seen in any free-online Coach reference and deserves prominence — it crushes anything yourgreatfinds publishes. **Rationale: Folklore-correction.**

15. **The toolkit page recommends a 365 nm UV flashlight without explaining what to look at.**
    "UV inspection of the creed perimeter and lining seams surfaces post-restoration repairs and creed swaps that are otherwise invisible. A fluorescing creed edge on a '1980s' bag means somebody has been in there." That is correct in principle but missing the practical knowledge: which specific glues fluoresce blue vs yellow, what original Coach stitching looks like under UV, what a Coach Repair re-stitching campaign looks like under UV vs a counterfeit re-glue. As written, a user buying a $30 UV flashlight will not know what they are seeing. Either add an example image set (under-UV authentic creed, under-UV counterfeit creed) or downgrade the recommendation to optional. **Rationale: Photos-needed / Authentication.**

16. **"What this bag is worth" is missing — and the market.md page actively refuses to answer.**
    `market.md` states "The archive does not publish snapshot prices." For a collector site, this is a defensible editorial choice but a major utility hit. The compromise: per-model "typical sold range" tables with date-stamped data sources (eBay sold listings export, Etsy completed sales) refreshed quarterly. Without any pricing context, a Willis owner cannot tell if they have a $90 brown Willis or a $300 red Willis (and red Willis is flagged as rare on the model page itself). Even rough buckets — "common," "rare," "high-demand" — applied to each model entry would raise the practical value substantially. **Rationale: Resale.**

17. **The hangtag page does not show the silhouette evolution it describes.**
    "Squarer rectangular tag in the earliest creed-era bags toward a rounder, softer-cornered 'beanbag' silhouette in later production" — that is exactly what an image grid solves and prose cannot. Same with the three attachment systems (brass clip, leather strap, plastic-coated wire). A user who has never held a brass-clip hangtag cannot identify one from this description. Hangtag silhouette is also one of the easiest "instant decade" reads for a quick triage. **Rationale: Photos-needed.**

18. **The wizard's "no-creed era" verdict pattern incorrectly treats E2c (early creed, no serial) as the same path.**
    Step 1 Option 4 is "There's a creed but no serial" with sub-text "Pre-1980 bags, small accessories, and some 1990s pebbled-leather models legitimately shipped without serials" — and routes to the wordmark step. But the early-1970s creed-no-serial path (E2c) is a meaningfully different authentication exercise than a 1990s pebbled SLG without serial. The route conflates them. Add a follow-up disambiguator: "Is the creed glove-tanned cowhide and visibly weathered, or is it a smaller printed/embossed creed on pebbled leather?" Saves the user from a non-sequitur era-consistency verdict. **Rationale: Wizard-flow.**

19. **The Coach Repair / Jacksonville July 1999 caveat is repeated across every authentication page — and it deserves its own page.**
    Same paragraph appears in `decision-tree.md`, `hardware.md`, `lining.md`, `zippers.md`, `hangtags.md`, `chinese-fakes.md`, `frankenbags.md`, `korean-repros.md`, `counterfeits/index.md`. It's load-bearing — it underwrites the "single flag = ambiguous, two flags = fake" logic — but it's never explained in depth. Promote it to `/authentication/coach-repair/` with: timeline (pre-1999 distributed repair, July 1999 Jacksonville consolidation), what they replaced (creeds, hardware, hangtags, zippers), what they did NOT replace (linings, body leather), how to recognize a Coach Repair bag (era-appropriate replacement components, often retained correspondence/receipts in the 1990s-2000s repair stream). Then have every other page link out instead of duplicating. **Rationale: Authentication / Utility.**

20. **The counterfeits/index.md "canonical photo set" requirement is buried — promote it to a print-friendly checklist.**
    "Full bag (front, back, both sides); creed close-up with a ruler in frame; turnlock front and back; zipper pull and tape stamp; lining and any interior tags; country-of-origin stamp; hangtag both sides; base and feet." This is the single most useful piece of resale-side guidance on the entire site — every PurseForum authenticator wants exactly these photos before they will commit. Surface it as a one-page printable checklist linked from the wizard's verdict page (currently links to the decision tree which has the same problem). Sellers asking sellers for "the canonical Coach photo set" raises the floor on every transaction. **Rationale: Utility / Resale.**

21. **No coverage of color-specific desirability and palette swaps — a known fake pattern.**
    `colors/` exists with 21 entries, which is good infrastructure. But individual color desirability (Hunter Green Willis vs Black Willis) is missing, and there's no callout that bag bodies are sometimes recolored from common to rare colorways (Frankenbag pattern: dye-stripped Black to "Hunter Green"). The Frankenbags page mentions recoloring abstractly but nothing on the model or color pages cross-references which color combinations are red flags ("Red Willis" is rare per Willis page itself — meaning a Red Willis at common-color price is suspicious). Tie color rarity to authentication: rare-color bags should clear a higher evidentiary bar. **Rationale: Authentication / Resale.**
