# Madison Satchel

## Lead — primary-confirmed Spring 1986 launch

**The Madison Satchel debuted in the Spring 1986 catalog at Style
9725, $104** — flagged "NEW" on the "Additions to Our Collection"
page primary [coach-catalogs-1960-1989]. Earlier archive framing
of the Madison Satchel as a 1990s+ Madison-Collection-line model
was wrong: the silhouette is a **late-NYC-era Classic Collection**
bag from 1986 onward, ~5 years before the separate Madison
Collection sub-line launched.

A larger variant (**Madison Satchel Large, Style 9765**) appears in
1986 as well; by Spring 1989 (per M2) both 9725 ($140) and 9765
($168) are in the Classics roster.

The Madison Satchel should NOT be confused with:
- The **Madison Avenue flagship store** (a retail location)
- The **Madison Collection** (a 1990s+ pebble-textured leather
  sub-line, distinct sub-collection)
- The **Madison Royalton** (a separate 1990s bag)

The "Madison" name was reused across multiple Coach products. The
chief source of confusion: **the Madison Satchel (1986+ single
model) and the Madison Collection (~1991+ sub-collection) share
only the place-name.** The Madison Satchel is in the standard
9XXX Classics numbering; the Madison Collection uses the 4XXX
sub-collection numbering.

## Description

A structured satchel in glove-tanned cowhide with solid-brass hardware,
consistent with Coach's pre-Krakoff construction standards. Style 9765
is documented as a Madison Satchel in the Yourgreatfinds collector
compilation [yourgreatfinds-collectors-guide]; specific dimensions,
closure mechanism, strap configuration, and color range are not
anchored in a primary catalog scan in the current research and are
deferred until one is located.

## Madison name reuse

"Madison" is one of the most overloaded names in vintage Coach
research. At least four distinct referents share the word and are
routinely conflated:

- **Madison Satchel (style 9765)** — this entry.
  [yourgreatfinds-collectors-guide]
- **Madison Royalton** — a separate 1990s bag listed in the same
  collector source as part of the Madison family. Different silhouette,
  different style number (not yet recorded), same line label.
  [yourgreatfinds-collectors-guide]
- **Madison line / Madison collection** — a named product line that
  ran in the early 1990s and included exotic-skin variants. Per the
  authentication research, the Madison and Gramercy lines used **Italian
  production** for exotic-skin examples — meaning a "Made in Italy"
  Madison-line bag is consistent with the line's documented
  manufacturing geography rather than a fake tell on its face.
- **Madison Avenue flagship** — Coach's New York flagship store on
  Madison Avenue, reopened 1987. A retail location, not a bag.

Treat any "Madison" attribution as ambiguous until the specific
referent (which bag, which line, or the store) is established.

## Authentication

Specific to a candidate Madison Satchel:

- **Style number 9765 in the creed** verifies the Madison Satchel
  silhouette specifically — not the broader Madison line or a Madison
  Royalton. Style number alone does not date a bag; cross-check creed
  wording and serial format per `research/E-authentication.md`.
- **"Made in Italy" stamp** on a Madison-named bag is consistent with
  the documented Italian production of Madison-line exotic-skin
  variants and is **not** automatically a fake tell. It does, however,
  shift the bag from "Madison Satchel 9765" toward "Madison-line exotic"
  — verify the style number rather than relying on the line label.
- **Pre-1994 vs post-1994 creed.** "COACH LEATHERWARE" in small caps
  beneath "COACH" places the bag pre-1994. Madison-line production
  spans the early 1990s, so both pre- and post-1994 creeds are
  plausible depending on the specific example.
- **Solid brass hardware**, non-magnetic. Standard NYC-era / early-1990s
  Coach construction applies; see Willis or Court entries for the
  general checks.
- **Designer attribution.** Madison-line bags are **not** Bonnie Cashin
  designs. Cashin's tenure ended in 1974; the Madison line is in-house
  early-1990s work. Any listing attributing a Madison Satchel to Cashin
  is wrong on its face.

Confidence on this entry overall is **tentative**: the style-number
attribution comes from a single Tier 3 collector compilation, and
neither dimensions, introduction year, nor color range are anchored to
a primary catalog or dated bag. Promotion to `likely` requires a second
independent source — ideally a dated catalog scan showing 9765 as the
Madison Satchel.
