## Lead

The Sutton (style 9814) is a Classic NYC-era Coach zip-closure bag with
two exterior pockets and an interior zippered pocket. It sits in the
same in-house, glove-tanned-cowhide tradition as the Court and Willis,
but distinguished by its brass zipper main closure rather than a
turnlock flap.

## Description

Per D1: "Two exterior pockets with brass zipper interior closure and
interior zippered pocket." The bag carries solid-brass hardware
throughout, consistent with NYC-era construction.

## Notable variant

A **hunter green leather with British tan trim** Sutton is documented
in collector references — a distinctive Coach two-tone combination
that pairs the deep saturated green of the hunter palette against the
warm reddish-brown British tan on edges and trim. Two-tone Coach bags
are uncommon relative to single-color examples, and the hunter/British
tan pairing is among the more sought-after Classic NYC-era color
combinations.

## Authentication

Tells specific to a vintage Sutton (cross-reference the era-keyed
matrix in `authentication/` for full feature lists):

- **Style number 9814.** Visible on the leather creed inside the bag
  on numbered-creed examples. A style number alone does not date a
  specific bag — Coach reused style numbers across production years.
- **Brass zipper.** Era-appropriate Sutton examples should carry a
  **YKK or Talon** zipper with a solid-brass pull. YKK dominated from
  the late 1970s onward; Talon appears on earlier NYC-era pieces. A
  zipper maker that postdates the creed-format era is suspect.
- **Era-appropriate creed.** A pre-1994 Sutton should read "COACH
  LEATHERWARE" with "LEATHERWARE" stamped in small letters beneath
  "COACH," typically with "Made in United States" or "Made in New
  York City, U.S.A." on earlier examples (E1, E2). This is the single
  most cited textual anchor for dating.
- **Hardware.** Solid brass, non-magnetic. A magnet that sticks
  indicates plated steel and is a strong fake or post-vintage signal.
