## Lead

The Convertible Clutch is **likely** one of the four early-1960s
Coach silhouettes Bonnie Cashin designed for the Cahns'
Gail-Leather-era Coach division. Vintage Fashion Guild lists Cashin's
classic Coach designs of that period as "the shopping bag tote, the
bucket bag, shoulder bag and a clutch-style purse with a removable
shoulder strap" [vintagefashionguild-cashin]; the fourth item — the
clutch-style purse with a removable shoulder strap — maps directly to
this model. That gives the Convertible Clutch a stronger Cashin
attribution than most bags in the working canon: Tier 2 reference
naming plus the multi-source agreement that the silhouette dates to
the early-1960s Cashin period [vintagefashionguild-cashin,
yourgreatfinds-collectors-guide].

The bag is convertible between two carries — clutch (strap removed)
and shoulder (strap attached) — which is the function the name
describes [vintagefashionguild-cashin]. A separate dealer compilation
[yourgreatfinds-collectors-guide] also references the model under the
Cashin-era umbrella, sometimes alongside or as "Slimline."

## History

Per Vintage Fashion Guild's Cashin label entry, "her classic designs
for Gail's Coach division during the early 1960s included the shopping
bag tote, the bucket bag, shoulder bag and a clutch-style purse with a
removable shoulder strap" [vintagefashionguild-cashin]. This is the
four-design summary of Cashin's foundational early-1960s output for
Coach, and it is the strongest free-source anchor for designer
attribution on a non-Cashin-Carry silhouette in the archive.

Cashin was hired by Miles and Lillian Cahn in 1962 and her Coach
contract ran through 1974 [wwd-coach-early-years]. The clutch-style
purse with a removable shoulder strap therefore originates in the
1962–early-1960s window. A dated catalog page would resolve the
introduction year precisely; that remains an open verification item
against the UCLA Cashin papers [ucla-cashin-papers].

## Description

The Convertible Clutch is a clutch-format bag with a removable
shoulder strap, allowing two distinct carries — hand-held clutch with
the strap detached, and shoulder-carry with the strap attached
[vintagefashionguild-cashin]. Cashin-era visual signatures expected on
an early-1960s example, by analogy with the Cashin Carry tote of the
same period:

- **Solid brass hardware**, including the post-1964 brass turnlock
  Cashin introduced to Coach [vintagefashionguild-cashin]. Pre-1964
  examples (if they exist for this silhouette) would predate the
  turnlock and could carry kiss-lock or other Cashin-era closures.
- **Glove-tanned cowhide** body, drum-dyed through.
- **Striped fabric lining**, a Cashin signature on Coach bags of the
  period.

Specific dimensions and a primary catalog match are open verification
items.

## Authentication (Cashin-era Convertible Clutch)

Authenticate against the Cashin-era profile (E2):

- **No numbered creed.** A leather creed patch with style and serial
  number is a feature of the post-Cashin NYC era. Early-1960s
  Cashin-era pieces carry either a small interior metal tag or no
  interior label. A Convertible Clutch with a numbered creed is a
  later production run, not a 1962–1974 original.
- **No serial number.** Pre-numbered-creed; absence is not a fake
  tell for this era.
- **Solid brass hardware**, riveted through. Non-magnetic and heavy
  for size; a magnet that sticks indicates plated steel and a much
  later piece. Post-1964 examples carry the brass turnlock
  [vintagefashionguild-cashin]; pre-1964 examples (if confirmed) would
  carry an earlier closure.
- **Glove-tanned cowhide**, cut edges showing the same color as the
  surface. Painted edges are not period-correct for a Cashin-era bag.
- **Striped fabric lining** consistent with Cashin's Coach output.
- **Removable shoulder strap.** A clutch-style bag of this name with
  a permanently sewn-in strap, or with no strap fittings at all, is
  inconsistent with the documented description.
- **No "Made in [country]" stamp.** Country-of-origin stamps in the
  creed are an NYC-era feature.

The era cue that resolves most authentication questions on this model
is the same as for the Cashin Carry: a numbered creed places the bag
firmly **after** the Cashin period regardless of model name on the
listing.

## Designer attribution

The Convertible Clutch is one of the **few** Coach bags besides the
Cashin Carry tote whose Cashin attribution rests on a Tier 2 reference
naming the silhouette as Cashin's. Vintage Fashion Guild's Cashin
label entry lists the "clutch-style purse with a removable shoulder
strap" as one of her four classic early-1960s Coach designs
[vintagefashionguild-cashin]. That mapping to this model is the basis
for `designer_confidence: likely`.

The attribution is `likely` rather than `confirmed` because:

1. The mapping from "clutch-style purse with a removable shoulder
   strap" (Vintage Fashion Guild's description) to "Convertible
   Clutch" (the dealer-compiled name) is interpretive — it depends on
   the silhouette match, not a name match. A primary Cashin-papers
   illustration [ucla-cashin-papers] or a 1962–1974 catalog page
   would confirm.
2. Coach reused silhouettes and names across decades; later
   "convertible clutch" production runs may exist that are not
   Cashin's design.

The archive treats this as one of the strongest non-Cashin-Carry
attributions available from free online sources, but stops short of
`confirmed` until a primary citation (UCLA papers or dated catalog) is
matched. See `research/C-cashin.md` §4–5 for attribution discipline.
