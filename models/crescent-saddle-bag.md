## Lead

**The Crescent (Style 9235) is a Classic NYC-era Coach silhouette
documented in catalog through Spring 1984 at $100, then
discontinued** [coach-catalogs-1960-1989]. Per research stream N3,
the Crescent appears in the Spring 1984 catalog but is **absent
from the Fall-Winter 1984 catalog and from all 1985 catalogs (per
M1)**. The Crescent is therefore one of the documented Cahn-era
discontinuations between Spring 1984 and Fall-Winter 1984 — among
the last silhouettes cut before the July 1985 Sara Lee acquisition.

The "Saddle Bag" framing in the name reflects the saddle silhouette;
catalog text simply says "Crescent" (not "Crescent Saddle Bag") in
the 1984 Spring catalog primary-confirmed appearance.

## Description

Per D1 (`yourgreatfinds-collectors-guide`): black full-grain leather
with brass hardware, dimensions 10.25" × 9.5" × 2.875", a large
interior pocket, and an adjustable strap.

## Authentication

Era diagnostics applicable to a Classic NYC-era Crescent Saddle Bag
(cross-reference `authentication/` for the full era-keyed matrix):

- **Style number TBD.** Not yet documented in the available
  literature; per-bag verification depends on the creed and a
  catalog match.
- **Creed wording (E1 diagnostic).** A pre-1994 example should read
  "COACH LEATHERWARE" with "LEATHERWARE" stamped in small letters
  beneath "COACH," typically with "Made in United States" or "Made
  in New York City, U.S.A." on earlier examples
  [coachbagrehab-dating].
- **Serial format.** Pre-1994 Classic NYC-era bags carry a digit-only
  unique serial (`xxx-xxxx` 1970s, `xxxx-xxx` 1980s, per
  `coachbagrehab-dating`, `tentative`).
- **Hardware.** Solid brass, non-magnetic. A magnet that sticks to
  the hardware indicates plated steel and is a strong fake or
  post-vintage signal.
- **Construction.** Glove-tanned cowhide with raw or burnished
  edges. Painted glossy edges on a bag claiming 1980s manufacture is
  a strong reproduction signal.
- **Designer.** Attribution to any specific designer is `tentative`;
  Classic NYC-era bags were in-house staff designs and any
  attribution to Bonnie Cashin (whose contract ended 1974) is
  folklore.
