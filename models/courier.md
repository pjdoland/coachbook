## Lead

The Courier is a rare 1970s-only Coach silhouette, "made exclusively
during the 1970s in NYC factories" per the single Tier 3 collector
compilation that documents it [yourgreatfinds-collectors-guide].
Confidence on this entry is `tentative` — only one source names the
model.

## Description

The Courier was produced in two sizes: a smaller "baby Courier" and a
larger standard variant [yourgreatfinds-collectors-guide]. Hardware is
solid brass, consistent with 1970s NYC construction. A rare
**orange-rust** colorway is documented [yourgreatfinds-collectors-guide];
other 1970s NYC palette colors are plausible by analogy but are not
specifically attested for the Courier and are not listed here.

Style number, dimensions, and closure type are not recorded in the
research files currently consulted and are open verification items.

## Resale market

The collector compilation notes that the Courier "typically sells for
over $200" [yourgreatfinds-collectors-guide]. This is a snapshot
observation, not an authoritative market figure — resale prices change,
the source is a single Tier 3 dealer compilation, and the archive does
not editorialize about resale value generally (`STYLE.md` §1). Treat
the figure as one dated data point.

## Designer attribution

The 1970s span both the tail of Bonnie Cashin's tenure (which ended in
**1974**) and the early Classic NYC in-house era. Without a dated
catalog or a primary archive cite, attribution to Cashin vs. an
in-house designer cannot be settled. `designer_confidence: tentative`.
A Courier introduced 1970–1974 could plausibly be a Cashin design; one
introduced 1975–1979 is not, regardless of community claims
(`STYLE.md` §4). The default position is `tentative` until a primary
source — UCLA Cashin papers (`ucla-cashin-papers`) or a dated 1970s
Coach catalog — anchors the introduction year.

## Authentication

Authentication tells for a Courier follow the 1970s NYC diagnostic set.
Cross-reference `research/E-authentication.md` and the era-keyed matrix
in `authentication/` for full feature lists.

- **Country of origin.** "Made exclusively during the 1970s in NYC
  factories" [yourgreatfinds-collectors-guide] means a Courier with a
  non-NYC origin tag — "Made in Costa Rica," "Made in Hungary," "Made
  in China," or any post-1980 US-factory wording — would indicate a
  fake, a misattributed model, or a re-issue rather than an original
  Courier.
- **Creed era.** A 1970s NYC bag falls in the early-creed window:
  "MADE IN NEW YORK CITY, U.S.A." wording appears on the earliest
  numbered creeds; some late-1960s/early-1970s examples carry a leather
  stamp inside with no creed patch and no serial at all
  [coachbagrehab-dating]. Absence of a creed or serial is **not** by
  itself a fake tell for this era.
- **Serial format.** Pre-1994 examples carry a digit-only unique
  serial; the 1970s format is reported as `xxx-xxxx`, distinct from
  the 1980s `xxxx-xxx` (Tier 3, `tentative`)
  [coachbagrehab-dating]. The post-1994 letter-digit-letter style code
  format does not appear on a genuine Courier.
- **Hardware.** Solid brass, non-magnetic. A magnet that sticks
  indicates plated steel and points to a fake or post-vintage piece.
  Cashin-era 1970s hardware is often unstamped; absence of a "COACH"
  stamp on the turnlock is not a fake tell on a 1970s bag
  [coachbagrehab-dating].
- **Construction.** Glove-tanned cowhide with raw or burnished edges.
  Painted glossy edges on a bag claiming 1970s manufacture is a strong
  reproduction signal.
- **Lining.** Unlined or minimally lined is correct for a 1970s NYC
  Coach bag; absence of lining is not a fake tell. Heat-cut polyester
  lining is a fake tell.
