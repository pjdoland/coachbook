# Bonnie Tote

The Bonnie Tote is a Coach tote silhouette named for Bonnie Cashin
[yourgreatfinds-collectors-guide, vintagecoachie]. The naming suggests
Cashin authorship but does not establish it. Designer attribution and
introduction era are both `tentative` pending a primary source — a
1962–1974 Coach catalog page or a UCLA Cashin papers
[ucla-cashin-papers] reference attributing the tote to her.

## Description

Specific dimensions, body shape, lining, strap configuration, and the
tote's relationship (if any) to the Cashin Carry, Skinny Tote, or
Embassy Tote silhouettes are not pinned to a primary catalog source
in the archive's working references. The bag is named in the
dealer-compiled record [yourgreatfinds-collectors-guide] and tracked
by the vintagecoachie style guide [vintagecoachie] without dated
introduction data.

## Naming versus authorship

The "Bonnie" name is a tribute to Cashin. Tribute naming is not
authorship. Coach has named bags after non-designers in the same
catalog tradition — the Legacy series alone includes Patricia's
Legacy, Lula's Legacy, and Rambler's Legacy (`research/D-models.md`),
none of which document the namesake as the designer.

Cashin's contracted tenure at Coach ran 1962–1974
(`research/C-cashin.md` §3, citing
[wwd-coach-early-years]). A bag named "Bonnie" could have been:

- introduced **during** her tenure as her own design (attribution
  plausible);
- introduced **during** her tenure as an in-house design named for
  her (tribute, not authorship);
- introduced **after 1974** as a posthumous-style tribute by Coach's
  in-house team (definitively not Cashin's design, regardless of the
  name).

Without a dated catalog page or a UCLA Cashin papers cite, the
archive cannot distinguish among these. The designer attribution
holds at `tentative`, per the discipline laid out in `STYLE.md` §4
and `research/C-cashin.md` §5.

## Authentication

Authentication of a Bonnie Tote depends on era diagnostics read off
the bag itself — and those diagnostics establish era of *production*,
not era of *introduction*.

- **Pre-creed era markers (E2).** A small interior metal tag only,
  or a leather stamp inside with no creed patch and no serial,
  is consistent with a Cashin-era introduction date.
- **Numbered creed with `xxx-xxxx` digit-only serial.** Places the
  bag in the mid-1970s or later (E3+) per
  [coachbagrehab-dating]. A Bonnie Tote with this creed could have
  been introduced post-1974 — in which case the Cashin attribution
  fails on its face — or could be a continuation of a pre-1974
  silhouette, which the format alone does not distinguish.
- **Solid brass hardware — magnet test.** Brass is non-magnetic.
  Hardware that attracts a magnet indicates plated steel, ruling
  out a Cashin-era bag.
- **Glove-tanned cowhide, raw or burnished edges.** Painted glossy
  edges on a "Cashin-era Bonnie Tote" are a strong fake or
  reproduction signal.

None of these resolve authorship. They establish whether a given
example is consistent with E2 or E3 production.

2. **UCLA Cashin papers** [ucla-cashin-papers] reference

3. **Stephanie Lake's *Bonnie Cashin: Chic Is Where You Find It***

   Bonnie Tote.
4. **Style number and dimensions.**

Until at least one of items 1–3 lands, this entry stays at
