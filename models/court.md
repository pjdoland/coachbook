## Lead

The Court is a coveted Classic NYC-era Coach in the same shoulder-bag
family as the Willis: a structured, glove-tanned cowhide flap bag
identified by style number 9870. Alongside Willis, it is one of the
most-recognized silhouettes from Coach Leatherware's pre-1994 US-made
period and is a staple of vintage-Coach collecting today.

Hardware is solid brass throughout — turnlock, strap fittings, and
rivets — typical of NYC-era construction. The silhouette is squarer
and more architectural than the rounded Plaza variant.

## History

**Introduced Fall 1989** as part of the Coach **Continental Series**
launch [coach-catalogs-1960-1989]. Style 9870. The Continental Series
debuted with four styles: Plaza Bag (9860), Plaza Bag Large (9865),
**Court Bag (9870)**, and Regency Brief (5255). The Court was later
reabsorbed into the Classic Collection branding by Holiday 1991 — when
it appears in the Classic Collection pages without explicit Continental
Series framing. This is a documented Coach pattern: launch a named
sub-series, then merge surviving styles into the perennial Classics.

The Court is the parent silhouette for two related models documented
in research stream D: the **Plaza**, a rounded version of the Court,
and the **Bella**, a smaller variant of the Plaza. Both are noted as
harder to find than the Court itself.

Earlier archive framing as a "tentative" introduction date with
Holiday 1991 as the earliest primary citation is superseded by the
Fall 1989 Continental Series launch evidence
[coach-catalogs-1960-1989].

**Designer attribution.** The Court is **not** a Bonnie Cashin design.
Cashin's tenure ended in 1974, and the Court — like Willis — is a
Classic NYC-era staff/in-house design. Community attributions of any
post-1974 Coach silhouette to Cashin are folklore and should be
treated as such.

## Authentication

Tells specific to a vintage Court (cross-reference the era-keyed
matrix in `authentication/` for full feature lists):

- **Style number 9870.** Visible on the leather creed inside the bag
  on numbered-creed examples. A style number alone does not date a
  specific bag — Coach reused style numbers across multiple
  production years.
- **Creed wording (E3–E4 diagnostic).** A pre-1994 Court should read
  "COACH LEATHERWARE" with "LEATHERWARE" stamped in small letters
  beneath "COACH," typically with "Made in United States" or "Made in
  New York City, U.S.A." on earlier examples (E1, E2). This is the
  single most cited textual anchor for dating.
- **Serial format.** Pre-1994 Court bags carry a digit-only unique
  serial (formats like `xxx-xxxx` in the 1970s and `xxxx-xxx` in the
  1980s, per E3 — `tentative`). Post-1994 Court production (if any
  carried the style number forward) would use the
  `[A-M][year-digit][factory-letter]-9870` pattern; note the
  documented single-digit-year ambiguity (e.g., a "6" resolves to
  either 1996 or 2006 without contextual cross-checks).
- **Hardware.** Solid brass turnlock, non-magnetic. A magnet that
  sticks to the turnlock indicates plated steel and is a strong fake
  or post-vintage signal.
- **Lining.** Many NYC-era Court examples are unlined — an unlined
  Court is **not** a fake tell.
- **Construction.** Glove-tanned cowhide with raw or burnished edges.
  Painted/glossy edges on a bag claiming 1980s manufacture is a
  strong reproduction signal.
- **Designer.** Any seller listing a Court as "designed by Bonnie
  Cashin" is wrong; treat that listing's other claims with skepticism.

## Variants & related

- **Plaza** — described in research stream D as the rounded version
  of the Court. Style number not yet confirmed; noted in collector
  references as harder to find than the Court itself.
- **Bella** — a smaller variant of the Plaza per D1. Also `tentative`
  on style number and dating; even less commonly encountered than the
  Plaza.
- **Willis (9927)** — same Classic NYC-era family of structured
  flap-front shoulder bags; the most common point of comparison for
  the Court silhouette.

All three (Plaza, Bella, related Willis-family bags) deserve their
own entries once a catalog scan confirms style numbers and
introduction dates.
