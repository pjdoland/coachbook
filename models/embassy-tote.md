# Embassy Tote

The Embassy Tote — also called the "Legal Tote" or "Legal/Embassy Tote" — is
a 1970s NYC-produced large briefcase-style tote in glove-tanned cowhide
with solid brass hardware [yourgreatfinds-collectors-guide].

## Description

Yourgreatfinds documents the Embassy Tote as a 1970s briefcase-scale tote
measuring **15.75" L × 10.5" H × 3.5" D**, executed in **full-grain
leather** with **brass hardware** and produced in the United States
[yourgreatfinds-collectors-guide]. The only color recorded in the source
is **British Tan**. Dimensions, materials, and color are all `tentative`
pending corroboration from a dated catalog scan or measured-bag evidence.

## Designer attribution

The 1970s span both Bonnie Cashin's Coach tenure (1962–1974) and the
post-Cashin in-house design period (1975 onward). If the Embassy Tote was
introduced before 1974, it could be a Cashin-era design; if introduced
1975 or later, it is not [wwd-coach-early-years, vintagecoachie]. The
single Tier 3 source documenting the model gives only "1970s" with no
further pinning [yourgreatfinds-collectors-guide]. Until a primary-source
catalog page or UCLA Cashin papers entry locates this silhouette, the
designer is `tentative`.

The community frequently misattributes 1970s Coach bags to Cashin on
silhouette alone. Per `research/C-cashin.md` §5, that attribution requires
a dated 1962–1974 catalog or a Cashin-papers cite — neither has been
located for the Embassy Tote.

## Authentication

A 1970s NYC-produced Coach tote sits in a transitional authentication
window. Per `research/E-authentication.md` §1, two creed configurations
are plausible depending on the year of manufacture:

- **Late 1960s–early 1970s**: leather stamp inside, no creed patch and
  no serial number [coachbagrehab-dating].
- **Mid-1970s onward**: numbered creed reading "COACH LEATHERWARE / Made
  in United States," with the small-caps "LEATHERWARE" beneath "COACH"
  as the textual anchor for pre-1994 manufacture, and a digit-only
  serial in the `xxx-xxxx` 1970s format [coachbagrehab-dating,
  vintagecoachie].

Either configuration is consistent with USA production; the country line
should read "Made in United States" or "Made in New York City, U.S.A."
on the earliest numbered creeds [coachbagrehab-dating]. Hardware is solid
brass (non-magnetic) — a magnet that sticks indicates plated steel and a
fake or post-vintage piece. Edges should be raw or burnished, not painted;
glove-tanned cowhide construction; lining unlined or minimal is correct
for the era and is not a fake tell [coachbagrehab-dating,
yourgreatfinds-collectors-guide].

A "Made in Costa Rica," "China," or other offshore stamp on a bag
presented as a 1970s Embassy Tote is inconsistent with the era — offshore
production began circa 1994 [coachbagrehab-dating, purseforum-costa-rica].
