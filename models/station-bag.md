## Lead

The Station Bag is a Classic NYC-era flap silhouette catalogued under
style number **5130** in the collector literature [yourgreatfinds-collectors-guide].
It is sometimes confused with the Willis (style 9927) in resale listings,
where sellers will describe a Willis as a "Station Bag" or vice versa —
the two are distinct models with distinct style numbers, and the style
number on the creed is the disambiguator.

## Description

What is documented: a Classic NYC-era flap bag in glove-tanned cowhide
with solid brass hardware, identified by style 5130 in D1's
collector compilation [yourgreatfinds-collectors-guide]. Dimensions,
strap configuration, and color range are not yet documented in any
free online source located. This entry stays tight on purpose — the
Station Bag has substantially less public data than its better-
documented NYC-era siblings (Willis, Court).

## Naming-overlap note

A nontrivial fraction of Tier 3 / Tier 4 listings describe the
**Willis (9927)** as a "Station Bag." The two models share a general
flap-bag family resemblance and the misnomer travels quickly in resale
copy. **Always check the style number on the creed:**

- `5130` → Station Bag (this entry)
- `9927` → Willis (see `models/willis.md`)

Any listing that uses "Station Bag" without showing the creed style
number should be treated as ambiguous until the number is verified.
Do not infer the model from silhouette photos alone; flap-bag
silhouettes recur across the Classic NYC catalog (Willis, Court,
Murphy, and others), and the visual distinction between Willis and
Station Bag is not strong enough in compressed listing photography to
override a style-number mismatch.

## History

Introduced during the Classic NYC era (mid-1970s through 1994). A
specific introduction year is not yet anchored to a primary catalog
and remains `tentative`. Designer attribution is in-house staff —
Bonnie Cashin's tenure ended in **1974**, and any post-1974 Coach
silhouette is by definition not a Cashin design (see STYLE.md §4.2
on Cashin folklore).

## Authentication

Tells specific to a Station Bag (cross-reference the era-keyed
matrix in `research/E-authentication.md` for full feature lists):

- **Style number 5130 in the creed.** This is the single most
  important disambiguator from Willis. A bag listed as a "Station
  Bag" whose creed reads `9927` is a Willis, not a Station Bag.
- **Creed wording.** A pre-1994 Station Bag should read
  "COACH LEATHERWARE" with "LEATHERWARE" in small letters beneath
  "COACH," typically with "Made in United States" or "Made in New
  York City, U.S.A." on earlier examples [coachbagrehab-dating,
  vintagecoachie]. Presence of the small-caps "LEATHERWARE" is the
  single most cited textual anchor for pre-1994 manufacture.
- **Serial format.** Pre-1994 examples carry a digit-only unique
  serial (`xxx-xxxx` in the 1970s, `xxxx-xxx` in the 1980s, per
  [coachbagrehab-dating], `tentative`). Post-1994 production (if any
  carried 5130 forward) would use the
  `[A-M][year-digit][factory-letter]-5130` pattern. The single-
  digit-year ambiguity applies here as elsewhere — a "6" resolves
  to either 1996 or 2006 without contextual cross-checks.
- **Hardware.** Solid brass, non-magnetic. A magnet that sticks to
  the turnlock is a strong fake or post-vintage-plated-steel signal.
- **Construction.** Glove-tanned cowhide with raw or burnished
  edges. Painted glossy edges on a bag claiming 1980s manufacture
  are a strong reproduction signal [coachbagrehab-dating].
- **Lining.** Unlined or minimally lined is correct for the NYC
  era; an unlined Station Bag is not a fake tell.
- **Designer.** Any seller listing a Station Bag as "designed by
  Bonnie Cashin" is wrong; treat that listing's other claims with
  skepticism.
