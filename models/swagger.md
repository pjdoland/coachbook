# Swagger

The Swagger is a Coach silhouette grouped with the Cashin-era
small-and-mid-bag canon by community references
[yourgreatfinds-collectors-guide, vintagecoachie]. The Bonnie Cashin
attribution is **tentative**: the bag appears in dealer and
collector compilations alongside other Cashin-era silhouettes, but
no primary source — a 1962–1974 Coach catalog page or a UCLA Cashin
papers entry — has been matched to it in the archive's working
references. Until one lands, both the era and the designer
attribution hold at `tentative`.

## Description

Available data is minimal. The Swagger is named in the dealer-
compiled record [yourgreatfinds-collectors-guide] and grouped under
Cashin-attributed silhouettes in the model canon (`research/D-models.md`
§"Bonnie Cashin era"). Dimensions, body shape, closure, lining,
strap configuration, and style number are not pinned to a primary
catalog source in the archive's working references and will be
expanded once a dated catalog page is matched to the bag.

## Designer attribution — community-only

This entry sits in the same high-folklore zone as the Dinky,
Mailbox, and Musette (see `STYLE.md` §4 and `research/C-cashin.md`
§5). The discipline is identical:

**Cashin's contracted tenure at Coach was 1962–1974, full stop**
[wwd-coach-early-years]. She was a contracted designer, not a
Coach employee; the contract ended in 1974. Bags introduced from
1975 onward — even those that use her vocabulary — are by
definition not Cashin designs.

The Swagger's introduction year is **not pinned** in the working
references. The Cashin-attribution table in `research/C-cashin.md`
§4 marks it `tentative` ("community attribution"), and the model
canon in `research/D-models.md` carries the same `tentative` tag.
That is the ceiling the evidence currently supports. If the
Swagger was introduced 1975 or later, the Cashin attribution is
wrong on its face regardless of how widely it is repeated.

## Authentication

Authentication of a Swagger claimed to be Cashin-era depends on
reading era diagnostics off the bag itself, then asking whether
those diagnostics are consistent with a pre-1974 introduction.

- **Pre-creed era diagnostics align with E2 production.** Per
  `research/E-authentication.md` §1 and §8, early-1960s (E2a) bags
  carry a small interior metal tag only; late-1960s / early-1970s
  bags carry a leather stamp inside with no creed patch and no
  serial. A Swagger with no creed and either of these markers is
  consistent with a Cashin-era introduction date.
- **A numbered creed places the bag in the mid-1970s or later.**
  The numbered creed with the 1970s `xxx-xxxx` digit-only serial
  format was in use from roughly the mid-1970s onward. A numbered
  creed alone does not resolve the attribution timing.
- **Solid brass hardware — magnet test.** Brass is non-magnetic
  (`research/E-authentication.md` §4). A magnet that sticks to any
  hardware indicates plated steel, which is post-vintage and rules
  out a Cashin-era bag.
- **Glove-tanned cowhide, raw or burnished edges.** Painted glossy
  edges on a "Cashin-era Swagger" are a strong fake or reproduction
  signal — Coach NYC-era construction does not paint edges.

None of these diagnostics by themselves resolve the authorship
question. They establish that a given Swagger is consistent with
E2 production; they do not tell you whether the silhouette was
*introduced* during Cashin's tenure.

## Open verification items

1. **Dated Coach catalog page** showing the Swagger in production,
   ideally from the 1962–1974 window, with style number and
   dimensions.
2. **UCLA Cashin papers** [ucla-cashin-papers] reference
   attributing the Swagger to Cashin — design illustrations,
   contracts referencing it by name, or correspondence about its
   development.
3. **Style number and dimensions** for the Swagger.
4. **Introduction year** — the single fact that would move the
   entry off `tentative`.

Until at least one of items 1–2 lands, this entry stays at
`confidence: tentative` and the designer attribution at
`tentative`.
