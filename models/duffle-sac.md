# Duffle Sac

The Duffle Sac is one of Coach's longest-running silhouettes, in
production from the late 1960s through the early 2000s
[yourgreatfinds-collectors-guide]. Its longevity makes it a useful
diagnostic anchor for the archive: the same style number — **9085** —
spans the pre-creed era through the numbered-creed era and into the
letter-number style-code era. A single model, three diagnostic eras.

## Description

The Duffle Sac is a large soft duffel-silhouette shoulder bag in
glove-tanned cowhide. Yourgreatfinds nicknames it the "Mary Poppins
bag" for its capacity [yourgreatfinds-collectors-guide].

### Primary catalog data

**Fall 1975 catalog** (Style 9085, dimensions **9"×14"×9"**) — earliest
primary-confirmed appearance. Standard finish style 9085; **Ruff-Out
suede variant style 9088** also documented. The pairing of standard
+ Ruff-Out (suede) variants under separate style numbers (9085/9088)
is a 1970s Coach convention [coach-catalogs-1960-1989].

**Holiday 1991 catalog** (Style 9085, $210, dimensions **10½"×13"×9"**):
> "D. DUFFLE SAC  No. 9085  $210  Top zipper closure. Inside open
> pocket. 36" Adjustable extra-wide shoulder strap. 10½" × 13" × 9"
> Black, British Tan, Forest Green, Mahogany, Navy, Red. Pictured
> in Mahogany." [coach-catalogs-1960-1989]

| Spec | 1975 | 1991 |
|------|------|------|
| Style number | 9085 (and 9088 Ruff-Out) | 9085 |
| Dimensions | 9"×14"×9" | 10½"×13"×9" |
| Closure | unstated in pages read | Top zipper |
| Strap | unstated | 36" adjustable extra-wide |
| Price | unstated | $210 |
| Colors | unstated | Black, British Tan, Forest Green, Mahogany, Navy, Red |

The dimensions changed slightly between 1975 and 1991 — possibly a
silhouette refinement or measurement-method change. The model name
and core silhouette are stable across the 16+ year run.

Hardware is solid brass throughout. Late-1960s and early-1970s
examples show a visible center seam down the body — a construction
detail that predates the creed-patch era and that survives on early
examples as an era diagnostic [yourgreatfinds-collectors-guide].

## History

- **Introduced**: late 1960s. Yourgreatfinds calls it "one of the
  earliest styles Coach started making, dating back to the late 1960s
  through early 2000s" [yourgreatfinds-collectors-guide]. No primary
  catalog citation has been located yet that pins a specific year;
  confidence: `likely`.
- **Discontinued**: early 2000s, per the same source. Confidence:
  `likely`. The end of the run brackets the Reed Krakoff transition
  era and the 2001 Signature C launch — the archive's vintage cutoff.
- **Continuity**: 9085 appears to have been a stable style number
  across the full run. As with all Coach style numbers, the number
  alone identifies a model, not a year — see `research/E-authentication.md`
  §2.

## Era anchor

The Duffle Sac is the cleanest single-model demonstration of Coach's
creed evolution. The same silhouette, unchanged in essentials, carries
three different interior-marking conventions over its run:

- **Late 1960s / early 1970s (pre-creed, E2 / early E3)**: leather
  stamp inside, no creed patch and no serial number. This is correct
  for the era, not missing — `research/E-authentication.md` §8
  documents the "leather stamp inside but no creed" sub-era as
  late-1960s/early-1970s production [coachbagrehab-dating]. Center
  seam visible on the body [yourgreatfinds-collectors-guide].
- **Mid-1970s through 1994 (numbered creed, E3 / E4)**: leather creed
  patch with a digit-only unique serial — `xxx-xxxx` format in the
  1970s, `xxxx-xxx` format in the 1980s through 1994
  [coachbagrehab-dating, essexfashionhouse-serials]. Creed reads
  "COACH LEATHERWARE" with the small-caps "LEATHERWARE" beneath
  "COACH" — the strongest pre-1994 textual anchor.
- **Post-1994 (letter-number style code, E5)**: creed serial shifts
  to the `[A-M][0-9][A-Z]-9085` format — month letter, year digit,
  factory letter, dash, style number [coachbagrehab-dating]. The
  trailing 9085 identifies the bag as a Duffle Sac.

The pedagogical value is direct: a vintage seller advertising a
"no-creed Duffle Sac" is not necessarily selling an incomplete or
suspect bag. The absence of a creed on a 9085 is consistent with
late-1960s/early-1970s production. The same absence on a Willis,
which was not introduced until the 1980s, would be a strong fake
tell. Era-correctness is model-specific.

## Authentication

What to check by era:

### Late 1960s / early 1970s

- **No creed.** Leather stamp only, on the interior. Correct, not a
  fake tell.
- **No serial number.** Correct.
- **Center seam** visible on the body
  [yourgreatfinds-collectors-guide].
- **Solid brass hardware**, often unstamped (Cashin-era hardware
  frequently carries no "COACH" mark — see
  `research/E-authentication.md` §4 [coachbagrehab-dating]).

### Mid-1970s through 1994

- **Numbered creed** with the small-caps "LEATHERWARE" beneath
  "COACH" [coachbagrehab-dating]. Pre-1994 wording.
- **Digit-only unique serial**: `xxx-xxxx` in the 1970s, `xxxx-xxx`
  in the 1980s through 1994 [coachbagrehab-dating,
  essexfashionhouse-serials]. The serial is **not** a style code; the
  Duffle Sac style number 9085 does not appear in the creed in this
  era.
- **"Made in United States"** or "Made in New York City, U.S.A." in
  the creed text [coachbagrehab-dating]. NYC factory through ~1980;
  other US factories thereafter.

### Post-1994

- **Letter-digit-letter style code**: `[A-M][0-9][A-Z]-9085`. The
  trailing 9085 identifies a Duffle Sac.
- **Country of origin** may be Costa Rica, Hungary, Turkey,
  Dominican Republic, or other early-offshore countries — match the
  country to the era window [coachbagrehab-dating, purseforum-costa-rica].
- **Single-digit year ambiguity** applies: a code like `B6D-9085`
  resolves equally to Feb 1996 or Feb 2006 under the single-digit-year
  scheme [coachbagrehab-dating]. Cross-check creed wording, lining,
  and country of origin. Per `research/E-authentication.md` §2 the
  decoder cannot stand alone.

### Across all eras

- **Solid brass hardware**, non-magnetic [coachbagrehab-dating].
  A magnet that sticks indicates plated steel — strong fake or
  post-vintage signal.
- **Glove-tanned cowhide** with **raw or burnished edges**. Painted
  glossy edges on a Duffle Sac claiming pre-1995 production are a
  strong fake or reproduction signal [coachbagrehab-dating].
- **Unlined or minimally lined.** Unlined is correct for the NYC era;
  heat-cut polyester lining on a bag claiming pre-1995 manufacture is
  a fake tell [coachbagrehab-dating, yourgreatfinds-collectors-guide].

## Designer attribution

The Duffle Sac was in production during Bonnie Cashin's tenure at
Coach (1962–1974) [wwd-coach-early-years]. Whether Cashin **designed**
the Duffle Sac is a separate question, and the answer is not yet
established from free sources. Yourgreatfinds places the model's
introduction in the late 1960s, well within Cashin's run, but does
not attribute the design to her [yourgreatfinds-collectors-guide].

This entry treats the designer attribution as `tentative`. Per
`STYLE.md` §4 high-folklore zone 2, Bonnie Cashin attribution requires
a primary source — a 1962–1974 Coach catalog showing the Duffle Sac,
or a UCLA Cashin papers cite [ucla-cashin-papers]. Neither has been
pulled into this archive yet. Two specific verification paths:

1. A 1962–1974 Coach catalog showing the Duffle Sac would establish
   the era of introduction and thereby narrow whether the model is
   plausibly a Cashin design or a post-1974 in-house addition that
   inherited an earlier silhouette label.
2. The UCLA Bonnie Cashin Collection finding aid [ucla-cashin-papers]
   may attribute the silhouette directly. Cashin's design illustrations
   and contemporaneous press materials are held there.

Until one of those is in hand, this entry does **not** confirm Cashin
authorship. The bag was *in production* during her tenure; that is not
the same as being her design.
