# Skinny Tote

The Skinny Tote is a 1970s Coach silhouette with a kiss-lock exterior
coin purse and a Talon brass zipper, attributed to Bonnie Cashin in
the Tier 3 dealer record [yourgreatfinds-collectors-guide]. An earlier
variant — informally called "The Original Skinny Tote" — carries
features that point to a late-1960s or early-1970s production date:
no leather piping at the base, different handles, and a small metal
interior tag rather than a creed patch. Both the attribution and the
introduction year are unconfirmed, and the standard Cashin-timing
problem applies: the 1970s span the back half of Cashin's tenure
(through 1974) and the immediate post-Cashin in-house era. Without a
1962–1974 catalog page or a UCLA Cashin papers cite, the entry holds
the attribution at `likely` and the overall confidence at
`tentative`.

## Description

Per the dealer compilation
[yourgreatfinds-collectors-guide]:

> 1970s production, "designed by Bonnie Cashin." Features kiss lock
> coin purse exterior and Talon brass zipper.

The defining elements of the silhouette as documented are therefore:

- A **kiss-lock coin purse mounted on the exterior**, a Cashin-vocabulary
  feature consistent with her use of the kissing-lock closure on coin
  purses and bag accents through the 1962–1974 period (see
  `research/C-cashin.md` §3).
- A **Talon brass zipper**, which aligns with pre-mid-1970s era
  diagnostics (see `research/E-authentication.md` §5: Talon was the
  common zipper maker on Coach bags before YKK became dominant from
  the late 1970s onward).
- **Solid brass hardware** consistent with Cashin-era and early NYC-era
  construction.

Specific dimensions, colors beyond the **chamois** example noted in
the dealer record, and closure variants are not pinned to a primary
catalog source in the archive's working references; this section will
be expanded once a dated catalog page is matched to the bag.

## The "Original Skinny Tote" variant

The dealer record [yourgreatfinds-collectors-guide] distinguishes an
earlier variant from the later 1970s Skinny Tote by three tells:

1. **No leather piping at the base** — the later 1970s production has
   leather piping along the bottom seam; the original lacks it.
2. **Different handles** — the original's handles differ in shape
   and/or attachment from the later production.
3. **An early metal interior tag** in place of a creed patch.

These tells are consistent with the **E2a / E2b sub-eras** in
`research/E-authentication.md` §8: a small metal interior tag only is
the diagnostic of early-1962-to-mid-1960s Coach manufacture (E2a),
and a leather stamp inside with no creed patch is the late-1960s to
early-1970s diagnostic (E2b). An "Original Skinny Tote" with a metal
interior tag therefore reads as **early-to-mid-1960s production** on
the era diagnostics — pushing the introduction of the silhouette
earlier than the dealer record's "1970s" framing for the standard
Skinny Tote, and squarely inside Cashin's contracted tenure.

A **chamois color example** of the original variant is noted in the
dealer record [yourgreatfinds-collectors-guide].

The two-variant picture (early metal-tag original; later 1970s
production with piped base and revised handles) is the strongest
internal signal that the silhouette pre-dates 1974 in some form, even
if the named "Skinny Tote" entries in dealer compilations cluster in
the 1970s. It does not by itself confirm Cashin authorship.

## Designer attribution — the timing problem

Cashin's contracted tenure at Coach ran **1962–1974, full stop**
[wwd-coach-early-years, vintagecoachie]. She was a contracted
designer, not an employee; the contract ended in 1974 and that date
range is not negotiable. Bags introduced from 1975 onward — even ones
that use her vocabulary (kiss-lock, side pockets, brass turnlock,
saturated color) — are by definition not Cashin designs.

The Skinny Tote's "1970s" framing in the dealer record
[yourgreatfinds-collectors-guide] is therefore ambiguous in the same
way the Mailbox and Musette entries are: a 1970–1974 introduction is
plausibly Cashin's; a 1975–1979 introduction is plausibly not, no
matter how often the community calls it a "Cashin design." The
Cashin attribution table in `research/C-cashin.md` §4 marks the
Skinny Tote (listed there as "Slimline / Skinny Tote") as `likely
Cashin` pending a pre-1974 catalog cite; that is the ceiling the
evidence currently supports.

The "Original Skinny Tote" variant complicates this in Cashin's
favor: if the original carries a metal interior tag (E2a era
diagnostic, early-to-mid-1960s), the **silhouette itself** plausibly
originated inside her tenure even if the named-and-numbered "Skinny
Tote" reached its later form in the 1970s. That is suggestive, not
conclusive — era diagnostics on a single dealer-described example
cannot stand in for a catalog page or a UCLA Cashin papers
attribution.

**Verification path** — any one of the following would resolve the
attribution:

1. A **1962–1974 Coach catalog page** showing the Skinny Tote (or a
   "Slimline" / "Original Skinny Tote") in production.
2. A **UCLA Cashin papers** [ucla-cashin-papers] entry attributing
   the silhouette to her — design illustrations, contracts, or
   correspondence referencing the bag by name.
3. A **primary press piece** (period WWD, Vogue, Harper's Bazaar,
   NYT) with Cashin credited for the design.

Absent one of these, the attribution stays at `likely` and the entry
is honest about what it does and does not know.

## Authentication

Authentication of a Skinny Tote claimed to be Cashin-era depends on
reading era diagnostics off the bag itself, then asking whether those
diagnostics are consistent with a pre-1974 introduction.

- **Talon brass zipper — pre-mid-1970s.** A Talon zipper aligns with
  the pre-mid-1970s zipper-maker diagnostics in
  `research/E-authentication.md` §5; YKK became dominant from the late
  1970s onward. A Skinny Tote with a YKK zipper is consistent with
  later 1970s or post-Cashin production rather than a Cashin-era
  example.
- **Small metal interior tag — early 1960s (E2a).** Per
  `research/E-authentication.md` §1 and §8, a small metal interior
  tag with no leather creed patch is the diagnostic of early-1962-to-
  mid-1960s Coach manufacture. An "Original Skinny Tote" with a metal
  tag reads as E2a — squarely inside Cashin's tenure, though the era
  diagnostic on its own does not establish her authorship.
- **Leather stamp inside, no creed patch — late 1960s / early 1970s
  (E2b).** Bags from this window also fall inside Cashin's tenure
  through 1974.
- **A numbered creed patch places the bag in the mid-1970s or later.**
  The numbered creed with a 1970s `xxx-xxxx` digit-only serial format
  was in use from roughly the mid-1970s onward
  [coachbagrehab-dating]. A Skinny Tote with a numbered creed could
  therefore be either pre or post Cashin departure — the format itself
  spans 1973/1974/1975/1976+ and does not cleanly resolve the
  attribution.
- **Kiss-lock coin purse exterior — Cashin-vocabulary consistent.**
  The kissing-lock closure was a Cashin signature (see
  `research/C-cashin.md` §3). Its presence on the Skinny Tote is
  consistent with Cashin authorship but, like the turnlock on later
  bags, is a vocabulary signal rather than an authorship signal —
  the closure variant continued in use after 1974.
- **Solid brass hardware — magnet test.** Brass is non-magnetic
  (`research/E-authentication.md` §4). A magnet that sticks to the
  zipper pull, kiss-lock, or any other hardware indicates plated
  steel, which is post-vintage and immediately rules out a
  Cashin-era bag.
- **Glove-tanned cowhide, raw or burnished edges.** Painted glossy
  edges on a "Cashin-era Skinny Tote" are a strong fake or
  reproduction signal — Coach NYC-era construction does not paint
  edges (`research/E-authentication.md` §7).

None of these diagnostics by themselves resolve the authorship
question. They establish that a given Skinny Tote is consistent with
Cashin-era production; they do not tell you whether the silhouette was
*introduced* during her tenure.

## Open verification items

Listed explicitly so any future contributor knows exactly what would
upgrade this entry's confidence:

1. **Dated Coach catalog page** showing the Skinny Tote (or
   "Slimline" / "Original Skinny Tote") in production, ideally from
   the 1962–1974 window. The PurseForum vintage-Coach catalogs thread
   (member-uploaded scans, browser-only) is the most likely free
   source.
2. **UCLA Cashin papers** [ucla-cashin-papers] reference attributing
   the Skinny Tote to Cashin — design illustrations, named
   correspondence, or contract addenda. The online finding aid is at
   https://oac.cdlib.org/findaid/ark:/13030/kt9z09q97x/.
3. **Primary press feature** (period WWD, Vogue, Harper's Bazaar, NYT)
   with Cashin credited for the Skinny Tote / Slimline design.
4. **Stephanie Lake's *Bonnie Cashin: Chic Is Where You Find It***
   (Rizzoli, 2016) — check the Coach-period chapter for any direct
   reference to a Skinny Tote or Slimline.
5. **Style number and dimensions** — neither is recorded in the
   working references.
6. **Resolution of the "Skinny Tote" vs "Slimline" naming** — the
   `research/D-models.md` table lists them together; whether Coach
   used both names or whether one is a community coinage is open.

Until at least one of items 1–4 lands, this entry stays at
`confidence: tentative` and the designer attribution at `likely`. The
community's aggregate volume of "Cashin Skinny Tote" claims is not
sufficient to upgrade either tag; only primary evidence is.
