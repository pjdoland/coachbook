## Lead

The Cashin Carry was the inaugural design of Bonnie Cashin's Coach
collection, launched **Fall 1962** [wwd-coach-early-years,
vintagecoachie]. It is the bag that established the visual vocabulary
of the Cashin era: a soft tote silhouette in glove-leather, side
pockets, and the bright, considered color palette that distinguished
Coach from the brown-leather conservatism of its 1950s output.

**Hardware caveat — important for authentication:** the brass turnlock
most associated with the Cashin Carry came **two years after** the
1962 launch. Cashin introduced the turnlock to her work at Coach in
**1964** [vintagefashionguild-cashin]. The earliest Cashin Carry totes
(Fall 1962, 1963) used different closures — open-top tote variants
and kiss-lock variants are documented from this pre-turnlock window.
A Cashin Carry stamped or hardware-dated to 1962–63 should not have a
turnlock; one that does is either later production or a misdated bag.

The bag's name doubles as a pun on the retail phrase "cash and carry."
That pun was Cashin's; the design was hers; and for the next twelve
years almost everything Coach made for women rested on the language
this single bag introduced.

## History

In 1961, Miles and Lillian Cahn purchased Gail Leather Products, the
small Manhattan workshop that had been making Coach-branded cowhide
goods since around 1960 [fundinguniverse-coach]. Lillian Cahn pushed
for a women's handbag line and suggested the company hire an outside
designer to start one [fundinguniverse-coach]. The designer they
brought in, in **1962**, was Bonnie Cashin
[wwd-coach-early-years, fundinguniverse-coach, wikipedia-cashin].

Cashin was already a known quantity. She had won the Coty Award in
1950 for her work in sportswear and had been collaborating with leather
importer Philip Sills since 1953 [wikipedia-cashin,
encyclopedia-com-cashin]; the Sills work, in particular, had given her
the leather-craft vocabulary she would now bring to handbags. She was
contracted, not employed; her account at Coach was referred to
internally as "the Bonnie Cashin account" [wwd-coach-early-years].
Her contractual paperwork survives in the Bonnie Cashin Collection at
UCLA [ucla-cashin-papers].

The Cashin Carry was the first product of that arrangement. WWD's
"Coach: The Early Years" describes the launch as an immediate success
and credits the Cashin Carry as the design that established the line
[wwd-coach-early-years]. Cashin would design for Coach until 1974
[wwd-coach-early-years, vintagecoachie].

## Description

The Cashin Carry is a soft, structured tote in glove-tanned cowhide.
The Cashin-era visual signatures, all of which appear here and most of
which she introduced to Coach, are:

- **Solid brass turnlock** (on post-1964 production only — see hardware
  caveat in the lead), inspired by the lift-and-turn closures on her
  1940s convertible automobile [wikipedia-cashin,
  bonnie-cashin-archive-org, vintagefashionguild-cashin]. The same
  fastener appeared non-exclusively on Cashin's collections for
  Coach, Sills, Meyers, Crescendoe-Superb, HBA, and D. Klein.
- **Side pockets**, a Cashin contribution to the Coach handbag
  vocabulary [wwd-coach-early-years].
- **Brighter color palette** than the Coach product that preceded
  her — a deliberate break from the muted browns of late-1950s
  cowhide [wwd-coach-early-years].
- **Striped fabric lining**, a Cashin signature that recurs across her
  Sills outerwear and her Coach bags of the period [vintagecoachie].

The combination — turnlock-closed tote, side pockets, striped lining,
saturated color — is the visual definition of an early-1960s Cashin
Coach bag. Bags described as "Cashin Carry" without these elements are
either later production runs (when the line continued under reduced
Cashin involvement) or misattributed.

## Variants

Cashin-era totes appear under the "Cashin Carry" umbrella in several
configurations. The dealer-compiled record [yourgreatfinds-collectors-guide]
lists kiss-lock variants alongside open totes within the broader
Cashin output, and the Cashin Coach period is generally documented as
including double-strap and single-strap forms. Specific dimensional
variants and closure subtypes for the Cashin Carry itself are not
yet pinned to a primary catalog source in the archive's working

## Authentication (Cashin-era Cashin Carry)

The Cashin Carry predates every authentication-by-creed feature the
later NYC era relied on. Authenticate it on the Cashin-era profile:

- **No numbered creed.** Coach's leather creed patch with style and
  serial number is a feature of the post-Cashin NYC era. Early-1960s
  Cashin Carry bags carry either a **small interior metal tag** or
  no interior label at all. A Cashin-era bag with a "COACH
  LEATHERWARE / EST. 1941 / NEW YORK CITY" creed and a serial number
  is **mislabeled by era** — it is a later-production bag, not a
  1962–1974 example.
- **No serial number.** Pre-numbered-creed; absence is not a fake
  tell for this era.
- **Striped fabric lining.** A Cashin signature
  [vintagecoachie]. A "Cashin Carry" with a plain cotton or
  jacquard lining is more consistent with later production than with
  the 1962–1974 originals.
- **Solid brass turnlock**, riveted through. Brass is non-magnetic
  and heavy for its size; a magnet that sticks indicates plated steel
  and a much later piece. The turnlock back plate on early Cashin-era
  hardware is unstamped.
- **Glove-tanned cowhide** body, drum-dyed through; cut edges show
  the same color as the surface. Painted edges are not period-correct
  for an early Cashin Carry.
- **No "Made in [country]" stamp.** Country-of-origin stamps in the
  creed are an NYC-era feature. Cashin-era pieces are U.S.-made but
  not stamped as such on the creed (because there is no creed to
  stamp).

When in doubt, the era cue that resolves most questions is the creed:
a numbered creed places a bag firmly *after* the Cashin period regardless
of model name on the listing.

## Designer attribution

This is one of the **few** Coach bags whose attribution to Bonnie
Cashin is clean. The Cashin Carry tote and the kissing-lock coin
purse are the most clearly attributable Cashin designs in the
period; both appear in Cashin's own design output and in the
contemporaneous record of her Coach collaboration
[wwd-coach-early-years, vintagecoachie].

Attribution discipline matters because the vintage-Coach community
routinely calls bags introduced *after* 1974 "Cashin designs." They
are not. Cashin's Coach contract ended in 1974
[wwd-coach-early-years, vintagecoachie]; bags introduced from 1975
onward — including Willis, Court, Madison, Sheridan, Soho — were the
work of Coach's in-house team or other contracted designers, even
when they used the visual vocabulary (turnlock, side pockets) Cashin
had established. "Cashin-influenced" is the accurate description for
those bags. "Cashin-designed" is not.

For the Cashin Carry specifically: the design is hers, the launch is
documented as her debut Coach collection, and the bag is referenced
under her name in the secondary literature [wwd-coach-early-years,
encyclopedia-com-cashin, wikipedia-cashin]. Stephanie Lake's *Bonnie
Cashin: Chic Is Where You Find It* (Rizzoli, 2016) is the most
authoritative single biography and treats the Coach work as an
identified phase of her career [lake-cashin-2016]; Lake also operates
the curated Bonnie Cashin Archive online [bonnie-cashin-archive-org].

## Modern context

Coach's contemporary "Coach Originals" / archival re-release lines
have, at various points, revisited Cashin-era silhouettes; the brand
also maintains an official "From the Archives" image set documenting
historical pieces. Specific re-released SKUs branded as Cashin Carry
(versus generic "vintage tote" re-issues that share the silhouette)
have not been pinned to a Tier 1 brand-page in the archive's working
references and are an open verification item.
