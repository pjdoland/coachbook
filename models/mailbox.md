# Mailbox

The RFD Mailbox is a 1970s Coach silhouette widely attributed to Bonnie
Cashin in the vintage-Coach community [yourgreatfinds-collectors-guide,
vintagecoachie]. The attribution is plausible but **unconfirmed**:
resolving it requires placing the bag's introduction either inside
(pre-1974, Cashin's contracted tenure) or outside (post-1974, in-house
team) Cashin's contractual window with Coach. Until a dated catalog or
UCLA Cashin papers cite anchors the introduction year, the entry holds
the attribution at `likely` and treats the era as `tentative`.

## Description

The Mailbox is named for its silhouette, which resembles a rural-route
mail box: a vertical body with a curved or domed top, evoking the
arched cap of a classic American RFD ("Rural Free Delivery") roadside
mailbox. The front carries a turnlock or similar Cashin-era closure,
with the rest of the hardware in solid brass.

Two hardware caveats matter for dating:

- **The brass turnlock was introduced to Cashin's Coach work in 1964**,
  two years after her 1962 hire [vintagefashionguild-cashin]. A Mailbox
  with a turnlock therefore cannot predate 1964, but the turnlock by
  itself does not place the bag inside Cashin's tenure — Coach
  continued using turnlock hardware on in-house bags long after Cashin
  departed in 1974.
- The same fastener appeared non-exclusively across Cashin's
  collections for Sills, Meyers, Crescendoe-Superb, HBA, and D. Klein
  [vintagefashionguild-cashin]. Turnlock presence is a Cashin-era
  *vocabulary* signal, not an authorship signal.

Specific dimensions, lining, and closure-type variants for the Mailbox
are not pinned to a primary catalog source in the archive's working
references; this section will be expanded once a dated catalog page is
matched to the bag.

## Designer attribution — the timing problem

This is the section the entry is built around. Mailbox attribution is
in one of the highest-folklore zones in vintage-Coach research (see
`STYLE.md` §4 and `research/C-cashin.md` §5), and the timing problem
must not be collapsed.

**Cashin's contracted tenure at Coach was 1962–1974, full stop**
[wwd-coach-early-years, vintagecoachie]. She was a contracted
designer, not a Coach employee; the contract ended in 1974 and is not
negotiable as a date range. Bags introduced from 1975 onward — even
those that use her vocabulary (turnlock, side pockets, saturated
color) — are by definition not Cashin designs.

A "1970s Mailbox" lands on either side of that line:

- If the Mailbox was introduced in **1970, 1971, 1972, 1973, or
  1974**, it falls inside Cashin's tenure and the attribution is
  plausible. To upgrade to `confirmed`, the entry needs primary
  evidence that Cashin specifically designed it (not just that it
  existed during her tenure).
- If the Mailbox was introduced in **1975, 1976, 1977, 1978, or
  1979**, it falls outside Cashin's tenure and the attribution is
  wrong on its face — the bag was the work of Coach's in-house team
  using Cashin-era vocabulary, no matter how often the community calls
  it a Cashin design.

The dealer-compiled record [yourgreatfinds-collectors-guide] places
the Mailbox in the "1970s" without resolving which half of the decade,
and the Cashin attribution table in `research/C-cashin.md` §4 marks
the bag `likely Cashin` pending catalog confirmation. That is the
ceiling the evidence currently supports.

**Verification path** — any one of the following would resolve the
attribution:

1. A **1962–1974 Coach catalog page** showing the Mailbox in
   production (or a press feature dated to that window referencing the
   bag).
2. A **UCLA Cashin papers** [ucla-cashin-papers] entry attributing the
   Mailbox to her — design illustrations, contracts referencing it by
   name, or correspondence about its development.
3. A **primary press piece** (period WWD, Vogue, NYT) with Cashin
   discussing or being credited with the Mailbox design.

Absent one of these, the attribution stays at `likely` and the entry
is honest about what it does and does not know.

## Authentication

Authentication of a Mailbox claimed to be Cashin-era depends on
reading era diagnostics off the bag itself, then asking whether those
diagnostics are consistent with a pre-1974 introduction.

- **Pre-creed era diagnostics align with late-1960s / early-1970s
  production.** Per `research/E-authentication.md` §1 and §8, bags
  from this window carry either a small interior metal tag only
  (early 1960s) or a leather stamp inside with no creed patch and no
  serial (late 1960s–early 1970s). A Mailbox with no creed and only a
  leather stamp is consistent with a pre-1974 introduction date — but
  the absence of a creed is also consistent with a 1973 or 1974 bag
  manufactured just before the numbered creed era began.
- **A numbered creed places the bag in the mid-1970s or later.** The
  numbered creed with a 1970s `xxx-xxxx` digit-only serial format
  (E3) was in use from roughly the mid-1970s onward
  [coachbagrehab-dating]. A Mailbox with a numbered creed therefore
  could be either pre or post Cashin departure — the format itself
  spans 1973/1974/1975/1976+ and does not cleanly resolve the
  attribution timing.
- **Brass turnlock — post-1964 if present.** Cashin introduced the
  turnlock to her Coach work in 1964 [vintagefashionguild-cashin]. A
  Mailbox with a turnlock is post-1964 production; this is upper-
  bound consistent with Cashin authorship but does not prove it.
- **Solid brass hardware — magnet test.** Brass is non-magnetic
  (`research/E-authentication.md` §4). A magnet that sticks to the
  turnlock or D-rings indicates plated steel, which is post-vintage
  and immediately rules out a Cashin-era bag.
- **Glove-tanned cowhide, raw or burnished edges.** Painted glossy
  edges on a "Cashin-era Mailbox" are a strong fake or reproduction
  signal — Coach NYC-era construction does not paint edges
  (`research/E-authentication.md` §7).

None of these diagnostics by themselves resolve the authorship
question. They establish that a given Mailbox is consistent with
late-1960s / early-1970s production; they do not tell you whether the
silhouette was *introduced* during Cashin's tenure.

## The "RFD" name

"RFD" stands for **Rural Free Delivery**, the U.S. Postal Service
program (formally launched 1896, expanded through the early 20th
century) that delivered mail directly to rural homes via roadside
mailboxes — the curve-topped, flag-equipped tin boxes whose silhouette
the bag echoes. The Mailbox name is straightforwardly descriptive of
the shape, with "RFD" as a period-evocative qualifier.

Whether the "RFD" prefix was Coach's official catalog name or a
community label that attached later is **an open verification item**.
The dealer-compiled record uses "RFD Mailbox bag or tote" as
interchangeable phrasings; a 1970s Coach catalog spread would resolve
which form (if either) appeared in print.

## Open verification items

Listed explicitly so any future contributor knows exactly what would
upgrade this entry's confidence:

1. **Dated Coach catalog page** showing the Mailbox in production,
   ideally from the 1970–1974 window. The PurseForum vintage-Coach
   catalogs thread (member-uploaded scans, browser-only) is the most
   likely free source.
2. **UCLA Cashin papers** [ucla-cashin-papers] reference attributing
   the Mailbox to Cashin — design illustrations, named correspondence,
   or contract addenda. The online finding aid is at
   https://oac.cdlib.org/findaid/ark:/13030/kt9z09q97x/.
3. **Primary press feature** (period WWD, Vogue, Harper's Bazaar, NYT)
   with Cashin credited for the Mailbox. Pre-paywall WWD coverage is
   the most plausible target [wwd-coach-early-years].
4. **Stephanie Lake's *Bonnie Cashin: Chic Is Where You Find It***
   (Rizzoli, 2016) — check the Coach-period chapter for any direct
   reference to a Mailbox / RFD bag.
5. **Style number and dimensions** — neither is recorded in the
   working references; both should appear in any 1970s catalog page
   that surfaces.
6. **Origin of the "RFD" label** — Coach catalog name or community
   coinage.

Until at least one of items 1–4 lands, this entry stays at
`confidence: tentative` and the designer attribution at `likely`. The
community's aggregate volume of "Cashin Mailbox" claims is not
sufficient to upgrade either tag; only primary evidence is.
