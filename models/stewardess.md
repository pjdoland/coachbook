# Stewardess

The Stewardess (style 9525) is a 1970s NYC-era Coach bag carrying an
unusual and unverified historical claim: a single Tier 3 dealer
compilation reports it was "originally part of United Airlines approved
uniform for flight attendants" [yourgreatfinds-collectors-guide]. That
claim has not been corroborated by any primary source located so far.
Most of what is known about the Stewardess in this archive comes from
that one source and is therefore tagged `tentative`.

## Description

The Stewardess is a 1970s-era shoulder bag in glove-tanned cowhide with
solid brass hardware, described by Yourgreatfinds as having an
"interior pocket with creed" [yourgreatfinds-collectors-guide]. No
dimensions, color list, or strap specification have been located in
free public sources. A dated 1970s catalog page would settle the
silhouette and dimensions; none has been pulled into the archive yet.

## The United Airlines variant

Multiple independent resale listings (Tier 4) and a long-running
collector reference (vintagecoachie, Tier 3) document a **United
Airlines employees-only variant** of the Stewardess silhouette,
distinct from the standard retail style 9525. Confidence on the
existence of this variant: `likely`. Confidence on the specific
characteristics below: `tentative` — the details come from listing
descriptions, not a primary catalog or UA Heritage document.

### What's claimed about the UA variant

Per multiple Etsy / Poshmark / Whatnot listings and the vintagecoachie
TikTok account that compares the "classic 9525" to the "1980s United
Airlines model" as separate products:

- **United Airlines logo embossed on the strap** — the strongest
  diagnostic if confirmed; would distinguish the UA variant from the
  retail 9525 at a glance
- **British Tan only** — single colorway, per multiple listings
- **Made exclusively for United Airlines employees**, not sold to the
  public — a closed-distribution variant
- **Distinguishing features beyond the strap stamp**: ID photo
  pocket on the interior; suede interior; Coach hangtag; four brass
  protective feet on the base
- **Dimensions** ~10.5" L × 8" H × 4.5" W per a representative listing
- **Era**: vintagecoachie distinguishes a "1980s United Airlines
  model" from the classic 1970s Stewardess (9525). Whether the UA
  variant ran alongside the retail 9525 from the 1970s onward, or was
  a discrete 1980s commission, is open. Multiple listings call the
  bag "1970s"; vintagecoachie calls it "1980s." Mark `disputed`.

### What's documented about the retail 9525

The retail Stewardess 9525 in the dealer-compiled record [yourgreatfinds-collectors-guide]
predates the UA variant or runs alongside it; the Yourgreatfinds entry
notes generally that "some examples feature United Airlines emblem
stamp" — this is now better understood as conflating the two products.
Most 9525 examples in the wild are the public-retail bag without UA
branding.

### What would upgrade the variant claims to `confirmed`

- **A dated UAL employee handbook or uniform manual** listing Coach
  (or the bag by name/specification) as an authorized accessory for
  flight attendants. Strongest primary source.
- **A dated period press photo** (1970s/1980s) of a UA flight attendant
  in uniform carrying the bag, with photo provenance verifiable.
- **A USPTO trademark co-licensing record** or contract filing showing
  a Coach–United Airlines commercial relationship for the era.
- **Multiple independent dated listings** showing the UA strap-stamp
  on bags whose creed/serial places production in the same era —
  triangulating across listings is partial corroboration but does not
  establish primary truth.

### Verification path

- Query the **United Airlines Heritage** archives or the
  **United Airlines Historical Society** for period uniform
  documentation.
- Cross-reference dated UA flight-attendant period photography
  (1970s–1980s) for the silhouette and the UA-strap detail.
- Examine an actual UA-strap-stamped example in person (jeweler's
  loupe — see authentication/toolkit.md) to confirm the stamp is
  factory-applied at production rather than aftermarket.

### Authenticator's caution

A "Stewardess with UA strap stamp" is now a documented and
collector-recognized subtype, but it is also exactly the kind of bag
where Frankenbag construction (UA strap from a real UA bag swapped
onto a body of unknown provenance) is plausible — see
[authentication/counterfeits/frankenbags.md](../authentication/counterfeits/frankenbags.md).
A UA-marked example needs the same era-consistency cross-checks as
any other Coach: creed wording, body construction, hardware
consistency, edge treatment, lining, country-of-origin marking.

### Cross-reference: Cashin's American Airlines uniform commission

Tier 2 source Maharam reports that Bonnie Cashin designed
"the first-ever flight attendants' uniforms" for **American Airlines**
[maharam-cashin]. This is a separate fact from the Coach-Stewardess-
United-Airlines claim, but it has been cited in community discussions
as if the two were related. They are not:

- The Cashin AA uniform commission appears to predate her 1962 Coach
  engagement (date unstated in Maharam) and is unrelated to any Coach
  bag.
- The Coach Stewardess is a 1970s product and the community claim
  attaches to **United** Airlines, not American.
- AA-uniform credentials establish that Cashin had legitimate
  aviation-industry design experience but do NOT confirm any Coach
  product was part of any airline's uniform program.

A reader encountering "Bonnie Cashin designed for an airline" should
not infer that the Coach Stewardess therefore was a uniform bag. See
[designers/bonnie-cashin.md](../designers/bonnie-cashin.md) for the
broader Cashin client list.

## Designer attribution

The Stewardess's "1970s" introduction window straddles two design
regimes. Bonnie Cashin's contract with Coach ran 1962–1974
[wwd-coach-early-years, vintagefashionguild-cashin]; bags introduced
before her departure are candidates for Cashin attribution, while bags
introduced 1975 or later are not, regardless of community framing
(STYLE.md §4 high-folklore zone 2). Without a primary catalog or other
dated source pinning the Stewardess to a specific year, designer
attribution cannot be committed.

This entry does not claim Cashin authorship and does not claim a
specific in-house designer either. `designer: in-house` with
`designer_confidence: tentative` reflects that the bag is most likely a
Coach in-house design but that a Cashin-era introduction cannot be
ruled out. Promotion requires either a dated catalog pre-1975 (which
would open a Cashin attribution question) or a dated catalog 1975+
(which would close it).

## Authentication

For a bag presented as a vintage Stewardess:

- **Style number 9525 in the creed.** The number itself is `tentative`
  in this archive — D1 is the only source that ties 9525 to the
  Stewardess name [yourgreatfinds-collectors-guide]. A second
  independent corroboration (catalog scan, dated forum post with photo)
  would promote it.
- **Pre-1994 creed wording.** A 1970s Stewardess will carry the early
  NYC-era creed. The pre-1994 unique-serial format (digit-only, e.g.
  `xxxx-xxx`) applies; the post-1994 letter-digit-letter-dash-digits
  scheme would put the bag outside its claimed production window.
- **Era-appropriate hardware.** Solid brass throughout, non-magnetic.
  Magnetic hardware on a bag presented as 1970s is a strong fake or
  later-reproduction signal (see PLAN.md §authentication and
  `research/E-authentication.md`).
- **Optional UAL emblem stamp on some examples.** Community-reported
  per D1; primary confirmation absent. Treat the presence of such a
  stamp as a *flag for further investigation*, not as an authentication
  signal in either direction. Its absence proves nothing; its presence
  does not by itself authenticate the bag or confirm the UAL provenance.
- **Edge treatment.** Glove-tanned cowhide with raw or burnished edges.
  Painted glossy edges on a "1970s" Stewardess are a strong fake or
  reproduction signal (PLAN.md §6).

## Open verification items

1. **The UAL claim** — pursue via UAL Heritage archives and the United
   Airlines Historical Society as described above. Until a primary
   source surfaces, the claim remains `tentative` and is not
   characterized as fact in this entry.
2. **A dated 1970s catalog page showing the Stewardess** — would
   simultaneously confirm style number 9525, narrow the introduction
   year (pre- vs post-1974, with downstream implications for designer
   attribution), and provide canonical dimensions and colors.
3. **A second independent source** for the Stewardess name and style
   number beyond Yourgreatfinds — needed to lift any of the basic
   identifying facts above `tentative`.
4. **Dimensions, colors, and variant list** — none located in free
   public sources at present.
