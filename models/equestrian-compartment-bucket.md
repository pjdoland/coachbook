## Lead

The Equestrian Compartment Bucket (style 9809) is a Coach Classic
NYC-era bucket-silhouette bag distinguished by multiple interior
compartments, documented in a Tier 3 dealer compilation
[yourgreatfinds-collectors-guide]. A related "Slim Equestrian
Bucket Bag" is also documented in the same source, indicating at
least one variant within the Equestrian sub-family.

## Description

The Equestrian Compartment Bucket is identified as a bucket bag
with multiple interior compartments under style number 9809
[yourgreatfinds-collectors-guide]. The compartmented interior is
the diagnostic feature distinguishing it from other NYC-era bucket
silhouettes (Bradley Bucket 9936, Nicholas Bucket Drawstring 9946,
later Bleecker Bucket). The Slim Equestrian Bucket Bag is recorded
as a related narrower variant; a separate style number for the
slim form has not been located in free public sources. Dimensions,
strap configuration, and color list are unconfirmed.

## Authentication

A pre-1994 Equestrian Compartment Bucket should carry the
diagnostics common to the Classic NYC era (cross-reference
`authentication/` for the full era-keyed matrix):

- **Style number 9809.** Visible on the leather creed inside the
  bag on numbered-creed examples [yourgreatfinds-collectors-guide].
  A style number alone does not date a specific bag — Coach reused
  style numbers across multiple production years.
- **Creed wording (E3 diagnostic).** A pre-1994 Equestrian
  Compartment Bucket should read "COACH LEATHERWARE" with
  "LEATHERWARE" stamped in small letters beneath "COACH," typically
  with "Made in United States" or "Made in New York City, U.S.A."
  on earlier examples [coachbagrehab-dating, vintagecoachie].
- **Serial format.** Pre-1994 examples carry a digit-only unique
  serial (`xxx-xxxx` 1970s, `xxxx-xxx` 1980s) per
  [coachbagrehab-dating] — `tentative`. Any post-1994 production
  carrying 9809 forward (E4) would use the
  `[A-M][year-digit][factory-letter]-9809` pattern with the
  documented single-digit-year ambiguity.
- **Hardware.** Solid brass, non-magnetic. A magnet that sticks
  indicates plated steel and is a strong reproduction signal
  [coachbagrehab-dating].
