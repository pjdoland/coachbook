# City Bag (9790)

## Lead — primary-confirmed Early Spring 1987 launch with same-year SKU swap

**The City Bag debuts in the Early Spring 1987 catalog as Style 9785
at $134 (NEW)** [coach-catalogs-1960-1989]. By the next catalog
(Spring 1987), the City Bag is **renumbered to 9790** and the
abandoned 9785 SKU is **reassigned to the new "Zipper Tote"** by
Early Summer 1987. The 9790 number persists from Spring 1987 onward
through the rest of the deep-read corpus (per O2, M2, M3 evidence).

This same-year SKU renumbering is a documented Coach catalog
artifact — not an authentication red flag for vintage examples
made within the brief 9785 window.

## Authentication: brief 9785 window matters

A vintage **City Bag with Style 9785** in the creed should match
**Early Spring 1987 production only** — a narrow ~3-month window
[coach-catalogs-1960-1989]. After Spring 1987, the City Bag was
9790. A 9785 in good era-correct creed wording is therefore an
unusual but **correct-spec** rarity from the brief debut window,
not a fake or reproduction.

A 9790 with pre-1987 production date stamps would be wrong on its
face — the number wasn't yet active.

era.

## Era and designer

**Era E4 (Classic NYC late, 1985–1994).** Designer is **in-house
Coach team**. NOT Cashin (her contract ended 1974, 13 years before
the City Bag launched).

The City Bag launched ~18 months after the **Madison Satchel**
(Spring 1986 per O1). Both are Classic-line single-model 9XXX
silhouettes — not part of the 4XXX Lightweight Collection (which
also launched 1987 per O2) or the later-1990s Madison Collection
sub-line.

## Authentication

- **Style 9790** (or rare brief-window 9785) in creed
  [coach-catalogs-1960-1989]
- **Pre-1994 era**: LEATHERWARE creed wordmark expected
- **Solid brass hardware**, non-magnetic
- **Glove-tanned cowhide** with raw or burnished edges
- **All 10 Classics colors** documented Spring 1989
- **Hidden magnetic snap or top zipper closure** typical of 1989
  Lightweights — but City Bag is in the Classic line, so **brass
  turnlock or front-flap construction** more likely (verify per
  catalog imagery)
