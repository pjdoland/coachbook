## Lead

The Soft Sac (also rendered "Soft Sacs") was introduced in **1970**,
according to the vintagecoachie style guide [vintagecoachie] — a large,
supple bag in glove-tanned cowhide. The introduction is a notable
milestone in the Cashin era: vintagecoachie frames it as the moment
Coach pivoted away from Bonnie Cashin's bright-color-and-striped-lining
vocabulary and toward the brand's eventual standard "natural leather +
brass" identity [vintagecoachie]. Confidence is `likely` for the year
and `tentative` overall pending a 1970 catalog page or UCLA
finding-aid corroboration.

## The 1970 transition

vintagecoachie phrases the shift directly: "In 1970 we see the
introduction of 'Soft Sacs'; large, supple bags crafted in the glove
tanned cowhide Coach has become so famous for. The bright colors and
striped lining Bonnie was known for disappears" [vintagecoachie].

That framing matters for how the archive reads the back half of the
Cashin era. Cashin's contract did not end until **1974**
[wwd-coach-early-years]; she remained under contract through the Soft
Sac introduction. But the Soft Sac aesthetic — natural-leather body,
no striped fabric lining, restrained color — is the visual vocabulary
of the **post-Cashin** Coach. If vintagecoachie's reading is right,
Coach's design direction was already shifting away from Cashin's
signatures during her late tenure, four years before the contract
formally ended.

This is a high-folklore zone (per `STYLE.md` §4 zone 2). The 1970 date
itself rests on a single Tier 3 source; the broader claim — that Coach
shifted aesthetic direction mid-Cashin — is a contextual reading that
needs primary documentation before any of it promotes above
`tentative`.

The Soft Sac sits in the same general "softer carryall" family as the
Duffle Sac, which the dealer record places at "late 1960s" introduction

are siblings, successors, or separate models with overlapping naming

## Designer attribution

Marked `tentative`. Two readings are both consistent with what is
currently sourced:

1. **Cashin-designed.** The Soft Sac was introduced during her tenure
   (1962–1974) [wwd-coach-early-years]. A 1970 introduction would
   place it firmly inside her contract window, and a soft glove-leather
   silhouette is consistent with her broader Sills/Coach leather-craft
   vocabulary.
2. **In-house.** vintagecoachie's framing — that the Soft Sac
   introduction marks the disappearance of Cashin's striped lining and
   bright color — describes a *visual departure* from her signature.
   That is more consistent with an in-house design (or a design Cashin
   styled but did not originate) than with a Cashin-led continuation.

Without primary documentation — a 1970 catalog page, or a UCLA Cashin
papers cite [ucla-cashin-papers] — attribution stays open. Per
`STYLE.md` §4 zone 2, Cashin attribution requires a primary source;
absent one, this entry does not credit her.

## Authentication

Cashin-era diagnostics apply. Per `research/E-authentication.md` §8,
1970 production sits in the **E2c** sub-era (late 1960s / early 1970s),
characterized by:

- **Leather stamp inside, no creed patch.** The numbered creed is a
  feature of the mid-1970s onward; a 1970 Soft Sac should not carry
  one. A "Soft Sac" with a numbered creed is later production, not a
  1970 original.
- **No serial number.** Pre-numbered-creed; absence is correct, not a
  fake tell.
- **Glove-tanned cowhide** body [vintagecoachie]. Raw or burnished
  edges; painted glossy edges are a strong fake or reproduction signal
  on a bag claiming 1970 production (per
  `research/E-authentication.md` §7).
- **Solid brass hardware.** Non-magnetic; a magnet that sticks
  indicates plated steel and a much later piece. Cashin-era hardware
  is often unstamped (per `research/E-authentication.md` §4).
- **No "Made in [country]" stamp.** Country-of-origin stamps are an
  NYC-creed-era feature; 1970 production predates that convention.

The **transition diagnostic** specific to this model: vintagecoachie's
framing implies a 1970 Soft Sac should *not* have the bright color and
striped fabric lining that defines pre-1970 Cashin Coach work
[vintagecoachie]. A "Soft Sac" with a striped Cashin-style lining is
either misidentified, transitional pre-1970 stock, or a different
model.

## Open verification items

1. **UCLA Cashin papers** [ucla-cashin-papers] — does the Soft Sac
   appear in Cashin's design illustrations, or in correspondence about
   Coach's 1970 line? Either presence or absence narrows the
   designer-attribution question.
2. **A 1970 Coach catalog page** showing the Soft Sac. This is the
   single highest-value verification target; it would pin the
   introduction year and establish the silhouette, closure, and
   hardware specifics.
3. **Relationship to the Duffle Sac.** The Duffle Sac (style 9085) is
   dated to "late 1960s" by the same dealer source family
   [yourgreatfinds-collectors-guide]. Whether the Soft Sac is the same
   model under a different early name, an immediate successor, or an
   adjacent silhouette is unresolved.
4. **Confirmation of the "bright colors and striped lining
   disappears" framing.** vintagecoachie is the sole source for this
   reading; a second independent source — catalog imagery,
   contemporaneous press coverage, or a Lake biography excerpt
   [lake-cashin-2016] — would let the broader Cashin-era-pivot claim
   promote above `tentative`.
