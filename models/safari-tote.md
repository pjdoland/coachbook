## Lead

The Safari Tote is a Cashin-era Coach silhouette referenced in period
vintage dealer listings (notably 1stdibs and similar marketplaces) as
"Bonnie Cashin for Coach Safari Tote," typically dated to the 1960s
and characterized by a double-turnlock-pocket configuration. Designer
attribution to Bonnie Cashin is `likely` — the form, hardware
vocabulary, and dealer-consensus framing all sit consistently with
her documented Coach work — but **no Tier 1 or Tier 2 source has been
located that attributes the specific model name "Safari Tote" to a
Coach catalog entry**. This entry is published as `tentative` for that
reason.

## Folklore caveat — read first

This entry sits at the edge of what the archive can responsibly
document from free public sources. Specifically:

- The model name "Safari Tote" is **not present** in
  `research/D-models.md`. It does not appear in the Cashin-era table
  there alongside Cashin Carry, Dinky, Mailbox, Slimline, Musette,
  etc.
- The name surfaces in `research/J-cashin-supplemental.md` context
  only as a descriptor used in **period dealer listings** (1stdibs and
  comparable vintage-market sites), not in any catalog scan, museum
  caption, or UCLA Cashin papers reference yet retrieved.
- "Safari Tote" may therefore be a **dealer-coined descriptor** rather
  than Coach's catalog name for the bag. The actual catalog name (if
  any) is unknown, and the bag may have been catalogued under a
  different name, a style number only, or as part of an unnamed group.
- The double-turnlock-pocket configuration is consistent with Cashin's
  hardware vocabulary [vintagefashionguild-cashin] but is not unique
  to a single model.

Treat the model name itself as provisional pending a primary catalog
or UCLA-papers cite.

## Description

Per period dealer listings, the Safari Tote is a 1960s tote-form
glove-tanned cowhide bag carrying **two front pockets each closed
with a brass turnlock** — the diagnostic detail most cited by dealers
calling the bag a "Safari Tote." Solid brass hardware throughout.
Dimensions, color list, lining detail, and the Coach catalog name (if
different) are open verification items. No dated catalog appearance
has been located in free public sources.

The 1960s dating sits inside Cashin's Coach tenure (1962–1974
[wwd-coach-early-years]; 1961–1974 per `maharam-cashin`, disputed) and
post-dates the 1964 turnlock introduction
[vintagefashionguild-cashin], which is consistent with the
double-turnlock-pocket form.

## Authentication

Cashin-era diagnostics apply (cross-reference `authentication/` for
the full era-keyed matrix). The bag falls in **era code E2** —
pre-creed or early-creed Cashin-era construction:

- **No leather creed patch** on early E2 examples — small metal
  interior tag only on early-1960s production, transitioning to a
  leather stamp without a creed patch on mid-to-late-1960s production
  [coachbagrehab-dating]. A leather creed with a numeric serial is
  inconsistent with a 1960s dating and would push the bag into E3
  (Classic NYC) territory.
- **No serial number** on most pre-creed examples
  [coachbagrehab-dating]. Absence of a serial is not a fake tell on a
  Cashin-era bag.
- **Solid brass hardware, non-magnetic.** A magnet that sticks
  indicates plated steel and is a strong reproduction signal.
  "COACH" stamping on turnlocks is **not** expected on Cashin-era
  pieces; absence does not indicate inauthenticity
  [vintagefashionguild-cashin].
- **Double-turnlock-pocket configuration** is the descriptor most
  cited by period dealers attributing this model name. The turnlock
  itself was introduced at Coach in 1964
  [vintagefashionguild-cashin]; pre-1964 Cashin-era bags used kiss-
  lock or open-top closures and would not carry this configuration.
- **Glove-tanned cowhide, raw or burnished edges.** Painted/glossy
  edges are a strong reproduction signal.

## Designer attribution

`likely` Bonnie Cashin — not `confirmed`. The reasoning:

- The form, hardware, and 1960s production window are consistent with
  Cashin's documented Coach vocabulary [c-cashin §3,
  vintagefashionguild-cashin].
- Period dealers attribute the bag to Cashin under the "Bonnie Cashin
  for Coach Safari Tote" label.
- **However**, no UCLA Cashin papers cite, no dated 1962–1974 Coach
  catalog page, and no Tier 1/2 secondary source has been located
  attributing this specific model name to her. Per `research/C-cashin.md`
  §5, the over-attribution problem is severe in this domain — bags
  with Cashin-vocabulary hardware are routinely (mis)attributed to her
  even when introduced after her 1974 contract end. A 1960s dating
  here is plausible and the attribution is more defensible than for
  post-1974 bags, but the standard for `confirmed` is a primary cite
  the archive does not yet hold.
