# Bucket Bag

## Two distinct production eras

The "Bucket Bag" name refers to **two distinct Coach production
runs** that need to be disambiguated:

1. **Cashin-era original (early 1960s)** — one of the four
   documented Bonnie Cashin designs for Coach per Vintage Fashion
   Guild [vintagefashionguild-cashin]. Pre-style-number convention.
   Original specifications, dimensions, and dating require UCLA
   Cashin papers verification.

2. **Lightweight Collection variant (1987 onward)** —
   primary-confirmed in the **Spring 1989 Lightweights roster**
   per research stream M2 as Style **4075 (standard)** at $144 and
   Style **4070 (small)** at $128 [coach-catalogs-1960-1989]. Both
   continued in the Holiday 1991 Lightweight Collection at $176
   (4075) and $156 (4070) per M's main file. The Lightweight
   variant uses **thinned glove-tanned cowhide** (per the
   Lightweight Collection material spec) — a distinct construction
   from the Cashin-era original.

The Lightweight Collection's Bucket Bag is a 1987+ in-house
revival/reinterpretation of the Cashin silhouette, NOT a Cashin
design at the bag level — Cashin's contract ended 1974, 13 years
before the Lightweight Collection launched (Early Summer 1987 per
research stream O2).

## Cashin-era original

The Bucket Bag is one of the **four documented early-1960s Bonnie
Cashin designs for Coach**, listed alongside the shopping bag tote,
the shoulder bag, and a clutch-style purse with removable shoulder
strap [vintagefashionguild-cashin]. This places it among the small set
of Coach silhouettes whose attribution to Cashin rests on a named
secondary reference rather than community inference.

## Designer attribution

Vintage Fashion Guild's Cashin label entry states explicitly that
"her classic designs for Gail's Coach division during the early
1960s included the shopping bag tote, the bucket bag, shoulder bag
and a clutch-style purse with a removable shoulder strap"
[vintagefashionguild-cashin]. This is a Tier 2 attribution — better-
sourced than most Cashin attributions in the vintage-Coach community,
which typically rest on Tier 3 collector inference alone.

Attribution confidence is `likely`:

- The Vintage Fashion Guild listing is explicit and itemized
  [vintagefashionguild-cashin].
- The bucket silhouette appears in the Tier 3 dealer record as a
  recurring early-Coach form [yourgreatfinds-collectors-guide,
  vintagecoachie].
- Independent corroboration of the specific bucket bag against a
  1962–1974 Coach catalog page or against the Bonnie Cashin papers
  at UCLA [ucla-cashin-papers] would promote the attribution to
  `confirmed`. That cross-check is an open verification item.

The shopping bag tote in the same Vintage Fashion Guild list maps to
the Cashin Carry; the bucket bag is the bucket-silhouette member of
the same four-design group.

## Description

The bucket bag is a soft, cylindrical-bodied bag in glove-tanned
cowhide, narrowing or gathering at the top — the silhouette that
gives the form its name. Cashin-era Coach construction conventions
apply: solid brass hardware, drum-dyed cowhide with cut edges showing
body color, and (per the Cashin signatures documented across her
Coach output) likely a striped fabric lining and a saturated rather
than muted color palette [wwd-coach-early-years, vintagecoachie].
Specific closure type, dimensions, and color list for the early-1960s
original await catalog confirmation.

## Continuity into later Coach silhouettes

The bucket form persisted at Coach long after Cashin's 1974 departure
and is the ancestor of subsequent NYC-era and post-NYC-era bucket
bags — Equestrian Compartment Bucket (style 9809), Bradley Bucket
Crossbody (9936), Nicholas Bucket Drawstring (9946), and the
contemporary Bleecker Bucket Bag among them
[yourgreatfinds-collectors-guide]. These later buckets use the
silhouette Cashin established but are **not** Cashin designs — they
are post-1974 in-house revisions of a Cashin form. The distinction
matters: "Cashin-designed" applies to the early-1960s original;
"Cashin-influenced" is the accurate description for post-1974 bucket
bags that share the silhouette.

## Authentication (Cashin-era bucket bag)

The early-1960s bucket bag predates the numbered creed, so
authentication runs on the Cashin-era profile (E2) rather than the
NYC-era serial-decoding workflow:

- **No numbered creed.** The leather "COACH LEATHERWARE / EST. 1941
  / NEW YORK CITY" creed with a serial number is a post-Cashin NYC-
  era feature. Cashin-era bags carry a small interior metal tag or no
  interior label at all. A "Cashin bucket bag" with a numbered creed
  is mislabeled by era — it is post-1974 production, not a 1962–1974
  example.
- **No serial number.** Pre-numbered-creed; absence is not a fake
  tell for this era.
- **Solid brass hardware**, riveted through. Non-magnetic; a magnet
  that sticks indicates plated steel and a much later piece.
  Cashin-era turnlocks (where present on post-1964 examples) are
  unstamped on the back plate; the brass turnlock itself was
  introduced to Cashin's work at Coach in **1964**, so a bucket bag
  documented to 1962–63 should not have one [vintagefashionguild-cashin,
  bonnie-cashin-archive-org].
- **Glove-tanned cowhide**, drum-dyed; cut edges show body color.
  Painted glossy edges are not period-correct.
- **Striped fabric lining** is a Cashin signature [vintagecoachie];
  a plain or jacquard lining on a bag claimed as a Cashin-era
  bucket is more consistent with later production than with the
  1962–1974 original.
- **No "Made in [country]" stamp.** Country-of-origin stamps in the
  creed are an NYC-era feature. Cashin-era pieces are U.S.-made but
  not stamped as such on the creed (because there is no creed to
  stamp).

When in doubt, the era cue that resolves most questions is the
creed: a numbered creed places a bag firmly *after* the Cashin period
regardless of silhouette.
