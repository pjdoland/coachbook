# Bleecker Satchel

**Dispute flagged 2026-05-02.** Primary catalog evidence contradicts the
brand-published 2000 introduction date and surfaces multiple "Bleeker"
SKUs across the 1995-1997 window:

- **1995-1996 Source Book** (Soho Collection): **"Bleeker 4154"** at
  $220 [coach-catalogs-1960-1989]
- **1997 Spring Preview catalog** (Soho Collection): "**Bleeker Bag**"
  Style **4153** at $220 [coach-catalogs-1960-1989]
- **2000 Coach.com brand page**: states the Bleecker Satchel was
  "first introduced in 2000" [coach-vintage-bleecker]

Both 1995-96 (4154) and 1997 (4153) appear in the Soho Collection at
$220 — possibly the same silhouette with a renumbered SKU between
years, or two related Bleeker variants. Catalog spelling is "**Bleeker**"
(single 'c') across the 1990s — a documented Coach name variant, not
a misspelling. The 2000 "Bleecker Satchel" with double-c spelling is
the form Coach uses on the modern Vintage Bleecker re-release.

Two plausible reconciliations: (1) **Same name, different silhouette** —
the 1997 "Bleeker Bag" (Soho Collection) and the 2000 "Bleecker Satchel"
(Krakoff-transition) may be distinct models that reuse the place-name
following Coach's habit of cycling NYC neighborhood names through
generations; (2) **Brand misdates its own re-release** — Coach's modern
re-release product pages have been wrong before. Until a 2000-or-later
catalog is read for the Bleecker Satchel, this entry holds 1997-spring
as the earliest primary appearance.

## History

Coach's own brand-published "Vintage Bleecker Satchel" product page
(CK664, in the current Coach Originals re-release line) states the model
was "first introduced in 2000" [coach-vintage-bleecker, coach-originals].
The 2000 date conflicts with the 1997 Spring Preview catalog primary
evidence above.

That same year, Sara Lee Corporation took Coach Inc. public: the IPO
priced at $16/share on October 5, 2000, raising roughly $118 million on
the NYSE under ticker COH [fundinguniverse-coach]. Sara Lee spun off its
remaining holdings in April 2001, and the Signature C collection (Double
C logo) launched later in 2001 [fundinguniverse-coach,
encyclopedia-com-coach-leatherware, wwd-coach-early-years]. The Bleecker
Satchel therefore arrived at the inflection point where Coach pivoted
from family-owned-then-Sara-Lee leatherware house to publicly traded
logo-driven brand.

Coach has since re-released the model as the **Vintage Bleecker Satchel
(CK664)** in its current Coach Originals line [coach-vintage-bleecker,
coach-originals]. A separate current product, the **Bleecker Bucket Bag
(CCC24)**, also carries the Bleecker name but is a different silhouette
— do not conflate them.

## Description

Public free-source detail on the original 2000 Bleecker Satchel is thin.
The research files name it as a satchel silhouette in the Krakoff
transition group, alongside the Ergo line (1997–1998) and the Signature
C collection (2001), with no dimensions, style number, or color list
documented in surveyed Tier 2/3 sources. Treat the modern Coach
Originals re-release page as evidence of the silhouette and name only,
not of the original 2000 build specs.

## Era context

The Bleecker Satchel belongs to the **Reed Krakoff transition era
(1996–~2002)**, which this archive treats as contrast only per project
scope [PLAN §"Scope: what counts as 'vintage'"]. By 2000 the model
landed:

- **post-Cashin** — Bonnie Cashin's contract ended in 1974, twenty-six
  years earlier [wwd-coach-early-years]. The Bleecker Satchel is not
  a Cashin design; designer attribution is in-house Krakoff-era staff.
- **post-Sara-Lee-only** — Sara Lee owned Coach outright from 1985 until
  the October 2000 IPO; the Bleecker Satchel launched in the IPO year
  itself [fundinguniverse-coach].
- **one year before Signature C** — the 2001 launch of the Double C
  monogram collection is the visual-brand pivot this archive uses as
  the end-of-vintage cutoff [fundinguniverse-coach, wwd-coach-early-years].

The Bleecker Satchel is a useful date marker precisely because it sits
in that one-year window between IPO and Signature C — the close of the
era this archive documents in depth.

## Authentication

Light coverage only, since this entry is a contrast marker rather than
a per-model authentication target. A 2000-production Bleecker Satchel
should show:

- **Post-1994 letter-digit-letter creed serial.** A Bleecker with a
  pre-1994 digit-only serial would be wrong on its face — the model
  did not exist before 2000 [coach-vintage-bleecker]. Note the standard
  single-digit-year ambiguity caveat (STYLE §4 item 10): a code reading
  `K0?-####` or similar is 2000 *or* 2010 without independent
  cross-checks. Cross-reference creed wording, lining, and country of
  origin before committing to a year reading.
- **Multiple offshore production countries possible.** By 2000 Coach
  manufacturing had moved across several offshore facilities (Costa
  Rica appears mid-1990s, Dominican Republic late 1990s, with China
  ramping up in the 2000s — see `research/E-authentication.md` and
  PLAN §10). A Bleecker Satchel "Made in U.S.A." stamp would be the
  surprise; offshore stamps are expected. No single country is the
  sole correct answer for this model.
- **Vintage Bleecker Satchel re-release vs original.** Coach's current
  CK664 re-release is not a 2000 original. Modern re-releases carry
  post-2014 sewn-in country-of-origin tags rather than creed serials
  and will not match early-2000s creed wording.

For deeper authentication coverage of this era, see the forthcoming
`authentication/` E5 (Transition / early offshore, 1995–~2002) entries.

## Note on scope

This archive treats Krakoff-era bags (1996–~2002) as **contrast only**
per `PLAN.md` §"Scope" decision 4. The Bleecker Satchel entry exists
specifically to bound the vintage period: the 2000 introduction date is
Tier-1 brand-confirmed, the IPO is the corporate-structure inflection,
and Signature C in 2001 is the visual-brand cutoff. No equivalent
deep-dive (variants, dimensions, colors, common-fakes catalog) is
planned for this model. Readers wanting fuller treatment should look
to NYC-era entries (Willis, Court, Cashin Carry).
