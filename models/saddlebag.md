# Saddlebag

The Saddlebag is a curved-silhouette Coach bag with origins in the
Cashin era; the saddle shape persisted in production well past
Cashin's 1974 departure, with later variants — including the
Crescent Saddle Bag — carrying the silhouette forward into the
Classic NYC era [yourgreatfinds-collectors-guide, vintagecoachie].
The early-version Cashin attribution is `tentative` pending a primary
catalog or UCLA Cashin papers cite; the silhouette's persistence
post-1974 is `likely`.

## Description

The Saddlebag is a saddle-style curved-shape bag designed to sit on
the hip [yourgreatfinds-collectors-guide]. The silhouette references
equestrian saddle bags — a curved body with a contoured back panel
that contours against the wearer's side — and reads as descriptive of
shape rather than of a single fixed pattern. Specific dimensions,
strap configuration, and closure are not pinned to a primary catalog
source in the working references and vary across the production span.

## Era-span complication

The Saddlebag entry sits across an era boundary, which is the
entry's primary content. Two distinct production states share the
name and silhouette:

- **Early-version Saddlebag (Cashin era, E2)** — listed in the
  Cashin attribution table as a Cashin-era silhouette,
  `tentative` pending corroboration [yourgreatfinds-collectors-guide].
- **Post-1974 saddle bags (Classic NYC era, E3–E4)** — the saddle
  silhouette persisted in Coach's production after Cashin's contract
  ended, with the Crescent Saddle Bag and Saddle Pouch NYC documented
  as later in-house variants [yourgreatfinds-collectors-guide].

The two states share visual vocabulary — curved body, hip-sitting
proportions, brass hardware — and a silhouette read alone does not
distinguish them. **Authentication must establish era from creed and
serial, not from silhouette.** A bag whose only Cashin-era signal is
"it looks like a saddle bag" is not, on that basis, a Cashin-era bag.

## Designer attribution

This entry sits in the high-folklore zone described in `STYLE.md` §4
and `research/C-cashin.md` §5. The discipline is the same as for
other tentatively-attributed Cashin-era models:

**Cashin's contracted tenure at Coach was 1962–1974, full stop**
[wwd-coach-early-years]. The Cashin attribution applies to
**early versions only** (pre-1974). Post-1974 saddle bags are by
definition not Cashin designs even though they continue her
silhouette — they are in-house Coach work using Cashin-era
vocabulary. The Crescent Saddle Bag is documented as Classic NYC-era
in-house production (see `models/crescent-saddle-bag.md`).

The early-version Cashin attribution stays at `tentative` until one
of the following lands:

1. A **1962–1974 Coach catalog page** showing a Saddlebag in
   production.
2. A **UCLA Cashin papers** [ucla-cashin-papers] entry attributing
   the Saddlebag to her — design illustrations, contracts referencing
   it by name, or correspondence about its development.
3. A **primary press piece** (period WWD, Vogue, NYT) crediting
   Cashin with the Saddlebag.
4. **Stephanie Lake's *Bonnie Cashin: Chic Is Where You Find It***
   (Rizzoli, 2016) referencing the Saddlebag in the Coach-period
   chapter.

## Authentication

Because the silhouette is era-ambiguous, era diagnostics off the bag
itself carry the entire authentication burden. Cross-reference the
era-keyed matrix in `authentication/`.

- **Pre-creed markers indicate Cashin-era (E2) production.** A small
  interior metal tag only (E2a, early 1960s) or a leather stamp
  inside with no creed patch and no serial (E2b, late 1960s / early
  1970s) is consistent with a Cashin-era introduction.
- **A numbered creed places the bag in the mid-1970s or later.**
  Pre-1994 Classic NYC-era bags carry a digit-only unique serial
  (`xxx-xxxx` 1970s, `xxxx-xxx` 1980s) [coachbagrehab-dating]. A
  numbered-creed Saddlebag could be either late Cashin-era or
  post-1974 in-house production — the creed format does not by
  itself resolve the question.
- **Hardware.** Solid brass, non-magnetic. A magnet that sticks to
  the hardware indicates plated steel and rules out vintage
  production.
- **Construction.** Glove-tanned cowhide with raw or burnished edges.
  Painted glossy edges on a "Cashin-era Saddlebag" are a strong
  reproduction signal.
- **Silhouette is era-ambiguous.** A curved hip-sitting saddle shape
  does not, on its own, place the bag inside Cashin's tenure. Read
  era from the creed and serial, not the shape.

## Related

- **Crescent Saddle Bag** — a separate model with documented
  dimensions (10.25" × 9.5" × 2.875") in the Classic NYC era. See
  `models/crescent-saddle-bag.md`. The Crescent Saddle Bag carries
  the saddle silhouette forward into in-house post-Cashin
  production; it is not an "early Saddlebag" and the two entries
  should not be conflated.
