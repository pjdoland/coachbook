## Lead

The kissing-lock coin purse is one of Bonnie Cashin's signature
small-leather-goods forms at Coach [vintagecoachie,
yourgreatfinds-collectors-guide]. The form persisted in production
after Cashin's 1974 contract end; production-era cannot be assumed
from form alone. A piece bearing the kiss-lock silhouette may be
Cashin-era or a later in-house continuation, and authentication
must rely on era cues other than the closure itself.

## Description

The kissing-lock coin purse is a small-form leather pouch closed by
a hinged brass kiss-lock frame — the two-ball clasp that separates
("kisses apart") to open. The body is glove-tanned cowhide in
Cashin-era and early NYC-era examples. Per the dealer-compiled
record, the interior sometimes features a contrasting-color leather
"surprise" lining [yourgreatfinds-collectors-guide] — a Cashin-style
detail consistent with her broader vocabulary of considered color
and material contrast.

Specific dimensional variants and a full color-by-era list are not
yet pinned to a primary catalog source in the archive's working
references; this section will be expanded once a 1962–1974 catalog
or a dated NYC-era catalog page is matched to specific configurations.

## Designer attribution

The kissing-lock form is attributed to Cashin as a signature design
across multiple Tier 3 sources [vintagecoachie,
yourgreatfinds-collectors-guide]. The C-stream research treats this
as `likely` rather than `confirmed` because no primary catalog page
or UCLA Cashin papers reference has yet been matched to the model.
Either anchor — a 1962–1974 Coach catalog page showing the coin
purse, or an entry in the Bonnie Cashin Collection at UCLA
[ucla-cashin-papers] — would settle the authorship.

The Cashin Carry tote and the kissing-lock coin purse are the two
small-leather-goods forms most cleanly associated with Cashin in the
secondary literature; the tote has a Tier 2 anchor
[wwd-coach-early-years] that the coin purse currently lacks.

## Continuation post-Cashin

Variants of the kissing-lock coin purse remained in production well
after Cashin's 1974 contract end. The kiss-lock silhouette alone
does **not** date a piece to her tenure: a coin purse with the
kissing-lock form may be a Cashin-era original (1962–1974), an
early NYC-era continuation (1975–1984), or a later-NYC continuation
(1985–1994). Era discipline (per `STYLE.md` §4) requires that a
post-1974 example be described as "in the Cashin form" or "Cashin-
influenced," not "designed by Cashin."

## Authentication

Authenticate by era cue, not by closure. The kiss-lock frame is
shared across all production windows.

- **Pre-creed era examples have no creed.** A Cashin-era (1962–1974)
  coin purse predates the numbered leather creed patch and carries
  either a small interior metal tag or no interior label at all.
  Absence of a creed is not a fake tell for this era.
- **Later examples may carry a creed.** Post-1974 continuation
  pieces produced during the NYC era can carry the standard
  numbered creed. A "COACH LEATHERWARE / EST. 1941 / NEW YORK CITY"
  creed places a kissing-lock coin purse in the NYC-era continuation
  window, not the Cashin period.
- **Brass frame quality and stamping vary by era.** Earlier Cashin-
  era frames are unstamped solid brass; later NYC-era production
  may show "COACH" stamping on hardware adjacent to the frame.
  Brass is non-magnetic and heavy for its size; a magnetic frame
  indicates plated steel and a much later piece.
- **Style-number absence is era-appropriate** for small accessories
  in general; per `research/E-authentication.md`, coin purses
  legitimately ship without serials in some windows.
- **Interior contrast leather**, where present, is consistent with
  the Cashin-era detail per [yourgreatfinds-collectors-guide], but
  is not by itself a date anchor.

When in doubt, the creed (its presence, absence, and wording)
resolves era more reliably than the closure form.
