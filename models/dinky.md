# Dinky Bag

## Lead — primary-confirmed Style 9375

**The Dinky Bag is Style 9375**, primary-confirmed in continuous
catalog production from **Fall 1981 onward** [coach-catalogs-
1960-1989] across research streams N1 (1981-1982), N2 (1983), N3
(1984), M1 (1985), and M2 (1989). The Dinky's exact introduction
year is open (catalogs predating Fall 1981 are not yet in the
archive corpus), but the silhouette is documented as continuously
in production for at least 8 years (Fall 1981 → Spring 1989) at
the same style number, with consistent silhouette across that span.

The Cashin attribution remains **tentative**: the silhouette
pre-dates 1981 and could plausibly be a Cashin design (her tenure
1962-1974 ended 7 years before the earliest primary catalog
appearance), but no primary source — a 1962-1974 catalog page or
a UCLA Cashin papers entry — has been matched. **Cashin is NOT
named in any 1981-1984 Coach catalog** (per N4) despite the Dinky
appearing in all of them, so the catalog evidence does not directly
attribute the design to her.

## Description

The Dinky is a small bag — the name itself reads as descriptive of
scale rather than silhouette. The dealer-compiled record
[yourgreatfinds-collectors-guide] places it among the Cashin-era
small-bag silhouettes alongside the kissing-lock coin purse and the
Convertible Clutch. Specific dimensions, body shape, lining, strap
configuration, and hardware spec are not pinned to a primary catalog
source in the archive's working references and will be expanded once
a dated catalog page is matched to the bag.

## Variants

Two configurations are documented in the working references
[yourgreatfinds-collectors-guide]:

- **Standard Dinky** — the base small-bag form.
- **Kisslock Dinky** — a kiss-lock closure variant. The kissing-lock
  closure is itself a Cashin signature element on the
  contemporaneous coin-purse silhouette (`research/C-cashin.md` §3),
  so the Kisslock Dinky shares vocabulary with confirmed Cashin
  designs without that vocabulary alone establishing authorship.

Style numbers, dimensions, and the chronological relationship between
the standard and kiss-lock variants (which came first, whether they
were sold concurrently) are open verification items.

## Designer attribution — the timing problem

This entry sits in the same high-folklore zone as the Mailbox (see
`STYLE.md` §4 and `research/C-cashin.md` §5). The attribution
discipline is the same:

**Cashin's contracted tenure at Coach was 1962–1974, full stop**
[wwd-coach-early-years]. She was a contracted designer, not a Coach
employee; the contract ended in 1974. Bags introduced from 1975
onward — even those that use her vocabulary (turnlock, side pockets,
kiss-lock closures, saturated color) — are by definition not Cashin
designs.

The Dinky's introduction year is **not pinned** in the working
references. The dealer-compiled record [yourgreatfinds-collectors-guide]
groups it among Cashin-era silhouettes without anchoring it to a
specific year. That places the bag in one of three states:

- If the Dinky was introduced **1962–1974**, it falls inside Cashin's
  tenure and the attribution is plausible. To upgrade to `confirmed`,
  the entry needs primary evidence that Cashin specifically designed
  it (not just that it existed during her tenure).
- If the Dinky was introduced **1975 or later**, the Cashin
  attribution is wrong on its face — the bag was the work of Coach's
  in-house team using Cashin-era vocabulary.
- If the Dinky was introduced **pre-1962**, the Cashin attribution is
  also wrong: she had not yet been hired.

The Cashin attribution table in `research/C-cashin.md` §4 marks the
Dinky `tentative` pending catalog confirmation. That is the ceiling
the evidence currently supports.

**Verification path** — any one of the following would resolve the
attribution:

1. A **1962–1974 Coach catalog page** showing the Dinky (or Kisslock
   Dinky) in production.
2. A **UCLA Cashin papers** [ucla-cashin-papers] entry attributing the
   Dinky to her — design illustrations, contracts referencing it by
   name, or correspondence about its development.
3. A **primary press piece** (period WWD, Vogue, NYT) crediting
   Cashin with the Dinky.
4. **Stephanie Lake's *Bonnie Cashin: Chic Is Where You Find It***
   (Rizzoli, 2016) referencing the Dinky in the Coach-period chapter.

Absent one of these, the attribution stays at `tentative`.

## Authentication

Authentication of a Dinky claimed to be Cashin-era depends on reading
era diagnostics off the bag itself, then asking whether those
diagnostics are consistent with a pre-1974 introduction.

- **Pre-creed era diagnostics align with E2 production.** Per
  `research/E-authentication.md` §1 and §8 (cross-referenced via
  `models/cashin-carry.md`), early-1960s (E2a) bags carry a small
  interior metal tag only; late-1960s / early-1970s bags carry a
  leather stamp inside with no creed patch and no serial. A Dinky
  with no creed and either of these markers is consistent with a
  Cashin-era introduction date.
- **A numbered creed places the bag in the mid-1970s or later.** The
  numbered creed with a 1970s `xxx-xxxx` digit-only serial format
  was in use from roughly the mid-1970s onward
  [coachbagrehab-dating]. A Dinky with a numbered creed could be
  either pre or post Cashin departure — the format itself spans
  1973/1974/1975/1976+ and does not cleanly resolve the attribution
  timing.
- **Kiss-lock closure (Kisslock Dinky variant).** The kissing-lock
  fastener is a Cashin-era vocabulary signal but not an authorship
  signal — the closure persisted on Coach small leather goods after
  1974. Presence of a kiss-lock does not, on its own, place the bag
  inside Cashin's tenure.
- **Solid brass hardware — magnet test.** Brass is non-magnetic
  (`research/E-authentication.md` §4). A magnet that sticks to any
  hardware indicates plated steel, which is post-vintage and rules
  out a Cashin-era bag.
- **Glove-tanned cowhide, raw or burnished edges.** Painted glossy
  edges on a "Cashin-era Dinky" are a strong fake or reproduction
  signal — Coach NYC-era construction does not paint edges
  (`research/E-authentication.md` §7).

None of these diagnostics by themselves resolve the authorship
question. They establish that a given Dinky is consistent with E2
production; they do not tell you whether the silhouette was
*introduced* during Cashin's tenure.

## Open verification items

1. **Dated Coach catalog page** showing the Dinky in production,
   ideally from the 1962–1974 window, with style number and
   dimensions.
2. **UCLA Cashin papers** [ucla-cashin-papers] reference attributing
   the Dinky to Cashin.
3. **Primary press feature** (period WWD, Vogue, Harper's Bazaar,
   NYT) with Cashin credited for the Dinky.
4. **Stephanie Lake's *Bonnie Cashin: Chic Is Where You Find It***
   (Rizzoli, 2016) — check the Coach-period chapter for any direct
   reference to a Dinky.
5. **Style number and dimensions** for both standard and Kisslock
   Dinky variants.
6. **Chronology of variants** — whether the Kisslock Dinky preceded,
   followed, or ran concurrently with the standard form.

Until at least one of items 1–4 lands, this entry stays at
`confidence: tentative` and the designer attribution at `tentative`.
