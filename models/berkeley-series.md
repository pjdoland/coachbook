# Berkeley Series

## Lead

The Berkeley Series is an **all-suede** Coach sub-collection that
**launched Early Autumn 1996** [coach-catalogs-1960-1989]. The 1996
Early Autumn catalog debuts the line with the brand voice "casual
American sensibility." Berkeley is the **last named collection
initiated under the pre-Krakoff design organization** — Reed Krakoff
was hired December 1996, several months after the Berkeley launch.

## Launch palette (Early Autumn 1996 — 4 colors)

| Color | Notes |
|-------|-------|
| Mahogany | Carryover from the established Classics palette |
| Red | |
| Russet | Suede-specific warm brown |
| Black | |

The four-color launch palette is the documented Berkeley range.
Additional colors may have been added in subsequent seasons but are
not yet primary-confirmed and require verification before being
treated as authentic Berkeley colors.

## Material

The defining Berkeley element is **all-suede construction** — a
material departure from Coach's flagship glove-tanned cowhide,
distinct also from the Sonoma Collection's Nubuc (1995-1996 sister
sub-line) and from Madison's pebble-textured leather. Berkeley is the
suede-specific entrant in the late-NYC sub-collection era.

A "Berkeley" presented in glove-tanned cowhide, pebble-textured
leather, or Nubuc is wrong on its face — material is the load-bearing
authentication tell for this line.

## Brand voice

> "casual American sensibility"

The phrasing locates Berkeley within the broader pre-Krakoff "American
leathergoods" positioning that anchored Coach catalogs through 1996
(see also the 1996-1997 Source Book's celebrity-descendant model
casting and "By definition, Classics are for now and always"
heritage tagline).

## Era placement — late-NYC, pre-Krakoff

Berkeley sits at the **E4-E5 boundary**:

- **Launch: Early Autumn 1996** — pre-Krakoff
- **Krakoff hired: December 1996** — months after the Berkeley launch
- **Reintroduced: 1996 Fall catalog** — still pre-Krakoff
- **1996-1997 Source Book** — Berkeley listed as one of the named
  collections alongside Classic, Soho, Sonoma, Madison

Designer attribution belongs to the late-NYC in-house team. **NOT
Krakoff** — the line predates his hire by months. **NOT Cashin** —
22 years after her tenure ended. Any Berkeley attribution to either
designer is wrong.

This places Berkeley in the same management cohort as the Sheridan
Collection sunset (Spring 1995), the Sonoma expansion, and the first
"modern classics" rhetoric that surfaces in Holiday 1996 — a portrait
of a late-NYC organization actively rationalizing collections and
language even before Krakoff arrived.

## Authentication

For a Berkeley Series bag:

1. **All-suede construction** — the defining material tell. Glove-
   tanned cowhide, Nubuc, or pebble-textured leather rule out a
   Berkeley attribution.
2. **Launch palette** — Mahogany / Red / Russet / Black. Any other
   color requires primary verification before being treated as
   authentic Berkeley.
3. **Mid-1996 era diagnostics** — letter-digit-letter style code in
   creed (e.g. `[A-M]6[factory-letter]-XXXX`). A Berkeley bag with a
   pre-1994 "COACH LEATHERWARE" creed wordmark would be a contradiction
   given the Early Autumn 1996 launch.
4. **Solid brass hardware** consistent with the broader Coach
   construction standard of the period.

Style numbers for individual Berkeley pieces are not yet captured in
the archive's primary readings — verification pending against the
1996 Early Autumn and 1996 Fall catalog page-level extractions.

For background on the suede authentication question, cross-reference
the broader leather-type discussion in `authentication/leather.md`.
