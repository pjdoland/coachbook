## Lead

**The Plaza Bag (Style 9860) and Plaza Bag Large (Style 9865) are
Coach Continental Series styles introduced Fall 1989**
[coach-catalogs-1960-1989], alongside the Court Bag (9870) and
Regency Brief (5255). Earlier archive framing of the Plaza as a
mid-1980s Tier-3 model with `tentative` confidence is superseded by
the Fall 1989 Continental Series launch primary evidence.

By Holiday 1991 the Continental Series styles had been re-absorbed
into Classic Collection branding — the Plaza appears in Classic
Collection catalog pages without explicit Continental Series framing.
This is a documented Coach pattern: launch a named sub-series, then
merge surviving styles into the perennial Classics.

The Plaza is the **rounded variant of the Court** shoulder bag —
same Continental Series silhouette family, with the Plaza in two
sizes (9860 standard, 9865 large) and the Court (9870) as a
distinct silhouette. Tier 3 collector references frame the Plaza as
"harder to find" than the Court itself [yourgreatfinds-collectors-
guide].

## Description

A flap-front shoulder bag with a rounded silhouette, contrasted
against the squarer Court parent. Two sizes were produced; the Plaza
documented in the collector record is "the smaller of two available
sizes" [yourgreatfinds-collectors-guide]. Closure is a single brass
turnlock consistent with the Court family. Hardware is solid brass
throughout — non-magnetic, in line with NYC-era construction. A
**bottle-green** colorway is noted in research stream D
[yourgreatfinds-collectors-guide]; other Classic NYC palette colors
(camel, bone, British tan, black) are plausible by analogy to the
Court but are not specifically documented for the Plaza and are not
listed here.

The Plaza carries a back slip pocket — the structural feature that
distinguishes it from the smaller **Bella** variant, which lacks the
back slip pocket [yourgreatfinds-collectors-guide].

## History

Introduced during the Classic NYC era (mid-1970s through 1994) as the
rounded sibling of the Court (style 9870). A precise introduction year
is not yet confirmed from a primary catalog. **Style number is TBD**:
no number specific to the Plaza appears in the research files
currently consulted, and `style_numbers` is intentionally left out of
frontmatter pending corroboration.

**Designer attribution.** The Plaza is **not** a Bonnie Cashin design.
Cashin's tenure ended in 1974, and the Plaza, like its Court parent,
is a Classic NYC-era in-house design. Community attributions of any
post-1974 silhouette to Cashin are folklore.

## Variants & related

- **Court (9870)** — the squared parent silhouette. The Plaza is the
  rounded version of the Court [yourgreatfinds-collectors-guide]. See
  `models/court.md`.
- **Bella** — a smaller variant of the Plaza, distinguished by the
  absence of the back slip pocket present on the Plaza
  [yourgreatfinds-collectors-guide]. Style number and dating both
  `tentative`.

## Authentication

Authentication tells for a Plaza track the Court's diagnostics. Cross-
reference `models/court.md` and the era-keyed matrix in
`authentication/` for full feature lists.

- **Style number.** TBD — no Plaza-specific style number is recorded
  in research stream D. A creed bearing only a style number does not
  date a bag in any case; style numbers were reused across years
  [coachbagrehab-dating].
- **Creed wording (E3–E4 diagnostic).** A pre-1994 Plaza should read
  "COACH LEATHERWARE" with "LEATHERWARE" stamped in small letters
  beneath "COACH," typically with "Made in United States" or "Made in
  New York City, U.S.A." on earlier examples [coachbagrehab-dating,
  vintagecoachie].
- **Serial format.** Pre-1994 examples carry a digit-only unique
  serial (`xxx-xxxx` in the 1970s, `xxxx-xxx` in the 1980s, per
  E-stream sources, `tentative`) [coachbagrehab-dating].
- **Hardware.** Solid brass turnlock, non-magnetic. A magnet that
  sticks indicates plated steel and is a strong fake or post-vintage
  signal.
- **Lining.** Many NYC-era flap shoulder bags in this family are
  unlined; absence of lining is **not** a fake tell.
- **Construction.** Glove-tanned cowhide with raw or burnished edges.
  Painted glossy edges on a bag claiming 1980s manufacture is a
  strong reproduction signal.
- **Designer.** Any seller listing a Plaza as "designed by Bonnie
  Cashin" is wrong; treat that listing's other claims with skepticism.
