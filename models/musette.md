# Musette

The Musette is a 1970s briefcase-style Coach bag attributed to Bonnie
Cashin in the dealer-compiled record [yourgreatfinds-collectors-guide].
Like the Mailbox, the attribution is plausible but **unconfirmed**:
resolution requires placing the bag's introduction inside (pre-1974,
Cashin's contracted tenure) or outside (post-1974, in-house team)
Cashin's contractual window with Coach. Until a dated catalog or UCLA
Cashin papers cite anchors the introduction year, the entry holds the
attribution at `likely` and treats the era as `tentative`.

## Description

The Musette is a large briefcase-form Coach bag — closer in silhouette
to a working portfolio than to the soft totes and saddle bags that
dominate the Cashin-era catalog. The dealer-compiled record describes
it as "large and hard to find" [yourgreatfinds-collectors-guide],
which is consistent with a low-volume 1970s production rather than a
staple silhouette.

Specific dimensions, lining, and closure-type variants for the Musette
are not pinned to a primary catalog source in the archive's working
references; this section will be expanded once a dated catalog page is
matched to the bag.

## Designer attribution — the timing problem

Musette attribution sits in one of the highest-folklore zones in
vintage-Coach research (see `STYLE.md` §4 and `research/C-cashin.md`
§5). The dealer-compiled framing — "1970s brief-case style, thought up
by Bonnie Cashin" [yourgreatfinds-collectors-guide] — is Tier 3
attribution, and the timing problem must not be collapsed.

**Cashin's contracted tenure at Coach was 1962–1974, full stop**
[wwd-coach-early-years, vintagecoachie]. She was a contracted
designer, not a Coach employee; the contract ended in 1974 and is not
negotiable as a date range. Bags introduced from 1975 onward — even
those that use her vocabulary (turnlock, side pockets, saturated
color) — are by definition not Cashin designs.

A "1970s Musette" lands on either side of that line:

- If the Musette was introduced in **1970, 1971, 1972, 1973, or
  1974**, it falls inside Cashin's tenure and the attribution is
  plausible. To upgrade to `confirmed`, the entry needs primary
  evidence that Cashin specifically designed it (not just that it
  existed during her tenure).
- If the Musette was introduced in **1975, 1976, 1977, 1978, or
  1979**, it falls outside Cashin's tenure and the attribution is
  wrong on its face — the bag was the work of Coach's in-house team,
  no matter how often the community calls it a Cashin design.

A second caution comes from the Vintage Fashion Guild record: the
four early-1960s Cashin Coach designs documented there — shopping
bag tote, bucket bag, shoulder bag, and clutch-style purse with a
removable shoulder strap [vintagefashionguild-cashin] — do **not**
include a briefcase or Musette form. That is not proof Cashin did
not design the Musette later in her tenure; it does mean the Tier 2
record of her early-1960s Coach output does not corroborate the
Musette attribution.

The Cashin attribution table in `research/C-cashin.md` §4 marks the
Musette `likely Cashin` pending catalog confirmation. That is the
ceiling the evidence currently supports.

**Verification path** — any one of the following would resolve the
attribution:

1. A **1962–1974 Coach catalog page** showing the Musette in
   production (or a press feature dated to that window referencing the
   bag).
2. A **UCLA Cashin papers** [ucla-cashin-papers] entry attributing the
   Musette to her — design illustrations, contracts referencing it by
   name, or correspondence about its development.
3. A **primary press piece** (period WWD, Vogue, NYT) with Cashin
   discussing or being credited with the Musette design.

Absent one of these, the attribution stays at `likely` and the entry
is honest about what it does and does not know.

## Cashin design philosophy

The dealer-compiled framing of the Musette characterizes it as
reflecting Cashin's goal of "normalizing making women's bags more
masculine, durable, and functional" [yourgreatfinds-collectors-guide].
That phrasing is a Tier 3 paraphrase, not a Cashin quote, but it
aligns with Cashin's documented design vocabulary: industrial
hardware aesthetic, leather-craft borrowed from outerwear, and a
sportswear sensibility that prized utility over ornament
[bonnie-cashin-archive-org]. A briefcase-form women's bag fits that
vocabulary cleanly — which is precisely why the attribution is
plausible enough to hold at `likely`, and precisely why it is not yet
`confirmed`. Vocabulary alignment is an authorship hint, not an
authorship proof.

## Authentication

Authentication of a Musette claimed to be Cashin-era depends on
reading era diagnostics off the bag itself, then asking whether those
diagnostics are consistent with a pre-1974 introduction.

- **Pre-creed era diagnostics align with late-1960s / early-1970s
  production.** Per `research/E-authentication.md` §1 and §8, bags
  from this window carry either a small interior metal tag only
  (early 1960s) or a leather stamp inside with no creed patch and no
  serial (late 1960s–early 1970s). A Musette with no creed and only a
  leather stamp is consistent with a pre-1974 introduction date — but
  the absence of a creed is also consistent with a 1973 or 1974 bag
  manufactured just before the numbered creed era began.
- **A numbered creed places the bag in the mid-1970s or later.** The
  numbered creed with a 1970s `xxx-xxxx` digit-only serial format
  [coachbagrehab-dating] was in use from roughly the mid-1970s
  onward. A Musette with a numbered creed therefore could be either
  pre or post Cashin departure — the format itself spans
  1973/1974/1975/1976+ and does not cleanly resolve the attribution
  timing.
- **Brass turnlock — post-1964 if present.** Cashin introduced the
  turnlock to her Coach work in 1964 [vintagefashionguild-cashin]. A
  Musette with a turnlock is post-1964 production; this is era-
  consistent if the bag is Cashin-authored, but does not prove
  authorship — Coach continued using the turnlock on in-house bags
  long after Cashin departed in 1974.
- **Solid brass hardware — magnet test.** Brass is non-magnetic
  (`research/E-authentication.md` §4). A magnet that sticks to the
  turnlock or D-rings indicates plated steel, which is post-vintage
  and immediately rules out a Cashin-era bag.
- **Glove-tanned cowhide, raw or burnished edges.** Painted glossy
  edges on a "Cashin-era Musette" are a strong fake or reproduction
  signal — Coach NYC-era construction does not paint edges
  (`research/E-authentication.md` §7).

None of these diagnostics by themselves resolve the authorship
question. They establish that a given Musette is consistent with
late-1960s / early-1970s production; they do not tell you whether the
silhouette was *introduced* during Cashin's tenure.

## Open verification items

Listed explicitly so any future contributor knows exactly what would
upgrade this entry's confidence:

1. **Dated Coach catalog page** showing the Musette in production,
   ideally from the 1970–1974 window. The PurseForum vintage-Coach
   catalogs thread (member-uploaded scans, browser-only) is the most
   likely free source.
2. **UCLA Cashin papers** [ucla-cashin-papers] reference attributing
   the Musette to Cashin — design illustrations, named correspondence,
   or contract addenda. The online finding aid is at
   https://oac.cdlib.org/findaid/ark:/13030/kt9z09q97x/.
3. **Primary press feature** (period WWD, Vogue, Harper's Bazaar, NYT)
   with Cashin credited for the Musette. Pre-paywall WWD coverage is
   the most plausible target [wwd-coach-early-years].
4. **Stephanie Lake's *Bonnie Cashin: Chic Is Where You Find It***
   (Rizzoli, 2016) — check the Coach-period chapter for any direct
   reference to a Musette or briefcase-form Coach bag.
5. **Style number and dimensions** — neither is recorded in the
   working references; both should appear in any 1970s catalog page
   that surfaces.

Until at least one of items 1–4 lands, this entry stays at
`confidence: tentative` and the designer attribution at `likely`. The
community's aggregate volume of "Cashin Musette" claims is not
sufficient to upgrade either tag; only primary evidence is.
