# Miles and Lillian Cahn

## Lead

Miles Cahn (1921–2017) and his wife Lillian (born Sátoraljaújhely, Hungary; 1923–2013) joined the small Manhattan workshop Gail Leather Products in 1946 as **contractors** (not employees) [wwd-lillian-cahn-obituary], purchased the company in 1961, and sold it to Sara Lee Corporation in July 1985 for a reported ~$30 million [coach-10k-fiscal-2003, fundinguniverse-coach, wwd-coach-early-years]. Their thirty-nine-year stewardship — and their 1961 hire of Bonnie Cashin to design Coach's first women's handbag line — defined the Coach brand identity that this archive documents. They are not designers; they are owners and operators, and the entry sits in `designers/` only because that is the existing schema for people.

## Pre-Cahn era (1941–1946)

Coach predates the Cahns by five years. The company was founded in 1941 as **Gail Leather Products**, a family-run workshop in a Manhattan loft producing men's small leather goods (wallets, billfolds) by hand [coach-10k-fiscal-2003, fundinguniverse-coach, encyclopedia-com-coach-leatherware, wwd-coach-early-years]. The original 1941 founders are not yet identified in free public sources and remain an open research item. The Cahns are not the founders; this is the most-corrected fact in vintage Coach folklore (see "Common misconceptions" below).

## The Cahns' role

**Miles Cahn — operations and leather sourcing.** Miles ran the production side of the business. Per FundingUniverse, he developed Coach's signature glove-tanned cowhide process, inspired by the heavy, lanolin-treated leather of a baseball glove — leather that softens and develops patina with handling rather than cracking [fundinguniverse-coach]. He began managing factory operations around 1950, six years before he and Lillian bought the company [fundinguniverse-coach].

**Lillian Cahn — strategic direction.** Lillian pushed the company into women's handbags. Per FundingUniverse, the proposal to start a women's line — and the suggestion to hire Bonnie Cashin to design it — came from Lillian [fundinguniverse-coach]. The 1962 Cashin hire is the single most consequential business decision of the Cahn era, and it was hers.

## Key dates

- **1946** — Miles and Lillian Cahn join Gail Leather Products as **contractors** [wwd-lillian-cahn-obituary, fundinguniverse-coach, wwd-coach-early-years]. They are not the founders; the company is five years old. Some earlier secondary sources frame the 1946 join as "employees"; the WWD obituary of Lillian Cahn explicitly states they joined as contractors.
- **c. 1950** — Miles Cahn begins managing factory operations (`likely`; single source) [fundinguniverse-coach].
- **c. 1960** — "Coach" brand of cowhide handbags introduced under prior ownership; the Coach-branded handbag predates the Cahn purchase [fundinguniverse-coach].
- **1961** — Miles and Lillian Cahn purchase Gail Leather Products in a leveraged buyout from the original owners [fundinguniverse-coach, encyclopedia-com-coach-leatherware, wwd-coach-early-years].
- **1961** — Bonnie Cashin's contract is signed, at Lillian's suggestion, to design Coach's first women's handbag line [wwd-lillian-cahn-obituary, maharam-cashin]. Lillian's later recollection (`wwd-lillian-cahn-obituary`): "I was always dreaming after Bonnie's clothes in the Town & Country shop at Lord & Taylor. I couldn't afford them, but I loved them." Note: many sources frame the engagement as starting 1962 (when the first collection shipped); Lillian's WWD obituary and Maharam place contract signing in 1961.
- **Fall 1962** — Cashin's first collection (Cashin Carry tote) launches [coach-fb-1962-cashin, risd-cashin-1962, wwd-coach-early-years]. Cashin's design partnership with Coach runs through 1974.
- **1983** — Miles and Lillian Cahn start Coach Farm, a goat dairy in Gallatinville, New York [wikipedia-miles-cahn, fundinguniverse-coach].
- **July 1985** — Cahns sell Coach to Sara Lee Corporation. Reported sale price ~$30 million; Sara Lee acquires the factory, six boutiques, and the Madison Avenue flagship [coach-10k-fiscal-2003, fundinguniverse-coach, wwd-coach-early-years]. The Cahns reportedly sold to focus on Coach Farm [fundinguniverse-coach, wikipedia-miles-cahn].

The July 1985 sale date is `confirmed` against the Coach Inc. fiscal 2003 10-K, which states: "Coach was founded in 1941 and was acquired by Sara Lee Corporation in July 1985" [coach-10k-fiscal-2003]. Earlier secondary sources gave only the year; the 10-K is the primary anchor for the month.

## Coach Farm

The Cahns' post-Coach venture was a goat-cheese dairy. Coach Farm was started in 1983 — two years before they sold the handbag company — on a property in Gallatinville, in New York's Hudson Valley [wikipedia-miles-cahn, fundinguniverse-coach]. The dairy outlasted the Cahns' Coach tenure by decades and is the reason the 1985 sale to Sara Lee is widely reported as a deliberate transition rather than a distress sale.

## Common misconceptions

**The Cahns are not the founders of Coach.** This is the single most-corrected fact in vintage Coach folklore. Resale listings, social posts, and even occasional press articles credit Miles Cahn (sometimes both Cahns) as the 1941 founder of Coach. They are not. They joined a five-year-old company in 1946 as employees, and purchased it in 1961 [fundinguniverse-coach, encyclopedia-com-coach-leatherware, wwd-coach-early-years, coach-10k-fiscal-2003]. The original 1941 founders are unidentified in free public sources; the Coach 2000 S-1 prospectus (un-pulled) is the most likely free primary source to name them.

A related misconception: the company was not originally called "Manhattan Leather Bags." It was **Gail Leather Products** [fundinguniverse-coach, encyclopedia-com-coach-leatherware, wwd-coach-early-years]. "Manhattan Leather Bags" is a community misnomer.
